# SHIRTFACED STUDIO

## Prompt Construction Principles

**Version:** 1.0

---

## Purpose

This document captures the underlying mechanics behind the successful World 01 prompts.

It is not a photography guide.

It is not a style guide.

It explains why these prompts consistently produced authentic documentary photography while many technically correct prompts failed.

This document is permanent creative canon and should be read before any prompt generation.

---

## Core Principle

The prompts are not image descriptions.

They are memory reconstructions.

The model is never asked to "create an image."

It is asked to recreate a real moment that genuinely happened.

Everything else follows from that.

---

## The Prompt Hierarchy

Every production prompt follows the same hierarchy.

Never change the order.

---

### Level 1 — Emotional Truth

Every scene begins with emotion.

Never with location.

Never with products.

Never with camera settings.

Ask:

«What does this moment feel like?»

Examples:

- The night is only just beginning.
- Nobody wants to leave.
- Everyone hopes the night continues.
- The best part wasn't planned.
- The night just found a second wind.
- Everything still feels possible.

This emotional truth becomes the foundation for every later decision.

If this level fails, the image fails.

---

### Level 2 — Human Relationships

People are never isolated subjects.

They exist because of each other.

Nobody simply stands.

Everybody is interacting.

Examples:

- Someone steals another person's chips.
- One mate carries everyone's drinks.
- Somebody borrows a hoodie.
- A couple drift ahead of the group.
- Somebody laughs before the punchline.
- One bloke keeps trying to finish the same story all night.

Relationships tell the story.

Not faces.

---

### Level 3 — Event Before Composition

The event exists independently of the camera.

The camera simply witnesses it.

Never describe framing first.

Instead describe:

What is happening?

Why is it happening?

What happened five seconds earlier?

What happens five seconds later?

The photograph should feel like one frame extracted from continuous life.

---

### Level 4 — The Camera Is Another Friend

Only after the scene exists should the camera appear.

The photographer is another member of the group.

Never an invisible observer.

Never an impossible drone.

Examples:

- Across the road.
- Another table.
- Walking behind the group.
- Beside another parked car.
- Across the servo forecourt.
- From the dining room.
- Through the passenger window.
- Inside the back seat.

The camera always has a believable physical location.

---

### Level 5 — Documentary Imperfection

Perfect photographs rarely feel real.

Every successful image contains believable imperfections.

Examples:

- Motion blur.
- Slight underexposure.
- Someone cropped by the edge.
- A foreground obstruction.
- Poor framing.
- A passer-by blocking part of the frame.
- Headlights creating flare.
- Another table intruding into the foreground.

Imperfections must have believable causes.

Never add randomness simply because it looks "documentary."

---

### Level 6 — Environmental Truth

Locations support the story.

They are never the hero.

Examples:

- Beer garden.
- Kebab shop.
- Servo.
- Apartment stairs.
- Kitchen.
- Lookout.
- Pedestrian crossing.
- McDonald's car park.

Australia should be obvious without announcing itself.

---

### Level 7 — Technical Photography

Technical language reinforces the documentary aesthetic.

Typical settings:

- 35mm documentary photography.
- 50mm lens.
- Kodak Portra 400.
- Available light only.
- Natural film grain.
- Slight motion blur.
- Slight underexposure.
- No HDR.
- No cinematic colour grading.

These settings are not decorative.

They reinforce the emotional direction established earlier.

---

### Level 8 — Product Integration

Products always enter last.

Never first.

Never second.

The world exists before Shirtfaced exists.

Products are discovered naturally.

Never displayed.

Never modelled.

Never advertised.

---

### Level 9 — Negative Constraints

Only after the scene is fully established should constraints appear.

Examples:

- No logos.
- No branding.
- No graphics.
- No visible labels.
- Generic packaging only.
- No commercial trademarks.
- Never American pickups.
- Never posed.

These protect the world rather than define it.

---

## Constraint Alignment

Every instruction should reinforce every other instruction.

Examples:

- Kodak Portra.
- Available light.
- Friend taking the photograph.
- Motion blur.
- Natural framing.
- Nobody posing.
- Slight underexposure.

Nothing should pull in the opposite direction.

Avoid conflicting instructions such as simultaneously asking for documentary realism and cinematic perfection.

---

## Eliminate Creative Decisions

The prompt should remove unnecessary ambiguity.

Specify:

- Who.
- Where.
- When.
- Why.
- Relationships.
- Emotion.
- Lighting.
- Camera position.
- Environmental details.
- Documentary imperfections.

The fewer arbitrary decisions the model must invent, the more coherent the image becomes.

---

## Product Philosophy

Products never create the story.

Stories create opportunities for products.

If every Shirtfaced garment disappeared and the image would still be worth saving, the prompt succeeds.

Only then should products exist inside it.

---

## The Instagram Test

Every generated image must pass one question:

«Would someone post this photograph if every Shirtfaced product were replaced with plain clothing?»

If the answer is no:

Reject the image.

---

## The Memory Test

Every prompt should answer:

«Why would somebody keep this photograph for ten years?»

If the answer is obvious, the prompt is probably correct.

If the answer is:

«"Because of the shirt."»

The prompt has failed.

---

## Production Formula

Every production prompt should follow this sequence:

1. Emotional truth.
2. Human relationships.
3. The event.
4. Environmental truth.
5. Camera placement.
6. Documentary imperfections.
7. Technical photography.
8. Product integration.
9. Negative constraints.
10. Authenticity validation.

Changing this order weakens the result.

---

## Final Principle

Shirtfaced is not building clothing photography.

It is building a documentary universe.

Every image is another recovered memory from that universe.

The products simply happen to live there.
