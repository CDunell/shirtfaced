**Stage:** 2 — Product Photography & Social Content System

## 1. Purpose

This document defines how Shirtfaced products are photographed without leaving the established photographic universe. There is no documentary brand on social and separate ecommerce brand on the website. Product photographs are quieter, clearer fragments of the same world.

The world is the studio. Products are photographed in the locations where the night happened, under the light already there, on the people who belong there and among objects already present. The product may become clearer; the world must never disappear.

## 2. Canon relationship

- `WORLD.md` defines permanent emotional and visual rules.
- `CONTINUITY.md` records what has happened.
- `SHOTLIST.md` plans scenes and opportunities.
- This file defines how commercially useful product frames are extracted from those scenes.

Product images must remain continuous in cast, location, weather, time, clothing, product condition, light, camera logic and emotional progression.

## 3. Non-negotiables

- Documentary, never posed.
- Recognisably Australian without flags or clichés.
- Warm, optimistic and socially real.
- Available light; Kodak Portra-adjacent colour.
- Ordinary people and believable bodies.
- No unrelated readable commercial branding.
- Australian tray-back utes and ordinary cars; never American pickups or car photography.
- Every Shirtfaced garment remains completely blank unless an approved design asset is deliberately supplied: no logo, graphic, embroidery, tag or invented label.
- Product accuracy is absolute: type, colour, cut, proportions, seams, cuffs, pockets, fabric and fit must match the sellable item.

## 4. Product prominence

| Level | Meaning | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| P0 | Incidental | Documentary archive, teaser, Reel |
| P1 | Present but secondary | Lifestyle, collection, carousel |
| P2 | Clear while still in a real scene | Fit, product gallery, paid social |
| P3 | Product-led with recognisable environment | Primary product image, detail, packaging |
| P4 | Isolated or clinical | Prohibited |

The system uses P0–P3. It never requires P4.

## 5. Shot families

Required families are lifestyle hero, front, back, side, fit, movement, environmental, product detail, fabric texture, stitching, folded, flatlay, hanging, object study, packaging, shipping, care/construction and transitional documentary.

Not every product needs every family. Every published product must communicate desire, front shape, alternate or rear shape, fit, movement, fabric, construction, colour and environmental belonging.

### Lifestyle hero

P1–P2. A real action leads: walking between venues, waiting at a crossing, arriving at an apartment, sitting at a pub bench or talking beside a ute. Reject posing, directed expressions, fashion-campaign behaviour and decorative locations.

### Front, back and side

P2–P3. Each angle must be motivated by action. Front may emerge while carrying drinks or waiting at a kitchen bench; back while climbing stairs or walking toward the balcony; side while speaking to a friend or waiting at a counter. Nobody rotates to demonstrate the garment.

### Fit and movement

Use varied heights, builds, genders and proportions. Preserve natural posture, layering and fabric behaviour. Movement may include slight blur, obstruction and imperfect framing when those qualities are genuinely caused by the moment.

### Environmental and object studies

Products may rest on kitchen chairs, pub tables, concrete stairs, balcony rails, ute trays, old timber, worn laminate or esky lids. Placement must feel left behind, not styled. No enclosed space — no car cabin, lift, tent or phone box — is ever the setting, for the product or anything else in frame; see `WORLD.md` → Vehicle Canon.

### Detail, texture and construction

Show neckline, ribbing, cuff, hem, hood, pocket, seams, cap closure or tote reinforcement using real surfaces and available light. Prove quality quietly. Do not imitate factory inspection or macro CGI.

### Folded, flatlay and hanging

Neat is acceptable; geometrically perfect is not. Flatlays resemble the temporary contents of a night emptied onto a table, never a decorative grid. Hanging locations include a garage hook, chair, apartment door, rail or headrest—not a boutique rack.

### Packaging and shipping

Show parcels on kitchen tables, at doors, on a ute tray, near a garage or being packed on a worktable. Packaging is practical, tactile and premium. No confetti, influencer unboxing or invented delivery claims.

## 6. Environments, light and camera

Approved environments include pubs, apartment kitchens, balconies, stairwells, old brick, concrete car parks, generic servo forecourts, ute trays, garages, laundries, hallways, lookouts and front steps. No enclosed space is an approved environment, full stop — not a car cabin, not a lift, not a tent. Product photography gets no exception the rest of this world doesn't.

Prohibited environments include white seamless studios, cycloramas, luxury hotels, American diners, content rooms, fashion studios, pristine designer apartments and generic ecommerce sets.

Approved light comes from pubs, streetlights, servos, apartments, cars, shopfronts, overcast daylight and early windows. Added light may preserve product truth but must not announce itself. No beauty light, RGB, glossy rim light or shadowless studio finish.

Rotate camera positions: across the road, through a doorway, another table, beside a parked ute, pavement level, across the forecourt, inside the action and outside the conversation. The camera is another friend, never inside an enclosed space and never inside a vehicle cabin — see `WORLD.md` → Vehicle Canon, which this document does not get an exception to.

## 7. Styling and continuity

Styling should look accumulated through use: hoodie over tee, borrowed layer, sleeves pushed up, garment around waist, cap carried, tote holding ordinary things. Avoid head-to-toe Shirtfaced, coordinated uniforms and displaying the full range at once.

Garments may crease and compress naturally but remain cared for. Track product condition from frame to frame. A garment cannot change colour, construction, dryness or wear state without cause.

Hero products rotate across tees, hoodies, caps, totes and future categories. Supporting products may recur naturally; hero treatment may not repeat lazily.

## 8. Product-page sequence

Recommended order:

1. Desire — lifestyle hero, P1–P2.
2. Clarity — front or three-quarter, P2–P3.
3. Fit — natural full or near-full body.
4. Back or useful alternate angle.
5. Movement.
6. Construction detail.
7. Fabric texture.
8. Environmental object frame.
9. Documentary return.

The sequence is **feeling → understanding → proof → world**. Six excellent non-duplicative images are better than twelve repetitive ones.

## 9. Studio planning fields

Each shot records production, collection, world, scene, moment, time, location, cast, product, colour, size, wearer, supporting products, prominence, shot family, camera position and distance, light source, action, surface, continuity link, crops, primary and secondary placements, accuracy requirements, prohibited details, review state, rejection reason and approved asset.

## 10. Review gates

1. **World fit:** Australian, documentary, optimistic, socially real, continuous and saveworthy without the product.
2. **Product truth:** correct item, colour, construction, fit, fabric and blank state.
3. **Commercial usefulness:** assigned placement works, crop works and the frame adds useful information or desire.
4. **Continuity:** cast, garment, weather, time, light, objects and location agree with adjacent assets.

Standard rejection codes include `WORLD_POSED`, `WORLD_GENERIC`, `WORLD_NOT_AUSTRALIAN`, `WORLD_NO_OPTIMISM`, `PRODUCT_INACCURATE`, `PRODUCT_INVENTED_BRANDING`, `PRODUCT_TOO_DOMINANT`, `PRODUCT_NOT_VISIBLE`, `CONTINUITY_GARMENT`, `PLACEMENT_BAD_CROP`, `IMAGE_ECOMMERCE`, `IMAGE_INFLUENCER` and `IMAGE_NOT_SAVEWORTHY`.

## 11. Production recipe

Choose the next world moment; define product role; identify missing commercial coverage; build an independent social action; rotate camera positions; lock continuity; capture documentary frames first; extract natural product moments; capture quiet objects; review through all four gates; allocate each asset to its strongest placement; update continuity.

## 12. Final tests

**Instagram test:** If nobody wore Shirtfaced, would people still save the image?

**Website test:** Does the gallery explain the garment without leaving the world?

**Memory test:** Does it feel like something happened, the garment moved through it and the camera noticed occasionally?

The world sells the garment. The garment never shrinks the world.
