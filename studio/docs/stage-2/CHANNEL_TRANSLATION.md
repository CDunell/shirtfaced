# SHIRTFACED — CHANNEL TRANSLATION SYSTEM

**Status:** Locked foundation  
**Stage:** 2  
**Date:** 5 August 2026

## 1. Purpose

One photographic event becomes several interpretations, not several duplicates. Consistency comes from the same people, products, night, emotion and photographic character. Variation comes from viewpoint, duration, sequence, prominence and copy.

## 2. Source event and layers

Every output belongs to a source event such as the first pub, walking between venues, late food, servo detour, apartment arrival, kick-ons, lookout, car park or morning-after kitchen.

Strong events produce five layers:

1. Establishing — place, cast and stage of night.
2. Human — recognisable behaviour or relationship.
3. Product — natural garment visibility.
4. Detail — objects, surfaces, light and small actions.
5. Transition — how the event begins, changes or ends.

## 3. Channel jobs

- Instagram feed builds the documentary archive.
- Carousels reconstruct a short event.
- Reels extend the photograph into movement and sound.
- TikTok identifies familiar social behaviour through micro-stories.
- Paid social creates quick curiosity.
- Website turns atmosphere into confidence.
- Email reconnects one moment or release to one useful action.

No platform trend overrides canon.

## 4. Instagram

Long-term balance: 45–55% documentary, 15–20% quiet product, 10–15% objects/details, 10–15% groups and 5–10% direct drop communication.

Carousel rhythm: saveworthy entry, orientation, human detail, clearer product presence, night detail, then either product reveal or unresolved ending. Vary distance and prominence. Captions add one observation, location, useful product fact or release statement; they never explain the joke or force slang.

Stories may carry fragments, ambient video, availability, packing and temporary information. Context arrives before the commercial action.

## 5. Reels

Reels feel like a photograph continued for several seconds. Use real actions: crossing, carrying drinks, waiting for food, entering the apartment, stairs, balcony, lookout or pulling on a hoodie.

Use straight cuts, held shots and ambient sound. Avoid trend transitions, speed ramps, fake film effects, beat-synced reveals and autoplay noise. Typical durations are 6–12 seconds for one moment, 12–25 for a sequence and up to 40 only when the event supports it.

## 6. TikTok

TikTok builds micro-stories around recognisable roles: the drinks carrier, snack thief, person who says “one beer,” missing mate, charger seeker, borrowed-hoodie friend or person refusing to end the night.

Structure: premise, observed evidence, escalation, small or absent resolution, natural product presence. Humour comes from recognition, never acting, pranks, forced banter, dancing or direct-to-camera selling. Product prominence is normally P0–P2.

## 7. Paid social and email

Paid social uses saveworthy documentary, fit, environmental or object frames at P1–P3. Copy is minimal and factual or observational. No shouting, empty premium language, fake scarcity or promotional poster treatment.

Email has one job. A launch email may use one campaign frame, a short release statement, selected product, one useful fact, one action and a quiet close. It does not reproduce the website in miniature.

## 8. Crops, reuse and asset families

Plan 4:5, square only where natural, true 9:16 and distinct website/email crops. Alternate frames are preferred to destructive crops.

Reuse levels:

- R0 — unique.
- R1 — two connected placements.
- R2 — intentional campaign anchor.
- R3 — overused and prohibited.

Each event family can contain a wide documentary frame, vertical human detail, product-clear still, movement clip, object study, environmental product frame, ambient audio and close.

## 9. Content pillars

Track documentary moments, product moments, in-between moments, object studies, night details, group moments, morning-after calm, behind-the-scenes, collection drops and product proof. No pillar dominates merely because it is easy to produce.

Morning-after content remains optimistic: quiet kitchens, early light, coffee, folded garments and empty chairs—not misery or humiliation.

## 10. Campaign phases

- Trace: P0–P1, product noticed but unexplained.
- Recognition: product repeats naturally through distinct moments.
- Reveal: clear product and collection identity.
- Release: availability, time and genuine constraints.
- Proof: fit, fabric, construction, colour and alternate wearers.
- Continuation: later moments return the product to the wider world.

## 11. Studio workflow and review

For each placement record source production/scene/moment/asset, channel, format, crop, order, prominence, narrative role, caption role, date, phase, approval, reuse and continuity impact.

Review gates: world fit, platform fit, commercial role and channel differentiation. Reject duplicate sequences, trend-led execution, forced product, bad crops, no story, pillar imbalance or asset overuse using stable `CHANNEL_*` codes.

The audience should encounter several outputs and gradually realise they are seeing different pieces of the same night.
