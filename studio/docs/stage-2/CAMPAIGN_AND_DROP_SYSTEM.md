# SHIRTFACED — CAMPAIGN & DROP SYSTEM

**Status:** Locked foundation  
**Stage:** 2  
**Date:** 5 August 2026

## 1. Purpose

This document defines how a Shirtfaced collection enters the existing universe, becomes recognisable, goes on sale and continues after release. A drop is not a temporary visual takeover. It is a new chapter in the same memory.

The system governs documentary photography, product photography, website, Instagram, Reels, TikTok, paid social and email as one connected release.

## 2. Campaign unit

Every campaign is built from:

- **Collection:** the commercial range.
- **World:** the permanent universe.
- **Chapter:** the campaign’s distinct stretch of time and place.
- **Events:** connected social moments inside the chapter.
- **Asset families:** still, moving, product and detail evidence from each event.
- **Placements:** channel-specific uses.
- **Phases:** the audience’s progression from trace to continuation.

A collection can introduce new garments, colours and seasonal conditions. It cannot reset the cast, emotional logic or photographic language.

## 3. Campaign premise

Before production, define one plain-language premise that would still work without clothing.

Good: “The night refuses to end, so everyone drives to the lookout.”  
Weak: “A premium winter essentials campaign.”

The premise determines action, locations and emotional movement. Products are assigned roles after the event is believable.

## 4. Phase architecture

### Phase 1 — Trace

**Audience state:** Something new is present.  
**Product:** P0–P1.  
**Outputs:** documentary frames, night details, incidental wear, quiet objects.  
**Copy:** little or none.  
**Exit condition:** the product has appeared in at least two distinct, natural contexts.

Trace is recognition-building, not artificial secrecy. Never crop or conceal a product as a gimmick.

### Phase 2 — Recognition

**Audience state:** The recurring garment or colour becomes familiar.  
**Product:** P1–P2.  
**Outputs:** another wearer, alternate angle, movement clip, environmental object.  
**Copy:** observational; collection may remain unnamed.  
**Exit condition:** the range has a recognisable visual anchor without one image being overused.

### Phase 3 — Reveal

**Audience state:** Familiarity becomes clarity.  
**Product:** P2–P3.  
**Outputs:** collection name, clear hero garments, collection landing page, first fit and detail proof.  
**Copy:** what it is and when it arrives.  
**Exit condition:** audience can identify the collection and release timing.

### Phase 4 — Release

**Audience state:** It is available.  
**Product:** P2–P3.  
**Outputs:** launch homepage, complete collection/product pages, release email, paid rotation and direct social post.  
**Copy:** what, when, where and only truthful constraints.  
**Exit condition:** purchase is clear on mobile and desktop and all product truth is approved.

### Phase 5 — Proof

**Audience state:** Remaining uncertainty is reduced.  
**Product:** P2–P3.  
**Outputs:** fit across bodies, movement, fabric, construction, alternate colours, packaging and FAQs.  
**Copy:** factual and useful.  
**Exit condition:** each live product meets minimum coverage and common questions are answered.

### Phase 6 — Continuation

**Audience state:** The collection belongs to Shirtfaced, not merely launch week.  
**Product:** P0–P2.  
**Outputs:** later-night moments, morning-after calm, customer wear where valid, archive story and unresolved closing frames.  
**Copy:** returns to observation.  
**Exit condition:** the next chapter can begin without the current one feeling abruptly abandoned.

## 5. Product and cast rotation

Assign one or two lead products per phase, not per campaign forever. Rotate lead garment, colour, wearer and camera language. Accessories receive genuine hero opportunities rather than permanent supporting status. No group is dressed as a range display.

Studio must surface the previous three lead products, lead colours, cast leads, locations and camera positions before approving the next phase.

## 6. Release clock

Timing is configured per collection. A default shape is:

- T−21 to T−14: trace.
- T−14 to T−7: recognition.
- T−7 to T−2: reveal.
- T−1 to T+2: release.
- T+3 to T+14: proof.
- T+7 onward: continuation, overlapping proof.

This is a planning scaffold, not a visible countdown. Shorter campaigns may compress phases but cannot omit product truth or continuation.

## 7. Channel orchestration

| Phase | Instagram | Reels | TikTok | Website | Paid | Email |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Trace | Documentary/objects | Ambient fragment | Behaviour clue | No takeover | Usually off | Usually off |
| Recognition | Connected carousel | Short event | Character study | Archive/quiet module | Test anchor | Optional editorial |
| Reveal | Clearer carousel | Product naturally clearer | Product within story | Collection preview | Curiosity | First announcement |
| Release | Direct but restrained | Real action + date | Clear follow-up | Full launch | Broad rotation | Release email |
| Proof | Fit/detail | Movement proof | Useful micro-story | Product depth | Retargeting | Product fact |
| Continuation | Wider world | Later moment | Next behaviour | Archive continuation | Best performers only | Optional story |

Channel outputs must contribute different evidence from the same event family.

## 8. Launch asset matrix

Before release, the campaign requires:

- two saveworthy documentary anchors;
- one mobile and one desktop world hero;
- lead-product desire, front/alternate, fit, movement, construction and texture;
- equivalent minimum galleries for every live product;
- at least three distinct product/category hero opportunities;
- one vertical event sequence with ambient sound;
- one TikTok micro-story independent of a sales premise;
- two or more paid frames with different visual families;
- collection open, transitions and close;
- launch and proof email imagery;
- cart thumbnails and post-purchase image;
- approved crops and fallbacks.

## 9. Launch readiness

Studio calculates readiness in five independent gates:

1. **Canon:** all candidate assets pass world fit and continuity.
2. **Product:** every SKU/variant has accurate minimum coverage.
3. **Placement:** required website/channel placements have approved assets and crops.
4. **Operations:** product facts, price, stock, sizing, care, rights and release time are confirmed.
5. **Narrative:** phases connect and hero rotation is acceptable.

The overall campaign is blocked if any required gate fails. Readiness is not a single subjective percentage.

## 10. Scarcity, stock and changes

Scarcity is stated only when operationally true. Sold-out communication is calm and useful. Restocks are factual, not framed as manufactured drama.

If a product, colour or date changes, Studio identifies every affected placement. Outdated assets are withdrawn or re-reviewed. No image may imply an unavailable variant is the one being sold.

## 11. Performance learning

Record outcomes by asset family, prominence, placement and phase—not only by individual post. Useful signals include saves, qualified product-page visits, gallery engagement, size-guide use, add-to-cart and conversion.

Performance may influence selection and pacing. It may not overrule canon. A generic sales image performing once does not become the new world.

## 12. Review and rejection

Reject campaigns or placements for:

- `CAMPAIGN_NO_PREMISE`
- `CAMPAIGN_WORLD_RESET`
- `CAMPAIGN_PHASE_SKIPPED`
- `CAMPAIGN_PHASE_STALLED`
- `CAMPAIGN_PRODUCT_REPEAT`
- `CAMPAIGN_CAST_REPEAT`
- `CAMPAIGN_HERO_OVERUSE`
- `CAMPAIGN_FALSE_SCARCITY`
- `CAMPAIGN_INCOMPLETE_PRODUCT_TRUTH`
- `CAMPAIGN_CHANNEL_DUPLICATION`
- `CAMPAIGN_LAUNCH_NOT_READY`
- `CAMPAIGN_NO_CONTINUATION`

## 13. Final tests

**Recognition test:** Could someone recognise the campaign before seeing the name?  
**Release test:** Can a new customer understand and buy without sacrificing the world?  
**Continuation test:** Does the collection still exist after launch day as part of an ongoing memory?

The campaign starts before it looks like advertising and continues after it stops asking for a sale.
