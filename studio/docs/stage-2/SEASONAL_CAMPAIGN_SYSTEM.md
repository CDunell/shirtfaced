# SHIRTFACED — SEASONAL CAMPAIGN SYSTEM

**Status:** Locked foundation  
**Stage:** 2  
**Date:** 5 August 2026

## 1. Purpose

Season changes the conditions of the Shirtfaced world, not its identity. This document governs recurring seasonal chapters while protecting documentary truth, Australian specificity, optimism and continuity.

Seasonality comes from what people naturally do, wear and notice—not from imported retail calendars or decorative motifs.

## 2. Australian seasonal truth

Plan for the actual release region, climate and daylight. Australia is not one climate. Brisbane humidity, Melbourne cold, Hobart darkness and Perth summer are not interchangeable.

Each seasonal brief records region, dates, expected temperature range, sunset, likely weather, daylight character, relevant events and product function. These facts guide the chapter; they do not become clichés.

No northern-hemisphere Christmas snow, “fall” language, American school calendar, generic ski-lodge winter or July summer imagery.

## 3. What remains fixed

Across every season:

- same documentary camera logic;
- same recurring social world;
- ordinary Australians;
- optimism and possibility;
- products integrated through behaviour;
- blank-garment and unrelated-branding rules;
- saveworthy-image test;
- accurate products and believable conditions.

## 4. What may change

Season can change daylight, temperature, rain, layers, pace, locations, transport, objects, food, colour emphasis and the emotional stage of the night. These changes must have observable causes.

## 5. Seasonal chapter template

Every seasonal chapter defines:

1. real regional conditions;
2. one human premise independent of product;
3. place in the ongoing timeline;
4. recurring and new locations;
5. cast continuity and any justified additions;
6. product roles based on function;
7. light and colour notes;
8. weather contingencies;
9. campaign phases;
10. bridge into the next chapter.

## 6. Summer

Summer is later light, heat retained in concrete, open doors, outdoor tables, cars cooling down, short walks becoming long ones, lookout air and the relief of a servo or apartment.

Products appear through lighter layering, tees, caps and totes. Hoodies may be carried, tied around waists or borrowed later when temperature drops. Avoid beach-brand default, festival cosplay, resort imagery and relentless golden hour.

## 7. Autumn

Autumn is a transitional chapter: earlier darkness, inconsistent temperature, jackets appearing but not always worn, quieter outdoor areas and the first decision to move inside.

Use layering and garment movement honestly. Avoid imported orange-leaf symbolism where it does not exist locally and generic heritage styling.

## 8. Winter

Winter is compressed movement between warm interiors, breath in suitable climates, cold car seats, hands in pockets, borrowed layers, apartment heaters and bright servo light against dark streets.

Hoodies, crew necks, beanies and jackets can become clearer because they solve a real condition. Avoid fake snow, alpine cabins, luxury fireplaces and fashion-editorial rain unless the location genuinely supports them.

## 9. Spring

Spring is unstable weather, longer afternoons, layers removed during movement, outdoor tables returning, sudden rain and the social sense that more nights are beginning again.

Use optimism without turning the work pastel, floral or aspirational-lifestyle generic.

## 10. Cultural moments and holidays

Public holidays and cultural moments are used only when they naturally alter behaviour. Avoid flag-waving, costume, novelty copy and opportunistic appropriation.

Christmas may be hot, suburban, practical and socially messy without becoming a red-and-green retail set. New Year’s may be anticipation and movement without fireworks clichés. Local events require actual local knowledge and respectful casting.

## 11. Weather continuity

Weather is recorded per event and asset. Rain level, wet surfaces, wind, garment condition, umbrellas and vehicle state must agree. If conditions change, the sequence shows the transition.

Every production has a canon-preserving contingency: move naturally inside, under an awning, into a car, to a kitchen or to another real location. A contingency cannot become a studio reset.

## 12. Seasonal product logic

Lead products are chosen by believable use, commercial need and recent hero rotation. Do not force a hoodie into extreme heat or a lightweight tee as sufficient winter protection merely because stock needs promotion.

Carry-over products provide continuity between chapters. New products receive trace and recognition before reveal. Colour palettes remain accurate and emerge through the environment rather than seasonal graphics.

## 13. Annual rhythm

Maintain an annual continuity view showing campaign chapters, collections, lead products, cast emphasis, locations, time-of-night distribution, weather, colour and unresolved narrative threads.

At each seasonal boundary, include bridge assets: the first layer carried, last warm late-night frame, changing daylight, a product left in a car or another observed sign. The audience should feel time passing rather than a campaign changing templates.

## 14. Review and rejection

Review real climate fit, world continuity, functional product use, cultural accuracy, weather continuity and bridge quality.

Reject with:

- `SEASON_WRONG_REGION`
- `SEASON_WRONG_CLIMATE`
- `SEASON_IMPORTED_CLICHE`
- `SEASON_PRODUCT_FORCED`
- `SEASON_WEATHER_CONTINUITY`
- `SEASON_HOLIDAY_COSTUME`
- `SEASON_WORLD_RESET`
- `SEASON_NO_BRIDGE`
- `SEASON_GENERIC_PALETTE`
- `SEASON_UNVERIFIED_CULTURAL_CONTEXT`

## 15. Final test

Remove the seasonal props and ask whether the image still belongs unmistakably to Shirtfaced. Then restore the conditions and ask whether they feel locally true.

The season changes the memory’s weather. It does not replace the memory.
