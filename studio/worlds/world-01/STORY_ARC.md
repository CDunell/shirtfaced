# ONE — THE ARC

## The premise

**They get into the place the whole night was planned around, it isn't worth ten
minutes, so the night happens on the street instead.**

## The human truth

**Nobody has ever had a good night where the plan worked.**

The night you talk about for ten years is never the one you organised. It's the one
that stopped going to plan at half past ten and never recovered.

Nobody in this night is short of anything, hard done by, or to be felt sorry for,
and nothing is denied to them. The front door says no and Brock has the side one
open before anybody has finished being disappointed. They go in, they look at it,
and they choose the footpath.

## The rule the whole night runs on

**Shit goes down, and it leads to wilder better shit nobody saw coming.**

Not *it works out anyway*. Not *they make the best of it*. Every setback causes
something bigger than itself, and the payoff can arrive hours later.

| Goes wrong | Leads to |
|---|---|
| Refused at the front door | In through a bistro door propped on a milk crate |
| Four minutes of hard rain | The grate is running |
| A phone goes down the grate | Five people lift a stormwater grate off a wet road |
| The phone is dead | It turns itself back on at twenty to five on a stranger's fridge |
| A bloke two floors down tells them to shut up | He's on the balcony with them by twenty past six |

**Nothing is lost that isn't made up for**, even if it takes five hours and nobody
sees it coming.

## The night

Five of them at the corner pub at knock-off on a warm Friday. Brock got there first
and has ordered for everyone without asking and got all five right. The table seats
four, so Damo takes a stool off the next one. *"Two,"* Gary says. *"That's it."*
Nobody answers him.

By 9:26pm they're on the side street and Damo is carrying the stool. Nobody saw him
take it out and nobody asks.

At 10:31pm the bloke on the door goes down the line and stops at Damo. Nobody
argues. They're still standing there when Brock whistles from round the side, and
there's a bistro door propped on a milk crate with his foot in it. They're in.

At 11:05pm there's a four-piece in the back room and Damo is at the pool table not
taking his shot. Cue up like a mic stand, doing the whole chorus at the band. The
stool is on the pool table with a beer on it. Brock is singing and doesn't know this
song. Emma has both arms round a woman she met eleven minutes ago. Gary is at the
back wall watching all of it. Nobody in the band notices.

At 11:20pm it rains hard for four minutes. Damo makes the awning first and takes
the stool with him. Brock stops dead in the middle of the footpath and lets it
happen. Emma doesn't run. Gary walks through at the pace he was already walking.
Afterwards Casey does Emma's hair on the wet footpath with a tie off her own wrist.

At 11:32pm Emma's phone goes down the stormwater grate. Brock takes his hoodie off
and hands it to Casey before he gets down there. Gary comes back from up the street
with a wire coat hanger and nobody asks where from. It doesn't work, so they lift
the grate — all five, on a wet road, at twenty-five to twelve. The phone's dead. She
pockets it.

**That's the last time anybody sees five of them.**

Then nothing. A hundred and five minutes with no scene in them.

At 1:17am there are four at the servo. They came for ice and leave with three bags
of it, forty dollars of food nobody's going to eat, two chargers for phones that
aren't flat, and a pair of servo sunglasses Brock puts on at seventeen minutes past
one and doesn't take off for six hours. He gives Emma his hoodie.

At 2:35am they're on the kerb outside the takeaway with five containers, because
five is what they ordered without thinking about it. One stays shut. Eventually
Damo opens it and eats it.

At 3:41am Damo doesn't make the gate. He's flat on his back in the tray of a parked
ute with the stool on his chest and both arms round it. Brock looks back at him.
*"Nah,"* Casey says. *"Leave him. He's fine."* They all go up.

At 4:26am Casey's been through a stranger's freezer and Brock is cooking party pies
in a bucket hat that isn't his. Emma's phone turns itself back on at twenty to five
on top of the fridge.

At 5:58am four of them are on the balcony concrete watching it get light with the
stool empty between them. A bloke two floors down comes out in his jocks and tells
them to shut up. They go quiet for about forty seconds. He's up there with them by
twenty past six.

At 7:20am they walk back past the shut pub. Damo stands the stool against the doors.
Casey moves it two inches. On the way past he lifts a different one off the stack
under the awning and keeps walking.

## What changes from the first frame to the last

At 4:52pm they drag an extra stool to a table that doesn't fit them.
At 7:20am they give it back and take a different one.

The night loses a person and keeps the furniture, and only one of those gets dealt
with.

## Where the tension and the joke live

The tension is entirely in the viewer. The joke is that the group has none.

Nothing in any frame, caption, cut or overlay refers to the absence. Nobody looks
for anyone. Nobody rings anyone. Nobody says a name.

## The test every feed post has to pass

**Would anybody tell somebody about this on Monday?**

Recognition gets a nod. An event gets retold. A scene that is only *true* is texture,
and texture goes to the Story reservoir or the website, never one of ten slots.

## Story quality gates — `STORY_ENGINE.md` §5

- **World fit** — Pass. A stool that came along, a night that didn't go to plan, and a bloke asleep in a ute tray. None of it needs a logo.
- **Story change** — Pass. Five and a borrowed stool become four and a different stool. A phone is gone and comes back. Nobody got in anywhere they meant to.
- **Character motive** — Pass. Brock finds the other door, Damo takes the stool, Casey fixes what's in front of her, Emma moves first, Gary decides for himself.
- **Garment naturalism** — Pass. The hoodie moves at 1:17am because a bloke's cold-blooded generous, not because it needed a turn.
- **Australian grounding** — Pass. Corner pub, side street, pub band, stormwater grate, servo, late takeaway, brown-brick walk-up.
- **Originality** — Pass. Grammar borrowed by technique only. No director named anywhere.
- **Social extractability** — Pass. Twelve scenes, ten posts, and three of them recruit on their own.
- **AI producibility** — Five locked identities via reference images, one prop across eleven scenes, one weather change, one continuous fatigue curve.
- **Ending value** — Pass. They bring it back and take another one. The rare win immediately before fucking it up again.

# THREE — SHOT SIZE

Every camera in the first two drafts was ten to twenty metres away, because the
Locked Reference is a seven-person street scene shot from across the road and every
scene inherited its distance. At that range a garment is forty pixels wide and the
P2 levels above are physically impossible.

The observer rule says **close enough to be there, outside enough to be watching.**
Outside isn't distant. Somebody standing next to you at a bar is an observer.

| Size | Distance | Per ten-post cycle |
|---|---|---|
| WS / EWS | 10m+ | **No more than two.** Establishing only |
| MLS | 5–8m | Two |
| **MS / MCU** | **2–4m** | **Four. The default** |
| CU / ECU | Under 1.5m | Two |

**Every scene owes one frame under two metres.** That's where the garment is
legible and where the fit proof in `PROOF_LAYER.md` §4 comes from. A scene that
only exists as a wide hasn't been shot, it's been surveyed.

**Prompts state distance and lens.** "Photographed from across the street" is an
excuse. "Two metres, 85mm, standing" is a camera position.

Lens by size, as intent: WS 24–35mm · MLS 35mm · MS 50mm · MCU 85mm · CU 85–105mm.

**Close subject, compressed world behind.** `docs/foundations/DENSITY.md` §7 is the
other half of this rule and neither works alone. A wide of a dense street is busy
and the garment is forty pixels. The same street at 85mm from two metres — subject
filling the frame, forty metres of neon, poster, cable and other people's night
stacked and soft behind their shoulder — is the picture. **Every frame is four
layers deep: something cropping the near edge, the subject, strangers in motion
behind them, and light going away past all of it.**

# FOUR — THE TOWN

One town, never named, by anyone, in any frame or caption. No skyline, landmark,
bridge, stadium, water, legible plate or place name. The pub's own name lives on the
awning fascia above every camera line used here.

Everything inside a six-hundred-metre walk. **Everything here is built to
`docs/foundations/DENSITY.md`** — four layers, contaminated light, surfaces with
history, other people's night in every frame. Nowhere in this town has one of
anything.

**THE CORNER PUB** — two frontages meeting at a chamfered corner. Cream tile to
waist height, chipped along the bottom course where the kegs go past, dark green
above, repainted twice and both edges showing. Wide awning both sides with
fluorescent tubes under it, one dead, and forty years of moth in the diffusers.

Beer garden through a side gate: concrete with three different pours in it, one big
fig through the middle, roots lifting the slab, festoons stapled up the trunk in a
line that isn't straight and a second line somebody abandoned. Long timber tables
carved to death. Loose stools that don't match. A hosed patch by the servery hatch
that never dries. Full of people straight from work at five and full of different
people by nine.

**Back room** — low stage against the far wall, PA stack, a mess of cable gaffered
down the edge, gig posters four deep on the side wall with corners lifting. Pool
table under a single hanging lamp, felt worn white along the rail. **Red neon
behind the bar and cold fluoro over the pool table, and everyone in the room is lit
by both.** Bottle shop drive-through on the side street. Furniture upside down on
the tables at close.

**THE SIDE STREET** — one block, pub to crossing, and it is never empty except once.
Both walls are painted over graffiti painted over paint, three greys that don't
match. Paste-ups over paste-ups, torn back to four layers, none of them legible.
Sticker archaeology up every pole. A roller-door workshop halfway down with rust
runs under the brackets and a compressor line clipped along the wall.

Milk crates stacked by the workshop, a second stack down by the crossing, and a
loose one somebody sits on. **Two street lights, one sodium and one that's been
replaced with cold white, so the block has an orange half and a green half and
people walk out of one into the other.** A lit shopfront a hundred metres down that
nobody in this story ever goes into. A stormwater grate that's always slightly wet
because the workshop hoses out.

**Stencilled lettering on the workshop wall reading `LEAVE HIM, HE'S FINE`** —
Environmental Branding as street art, never centred, never in focus, never fully in
frame, half over the top of something older, and nobody ever looks at it.

**THE SERVO** — corner of the next block. Four bowsers under a canopy of hard
fluoro that reaches about four metres and then stops dead, so the frame is white in
the middle and black at the edges. A night window in the wall with a card reader on
a stalk, an ice freezer with a broken handle and a decal sun-bleached to nothing, a
dented bin, a squeegee bucket with no squeegee. Wet asphalt doubling the whole
canopy. Price board at the kerb, off to one side, soft. Somebody else is always
filling up.

**THE TAKEAWAY** — mid-block opposite. Wide window, stainless ledge worn to a shine
where forearms go, tube sign with the last letter dead. Menu boards over older menu
boards with the prices relabelled in different fonts. Two plastic chairs chained to
a post. A small fixed camera under the eave. Fluoro inside throwing a hard rectangle
onto the footpath, sodium everywhere outside it, and the gutter running.

**THE WALK-UP** — three storeys, brown brick, two blocks on. Residents' car park
with a painted number on each bay half worn off, a low front fence, a gate that
doesn't shut, a letterbox bank inside it with two doors missing and a dead succulent
on top. Open-air stairwell with one bulb, a bike frame chained to the rail, and
somebody's washing on every level. Top-floor balcony facing east over back yards and
low rooftops — aerials, air-con boxes, a water heater, three other balconies with
lights on at four in the morning. No skyline.
