# SHIRTFACED --- WORLD 01

## THE BIG NIGHT

> **The night has just started, or it has completely gone off the rails.
> Either way, nobody wants to go home.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Purpose

World 01 is the flagship visual universe for Shirtfaced.

It is not product photography.

It is not fashion photography.

It is a believable documentation of Australian nights out where the
people, places and atmosphere sell the lifestyle, and the products
simply belong within it.

Every photograph should be good enough that someone would share it even
if nobody was wearing Shirtfaced.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Emotional Tone

The night is full of possibility.

Every photograph should communicate some combination of:

-   Optimism
-   Spontaneity
-   Recklessness
-   Camaraderie
-   Humour
-   Confidence
-   Curiosity

The feeling is:

-   "Fuck yeah."
-   "One more."
-   "We'll work it out."
-   "This'll be funny."
-   "Who's driving?"
-   "Let's go."

Not chaos.

Not violence.

Not intoxication as the joke.

The humour comes from shared experiences and the confidence of being
with good mates.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Lighting

Lighting must always come from believable real-world sources.

Never studio lighting.

Never artificial beauty lighting.

Primary sources include:

-   Street lights
-   Pub beer garden festoons
-   Fluorescent servo canopies
-   Neon signage
-   Pool table lamps
-   Jukebox glow
-   Headlights
-   Brake lights
-   Bottle shop lighting
-   Motel signs
-   Venue spill light
-   Phone torch
-   Occasional harsh on-camera flash

Images may be dark.

Faces must remain readable.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Colour Palette

Warm Australian nights.

-   Amber
-   Sodium orange
-   Deep green
-   Brick red
-   Beer gold
-   Neon red
-   Wet asphalt reflections
-   Dark charcoal
-   Timber browns

No HDR.

No exaggerated cinematic grading.

No teal/orange blockbuster look.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Photography Language

35mm documentary.

High ISO.

Natural grain.

Available light wherever possible.

Occasional direct flash.

Imperfect framing.

Natural motion blur is welcome.

People are sometimes cropped.

Foreground objects may partially obscure the frame, but the object has
to be something that is actually there. A shoulder, a pole, a doorway,
the tray of the ute in the shot. Not a part of some other vehicle
hanging in the foreground with no car attached to it — a wing mirror
floating at the edge of frame reads as a mistake, not as reportage,
because a real photographer standing there would have been standing
somewhere.

The photograph should feel discovered rather than directed.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Locations

Authentic Australian environments only.

Examples include:

-   Country pubs
-   Beer gardens
-   RSLs
-   Bowlos
-   Music venues
-   Festivals
-   Servos
-   Maccas car parks
-   Taxi ranks
-   Bottle shops
-   Apartment balconies
-   House parties
-   Kitchen kick-ons
-   Side streets
-   Late-night takeaway shops
-   Motel car parks

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# People

Ordinary Australians.

Not models.

Not influencers.

No exaggerated styling.

No posing.

Nobody acknowledges the camera.

People interact with each other rather than the photographer.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Wardrobe

The products belong naturally within the scene.

A group may include a mix of:

-   Shirtfaced t-shirts
-   Hoodies
-   Caps
-   Accessories

Products are distributed naturally across the group.

No single product dominates the photograph.

The supplied artwork must remain accurate and legible.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Environmental Branding

The Shirtfaced world quietly exists beyond the clothing.

Examples include:

-   A neon Shirtfaced slogan in a pub.
-   A faded esky sticker.
-   A stubby cooler.
-   A coaster.
-   A small window decal.
-   A weathered poster.
-   Tasteful street art.
-   A hand stamp.
-   A tote bag.
-   A cooler sticker.

These are easter eggs, not advertisements.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Composition

The shirt should be visible because of what the subject is doing.

Never because they are showing it.

Allow natural opportunities for:

-   Blank front surfaces
-   Blank back surfaces
-   Caps
-   Hoodies
-   Accessories

Products should appear discovered.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Reference Standard — The Photo We'd Post Anyway

Every image in World 1 must be capable of standing on its own as an
Instagram-worthy documentary photograph, even if every item of clothing
were plain black.

The image itself is the hero.

The product is incidental.

The clothing should never look styled, displayed or advertised.

Instead, it should feel like genuine clothing people happened to be
wearing during a memorable night out.

Success is measured by one question:

> Would someone save or share this photograph if there wasn't a
> Shirtfaced product anywhere in it?

If the answer is no, the image fails.

If the answer is yes, the product placement becomes more valuable
because it exists inside a genuinely desirable photograph rather than a
staged advertisement.

## Locked Reference

The six-friends Friday night street photograph becomes the visual
benchmark for World 1.

It defines the tone for the entire collection:

- Genuine Australian friendships.
- Candid documentary photography.
- Warm pub and street lighting.
- Slightly imperfect framing.
- Natural film grain.
- Small moments between friends rather than big performances.
- Optimism, momentum and possibility.
- Clothing that feels worn, not modelled.
- Every image worthy of being posted on Instagram regardless of the
  apparel.

# Global Production Rule — Branding

These are everyday people doing everyday things. They do not live in a
sanitised world, and a night out where every sign is blank and every packet is
unmarked reads as a set, not as a memory.

So the test is not whether branding is visible. It is two questions, in order.

## One — Do We Sell It?

If Shirtfaced makes it, no other brand's version of it appears anywhere in
frame.

Every t-shirt, crop, hoodie, jumper, cap, hat, overshirt, jacket and tote
bag carries:

- No logos
- No graphics
- No printed text
- No embroidery
- No visible labels
- No recognisable brand marks

This holds whether the garment is worn, carried, folded on a seat, or printed
on a poster in the background. A competitor's tee beside a blank one is the
single image that costs us, and it costs us wherever in the frame it sits.

There is no background exemption for apparel. Blank, always.

## Two — Is It The Hero?

Everything we do not sell may carry its real branding, as long as it is
background filler.

Servo price boards, shopfronts, trams and buses, street signage, food
packaging, drink cans, delivery bags, till receipts — the ordinary marked
clutter of a Friday night is welcome, because its absence is what makes a
photograph look staged.

It stays background. That means:

- Never centred.
- Never the reason the frame exists.
- Never held up, presented or displayed.
- Never so legible or so lit that the eye goes to it first.

A servo sign across wet asphalt is scenery. The same sign filling a third of
the frame is an advertisement for somebody else.

## Shirtfaced Is Still The Exception

Shirtfaced's own marks are not bound by the hero test, because it is our
photograph.

The environmental easter eggs described under Environmental Branding are
permitted: a neon slogan in a pub, a faded esky sticker, a coaster, a window
decal, a weathered poster, street art, a hand stamp.

They must still remain incidental scenery rather than an advertisement.

The blank-garment rule under **One** is not relaxed by anything here.

# Product Rotation & Vehicle Canon

## Product Rotation

Across the World 1 image series, every Shirtfaced product shall naturally
become the most visible product at different times.

Do not repeatedly favour the same garment.

Rotate prominence between:

- Vintage-washed black heavyweight t-shirt
- Crop
- Black heavyweight hoodie
- Jumper
- Hat — cap, beanie or bucket hat

The hoodie tied around the waist is a way of wearing the hoodie rather
than a separate product, and it counts as the hoodie's turn.

What we sell is t-shirts, crops, hoodies, jumpers and hats, and hats
means caps, beanies and bucket hats. Everything else is cart filler: it
may appear, but it is never the hero of a frame, and no composition is
built around making it legible. The tote bag is the case that keeps
coming up. Either it is photographed deliberately as a product, which
is a different job from these scenes, or a subject simply happens to be
carrying one, which is incidental.

The range will expand. This list is what we sell today, not a closed
set, so read it as the current range rather than a permanent one, and
expect to add to it rather than rewrite it.

Black is the documented seller for the t-shirt, the hoodie and the cap,
so those stay black. It is not established for anything else, and the
colour of a filler item is free.

The cap must regularly be photographed front-on so the front panel is
clearly visible.

Product visibility must arise naturally from the scene, never from
modelling or deliberate display.

## Vehicle Canon

The camera never sits inside a vehicle, and nobody is ever entering or
leaving one.

Interacting with a car is fine. Using it as the mechanism of the scene
is not. Once the photograph depends on the vehicle working — getting
in, getting out, being carried from one place to the next — the
geometry has to be right, and it is not. A cabin produced cars with no
seats and three across a front row built for two. Piling in produced a
van with no back end, the road visible through the opening beneath a
correctly raised tailgate. A body is asked to occupy space the
structure does not have, so the structure is deleted to make room, and
fixing that costs more attempts than the frame is worth.

The scene happens beside the car, not through it: leaning on it,
sitting on the tray, standing at the kerb, talking through an open
window to whoever is already sitting in it. A seated passenger is fine.
The act of getting in or out is not.

The cast stands on the footpath. Nobody leans through a door opening
and nobody puts their head and shoulders inside the cabin — that is
half-entering by another name, and it is what forces the model to
invent room that is not there. The window is where the two sides meet,
and it stays a window: the opening is at the shoulder of a seated
person, not a doorway down to the seat.

The car is a prop, never the hero.

Where a ute appears it shall always be recognisably Australian.

Colour, cab configuration and age are open. A white single-cab tray-back
is a fine default, not a requirement: dual cabs are common on Australian
worksites and a black one is not a fault. What matters is that it reads
as a working vehicle someone actually owns rather than a marketing one.

Typical:

- Tray-back ute with an open tray
- Tradie specification
- Slightly used but well cared for

Never generate:

- American pickup trucks
- Enclosed pickup tubs
- Glossy lifestyle hero trucks, whatever the cab configuration
- Oversized lifted 4WDs
- Cab-over or forward-control trucks, where the cab sits over the front
  axle and the vehicle has no bonnet

# Operating System

World 1 is managed through two active production roles.

These roles are not optional commentary layers.

They are part of the production process for every generated image.

## Role 01 — World Architect

The World Architect owns the World 1 canon.

Its responsibilities are:

- Maintain the emotional, photographic and cultural rules of World 1.
- Add new production rules discovered through generation.
- Strengthen prompts against recurring model drift.
- Protect Australian authenticity.
- Maintain product visibility and product rotation rules.
- Maintain vehicle, wardrobe, lighting and composition canon.
- Record approved recurring motifs.
- Record rejected visual directions so they do not return.
- Convert useful critiques into permanent production rules.

The World Architect does not critique images generally.

It only changes canon when a new repeatable rule has been discovered.

After every generation it asks:

> What new permanent rule, if any, did this image reveal?

If no new rule was discovered, the World Bible shall not be changed.

## Role 03 — Continuity Director

The Continuity Director reviews every generated image before it is accepted.

It does not generate concepts.

It does not rewrite the entire prompt.

It checks whether the image belongs inside World 1.

Every image is reviewed against the following tests.

### Mood

- Does the image retain optimism, momentum and possibility?
- Does the night still feel alive?
- Has the scene drifted into resignation, exhaustion or end-of-night sadness?
- Has it become drunken comedy, stock photography or a fashion campaign?

### Australian Authenticity

- Does the environment feel unmistakably Australian?
- Are vehicles, streets, venues, architecture and behaviour plausible?
- Has the image drifted toward generic North America or Europe?

### Product Visibility

- Is the nominated hero product clearly visible?
- Is it completely blank?
- Is it naturally visible rather than deliberately displayed?
- Has the same product been favoured too often?
- Is the black cap front panel visible when it is the nominated hero?

### Vehicle Continuity

- Is every ute an Australian tray-back ute?
- Is the tray open aluminium alloy?
- Has the model generated an American pickup tub or lifestyle dual-cab?
- Is the vehicle secondary to the people and story?

### Wardrobe Balance

- Are Shirtfaced-ready black garments distributed naturally?
- Is there enough black clothing for placement?
- Is the entire group dressed in black without reason?
- Are non-product clothes varied in colour, cut and texture?
- Is all visible clothing free of logos, graphics and brand marks?

### Composition

- Is the image worth posting without the product?
- Does the framing feel accidental and human?
- Is the photographer's plausible physical position clear?
- Has the scene become overly clean, symmetrical or staged?
- Does one crop, obstruction or foreground element strengthen the documentary feeling?

### Documentary Credibility

- Does the photograph feel taken rather than designed?
- Can the viewer imagine where the photographer was standing?
- Are people interacting with each other rather than performing for the camera?
- Do motion, lighting and framing feel naturally imperfect?

### Story

- Can the viewer understand what happened five seconds before?
- Can the viewer imagine what happens five seconds after?
- Does the image contain a genuine social moment rather than people merely occupying a location?

## Review Outcome

Every image receives one of three outcomes:

### APPROVED

The image belongs in World 1 without material correction.

### APPROVED WITH NOTE

The image belongs in World 1, but a new rule or continuity note must be recorded.

### REJECTED

The image fails one or more foundational rules and shall not become a reference.

The Continuity Director reports only:

- The outcome.
- The strongest success.
- The material drift.
- Any genuinely new rule.
- The next hero product and unused camera perspective.

It shall not produce a long generic critique.

## Production Loop

Every image follows this sequence:

1. Select a scene.
2. Select one hero product.
3. Select a camera position not recently overused.
4. Generate the image.
5. Run the Continuity Director review.
6. Approve, approve with note or reject.
7. Record the result in the Continuity Ledger.
8. Add a new canon rule only when the image revealed a repeatable issue.
9. Build the next prompt using the updated ledger and canon.

## Continuity Ledger

A separate Continuity Ledger shall be maintained alongside this World Bible.

The ledger records:

- Scene name
- Time
- Location
- Hero product
- Secondary product placements
- Camera position
- Lighting source
- Vehicle type
- Mood result
- Australian authenticity result
- Branding result
- Approval status
- New rules discovered
- Rejected drift
- Reference image status
- Notes for the next prompt

The World Bible defines the rules.

The Continuity Ledger records what has already been used.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Prompt Construction Protocol

Before writing a new World 1 prompt, confirm:

- The emotional moment contains momentum, optimism or possibility.
- The scene has a clear social action.
- The nominated hero product differs from the recent sequence where practical.
- The camera position differs from the recent sequence.
- Product surfaces remain naturally visible.
- All garments and packaging are blank and unbranded.
- Any ute is an Australian tray-back with an open aluminium alloy tray.
- The photograph remains valuable without Shirtfaced placement.
- The scene adds something new to World 1 rather than repeating an approved frame.

Each prompt shall nominate:

- One hero product.
- One main social action.
- One camera position.
- One dominant real-world light source.
- One documentary imperfection.
- One emotional reason the night is continuing.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Success Test

Every image should answer "yes" to all of these:

-   Would someone share this on Instagram without the products?
-   Does it feel like a real memory?
-   Does it make the viewer wish they had been there?
-   Is the product clearly visible without feeling displayed?
-   Does it feel unmistakably Australian?
-   Does it feel unmistakably Shirtfaced?
