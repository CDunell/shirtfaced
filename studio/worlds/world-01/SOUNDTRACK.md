## 2. Song identity and dramatic job

### Concept: the week stays outside

The lyric is not about drinking, bravado or larrikin caricature. It is about
arriving depleted and becoming electrically present because everyone else is
present too. Damo is not performing at the crowd; he is the first visible
conductor of a release already spreading through the pub. The chorus makes a
packed room plausible because it is readable on first hearing and physically
easy to shout.

Make one compact, hook-first anthem — not "background music." It should sound
like the room discovers its own voice at once: relief after a long week turning
into collective permission to lose it. The signature is a four-hit verbal
release: **"Hands up / hearts out / whole room / right now."** Each unit lands
as a separate crowd punch, but the notes, rhythm, words, harmony and sound are
newly authored for WORLD 01.

### Title candidates

| Rank | Title | Usefulness |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **All In Tonight** | Emotional, communal and premium; central phrase is singable and editable. |
| 2 | The Week Stays Outside | More distinctive and cinematic; strongest campaign line, slightly less immediate as a chant. |
| 3 | Whole Room Right Now | Directly describes the hero scene and foregrounds the signature four-hit hook. |
| 4 | Lights Up Late | Sleeker and more impressionistic; useful if the brand wants less overt pub energy. |
| 5 | Leave Monday Outside | Memorable but narrower and more novelty-adjacent; reserve as a lyric, not the main title. |

> **LOCK RECOMMENDATION** — Use "All In Tonight" as the working and release
> title. Use "the week stays outside" as the lyrical thesis and campaign line.

### Originality boundaries

- Do not provide any reference recording to the composer as a structural template.
- Do not ask for "in the style of" any artist, band, producer or identifiable
  song. Describe energy, density, texture and dramatic function only.
- No pre-existing samples, loop-library melodic phrases, purchased beats,
  uncleared crowd recordings or cloned voices. Any production loops must be
  disclosed and licensed for standalone commercial release.
- Run a human similarity check before approval: melody contour, hook rhythm,
  bass motif, progression/voicing, lyric phrase and arrangement silhouette
  against obvious catalogue neighbours. If anyone says "that sounds like…",
  revise before mastering.

## 3. Musical design

| Element | Locked direction |
|---|---|
| Tempo / meter | 132 BPM, 4/4. Straight eighth-note drive; human push in choruses, no EDM four-on-floor feel. |
| Key / range | D major, verses coloured by B minor. Lead target A3–A4; optional D5 octave/double at final release. Transpose to C or E only after casting. |
| Tonal shape | Warm suspended major sonorities; one borrowed minor-subdominant colour at the chorus turnaround for emotional bite. |
| Rhythm identity | Muted downstroke guitar and dry kick establish motion; tom pickup and handclap flam open the chorus; signature hook occupies four emphatic attacks. |
| Dynamic promise | Verse feels shoulder-to-shoulder; chorus adds width, octave, room and human bodies — not just limiter level. |

### Harmonic direction

Suggested eight-bar chorus frame. The composer may improve it while preserving
distinctiveness.

```
CHORUS CHANGES
| D(add9)  A/C# | Bm7  G6 | D/F#  A(sus4) | G(add9)  A |
| Em7  Bm/D     | G6  Gm6 | D/A  A(sus4)  | D(add9)    |
```

The **Gm6** borrowed chord is the signature ache before home. Avoid a continuous
stock four-chord loop; let bass inversions and the two-bar harmonic answer give
the hook its own footprint.

- **Verse** may alternate Bm7 – Gmaj7 – D/F# with clipped A(add4) answers.
- **Pre-chorus** should simplify to Em7 – G(add9) – Bm7 – A(sus), thinning the
  arrangement so the room inhalation is audible.

### Instrumentation

- **Drums** — dry, close kick; woody snare; live hi-hat; floor-tom lift;
  imperfect group claps. Add a natural pub-room re-amp or owned room impulse,
  not a stadium preset.
- **Bass** — melodic electric bass with pick/finger attack; anchors inversions
  and briefly climbs into the hook. No sub-heavy dance bass.
- **Guitars** — one tight, slightly gritty mono rhythm guitar; one wide bright
  octave/answer guitar; sparse open-string shimmer. Avoid retro pub-rock crunch.
- **Keys / synth** — low-level analogue-style pulse, soft organ/noise bed and a
  restrained high counterline. Synth supports modern scale without becoming
  synth-pop pastiche.
- **Human texture** — boot/bench thump, breath, glass-safe room movement and an
  owned, directed crowd bed recorded after clean vocal takes. No identifiable
  venue ambience without release.
- **Optional colour** — one short baritone sax or processed brass swell in the
  final chorus only; it must read as pressure, not ska or comedy.

## 4. Lyrics and hooks

### Primary chorus — recommended

```
CHORUS A — "ALL IN TONIGHT"

We came in half alive
Now we're all in tonight
HANDS UP! / HEARTS OUT! / WHOLE ROOM! / RIGHT NOW!
If the week wants a fight
It can wait outside
We're all in, all in tonight
```

**Melodic instruction:** "half alive / all in tonight" rises by a newly composed
stepwise-plus-fourth contour, then the four crowd hits descend or pivot around
stable chord tones. Do not use four repeated "yeah" syllables, or the contour or
rhythm of any known chorus. "All in, all in tonight" resolves later than
expected across the barline.

### Alternative hook systems

| Option | Four-hit release | Chorus landing | Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| B | LIGHTS ON! / WEEK OFF! / COME IN! / LET GO! | Leave the whole week outside | Sharper campaign language; more percussive. |
| C | ONE ROOM! / ONE SOUND! / FEET UP! / GROUND DOWN! | The whole room's rising now | More physical; test "feet up" for visual fit. |
| D | BREATHE IN! / BREAK OUT! / ALL HERE! / RIGHT NOW! | We were waiting for tonight | Most emotional; slightly less pub-specific. |

Only one system survives. Do not stack alternatives into the final song. The
producer must create and record **two genuinely different melodic settings of
Chorus A** at demo stage; selection is based on first-listen recall, crowd
singability and similarity review.

### Full lyric architecture

| Section | Narrative content | Required images / exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| Verse 1 | Arrival: fluorescent week, phone face-down, doors open, familiar strangers become one room. | Concrete but not brand-name; no alcohol dependency, misogyny, fighting or blokey caricature. |
| Pre 1 | Sound drops; a look crosses the room; the first inhale. | Short vowels and rising consonants; leave one beat of air. |
| Chorus | Collective conversion from half-alive to fully present. | Primary Chorus A; plain language; immediate on first play. |
| Verse 2 | Different corners synchronise: table, bar, doorway, old mate / new face. | Widen beyond Damo; crowd is protagonist. |
| Bridge | Monday can knock but nobody answers; half-time unison confession. | One new line only; avoid sentimental speech. |
| Final chorus | Everyone owns it; extra response line and octave lift. | No key change required; increase human density, not cheese. |

### Seed lines for the commissioned writer

- "Phone face-down, let the bad week ring."
- "We walked in separate; the kick made us one."
- "If Monday wants us, it can wait outside."
- "Half alive at the door / all in by the first downbeat."

These are brief assets, not a mandate to force every line into the song. The
commissioned writer must warrant that the final lyric and melody are original,
and disclose all co-writers.

## 5. Arrangement and timeline

| Time | Bars / section | Production event |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:07 | 4 bars — cold open | One shouted room pickup ("oi—" or breath only), muted guitar, kick and bass motif. Title-level recognisability by second two. |
| 0:07–0:29 | 12 bars — verse 1 | Lead intimate and close; rhythm guitar mono; bass enters bar 5; room detail stays low. |
| 0:29–0:40 | 6 bars — pre 1 | Hats thin, tom heartbeat, gang breath / one-word response. Final half-bar near-silence. |
| 0:40–1:02 | 12 bars — chorus 1 | Full width, Chorus A, four-hit gang hook at bars 5–6; borrowed-minor turn; short post-hook guitar answer. |
| 1:02–1:14.5 | 7 bars — sync reprise / edit handle | Purpose-built clean pickup plus the primary 12.5-second hero sync. See §6. |
| 1:14.5–1:36 | 12 bars — verse 2 | Half-time first 2 bars, then drive returns; new counter-guitar; crowd answers grow. |
| 1:36–1:47 | 6 bars — pre 2 | Earlier lift with additional upper harmony; one beat longer breath before downbeat. |
| 1:47–2:09 | 12 bars — chorus 2 | Wider gang, lead ad-libs only after core words remain clear. |
| 2:09–2:21 | 6 bars — bridge | Half-time drums, unison low vocal, "Monday" line; rising floor tom. |
| 2:21–2:43 | 12 bars — final chorus / out | Full ensemble, optional high double, extra crowd tail. End on hard D(add9) plus 1.5 seconds clean decay. |

> **EDITABILITY RULE** — Record clean downbeats, pickups, cymbal-free
> transitions and at least two hard endings. The full song may breathe, but
> every campaign edit must cut without a cymbal tail or lyric fragment betraying
> the join.

## 7. Vocal and gang design

### Lead character

Australian in phrasing without exaggerated accent. Gender is open, with a
preference for a warm, slightly frayed mid-register voice that can sound
conversational at verse level and authoritative without belting in the chorus.
Preserve consonants and breath. Moderate tuning is acceptable; no glassy pitch
correction or cloned timbre.

### Gang-vocal recipe

- Cast 3–5 adults with contrasting registers and at least two non-identical
  accents/timbres. Everyone signs performer consent and assignment before the
  session.
- Record six group passes around one stereo pair at different distances: tight
  unison, loose unison, low octave, high octave, spoken attack and exhausted
  final pass.
- Record individuals for corrective editing, but keep at least **60% real group
  bleed** in the hero hook.
- For each of the four hits, change internal emphasis: HANDS (wide), HEARTS
  (centre), WHOLE ROOM (largest), RIGHT NOW (shortest). Leave 30–60 ms human
  spread; do not grid every onset.
- Print clean, no-room and room-heavy gang stems. Crowd murmur is a separate
  stem and must not contain intelligible private speech.

### Production / mix direction — general terms only

- Contemporary live-band impact with disciplined low end, short rooms and wide
  choruses.
- Verses close, tactile and slightly underexposed; choruses brighter through
  arrangement and upper-mid density, not harsh mastering.
- Snare transient remains human; bass audible on phones; vocal hook intelligible
  at low playback level.
- Restraint over maximalism: one signature guitar answer, one pulse, one room
  identity. No retro costume, festival-EDM drop, pop-punk nasal vocal, novelty
  chant or faux stadium reverb.
- Master target should preserve punch rather than chase maximum loudness:
  deliver an integrated streaming master around **-10 to -9 LUFS** as a starting
  range, true peak no higher than **-1 dBTP**, plus an unmastered premaster.
  Final choice follows platform test, not a number.

### Required masters

  no-gang; gang-only feature; a cappella; clean version if any lyric changes.

### Stem package

| Bass | DI; amp/processed; printed bass bus. |

| Vocals | Lead comp dry; lead processed; doubles; harmonies; gang tight; gang loose; gang room; crowd murmur. |
| FX / room | Risers, impacts, owned pub-room textures and reverb returns separated. |
| Mix groups | Drums, bass, guitars, keys, lead, backing/gang, FX — all starting 00:00 and sample-aligned. |

### Session and documentation

- Native DAW session plus consolidated 24-bit / 48 kHz audio from 00:00; no
  missing plug-ins needed to reconstruct the stem mix.
- Tempo map, key, chord chart, lyric sheet, cue sheet data, ISRC placeholder,
  writer/performer/producer legal names and contact details.
- Complete sample / loop / plug-in disclosure log, including "none used"
  declarations. Attach receipts and licences for any permitted third-party
  production elements.
- Two backups and SHA-256 checksums for final WAVs. Filenames follow:
  `SF_W01_AllInTonight_[asset]_[duration]_[mix]_v##.wav`

### Acceptance tests

- Hook is understandable after one exposure and can be shouted by non-singers.
- The 12.5-second edit lands four visual attacks without rushing and exits cleanly.
- Mono fold-down retains lead, kick, bass and gang words; no major phase loss.
- Phone, laptop, small Bluetooth speaker, car and headphones all preserve hook
  intelligibility.
- Similarity panel of at least three listeners produces no consistent
  existing-song identification.
- All rights documents, disclosures and final source sessions arrive before
  final payment.

## 9. Ownership, IP and commissioning terms

> **CRITICAL AUSTRALIAN POSITION** — Paying for a commission does not
> automatically transfer the composition. APRA AMCOS states the composer or
> lyricist is generally the first owner, and a commissioner needs an agreement
> to own it. The sound recording is a separate copyright, and performers may
> also have interests. Use a lawyer-reviewed written assignment — not an invoice
> note or an attribution promise.

### Contract must cover

- **Parties and scope** — identify the SHIRTFACED owning entity and every
  writer, producer, vocalist, musician and subcontractor. No undisclosed
  contributors.
- **Present assignment** — each contributor assigns all right, title and
  interest in the composition, lyrics, arrangement, master, performances and
  project files to SHIRTFACED, worldwide, for the full copyright term including
  extensions, effective on payment. Include a backup exclusive, irrevocable,
  transferable, sublicensable licence if any assignment is ineffective.
- **Permitted exploitation** — unlimited advertising, brand, film, social, web,
  broadcast, events, retail, games, remix, edit, translation, adaptation,
  AI-assisted post tools, platform distribution and future media, with no
  further approval or fee.
- **Moral rights** — obtain specific, informed written consents for
  non-attribution, edits, excerpts, overlays, juxtaposition and other reasonably
  foreseeable brand uses. Do not rely on a blanket "waiver"; Australian moral
  rights remain personal.
- **Performers** — written performer consent covering recording, fixation,
  editing, sync, communication, publicity and assignment of any performer or
  master interests; confirm no union or reuse payments unless stated.
- **Originality warranties** — no infringement, sample, interpolation, cloned
  voice, restricted loop, conflicting publishing/label/management agreement or
  generative output unless pre-approved and fully disclosed. Contributor
  indemnity should be proportionate and supported by approval controls.
- **Payment** — fixed fee, milestones (e.g. 30% start / 30% approved demo / 40%
  delivery and signed chain-of-title), two revision rounds, kill fee, and no
  royalties, points or backend unless SHIRTFACED deliberately chooses them.
- **Credit** — no on-screen attribution required; metadata credit may name real
  human authors if legally or operationally needed. Ownership and authorship are
  not the same thing.
- **PRO / publishing** — require disclosure of APRA/AMCOS or other society
  memberships before signing. Address licence-back and opt-out mechanics if
  direct brand control of communication and performance rights is required;
  assignment alone may not unwind prior society mandates.
- **Records** — contributor schedule, split sheet totalling 100%, assignment
  deeds, performer releases, sample log, invoices, paid receipts and version
  archive form one chain-of-title folder.

Have Australian entertainment counsel adapt an Arts Law or equivalent music
commissioning template. **This document is production guidance, not legal
advice.**

| B — Lean emerging producer + separate mix | 1,200–2,500 + GST | Economical but requires strong A&R direction, careful vocalist casting and explicit deliverables. | GOOD PILOT |
| C — Marketplace micro-commission | 300–1,200 + FX/tax | Listings can be cheap, but service terms and "royalty-free" language are not enough for composition/master assignment. Quality and contributor disclosure vary. | DEMO / SCOUTING |

| E — Production-library licence | Hundreds to low thousands | Fast and licensable, but usually non-exclusive and not owned composition/master; weak canonical identity. | FALLBACK ONLY |

The preferred production path is a single accountable writer-producer plus one
lead singer, two or three gang-vocal contributors, and a mix/master engineer if
the producer is not strong at final delivery. Fixed-fee buyout target: **AUD
2,500–5,000 plus GST**, with two structured revision rounds. A lean
emerging-producer version may land around AUD 1,200–2,500; below that, reduce
scope rather than weaken the rights paperwork or deliverables.

> **BUDGET TO APPROVE** — AUD 3,500 + GST target, AUD 5,000 + GST ceiling, and
> AUD 500 contingency for legal review, extra vocalist or session hire, or one
> additional mix revision.

### Current service and tool findings

- **AirGigs** says that if a seller's description or custom offer does not
  mention royalties, work is treated as royalty-free, while also noting that
  commercial clients often request a signed work-for-hire agreement.
  "Royalty-free" still does not equal transfer of composition or master, so
  attach SHIRTFACED's contract.
- A current AirGigs example advertises a basic custom indie/folk song for
  US$200, illustrating the low marketplace floor — not a realistic benchmark for
  premium production, stems, casting, revisions and a clean buyout.
- **SoundBetter** is useful for discovering vetted producers and managing funded
  jobs, but its pre-existing "Tracks" product is designed for licensing a track
  under a new topline, not for owning a wholly bespoke canonical asset. Hire a
  custom producer and contract rights directly.
- **Suno**'s current terms say paid Pro/Premier users receive an assignment of
  Suno's rights in outputs generated during the paid term, while its terms also
  take a broad perpetual licence to submitted content. This is still not a
  warranty of copyrightability, uniqueness or non-infringement.
- **Udio**'s 2025 transition with major-label partners removed downloads during
  its move toward a controlled service; it is therefore unsuitable as a
  dependable canonical production pipeline at present.
