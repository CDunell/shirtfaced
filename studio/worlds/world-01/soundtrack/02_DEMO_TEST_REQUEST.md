## What to write

The primary chorus, set to your own original melody:

```
We came in half alive
Now we're all in tonight
HANDS UP! / HEARTS OUT! / WHOLE ROOM! / RIGHT NOW!
If the week wants a fight
It can wait outside
We're all in, all in tonight
```

**Melodic direction.** "Half alive / all in tonight" should rise on a newly
composed stepwise-plus-fourth contour. The four crowd hits then descend or pivot
around stable chord tones. "All in, all in tonight" resolves later than expected
across the barline.

Do not use four repeated "yeah" syllables, and do not reach for the contour or
rhythm of a chorus you already know.

**A starting harmonic frame** — improve it if you can, as long as it stays
distinctive:

```
| D(add9)  A/C# | Bm7  G6 | D/F#  A(sus4) | G(add9)  A |
| Em7  Bm/D     | G6  Gm6 | D/A  A(sus4)  | D(add9)    |
```

The Gm6 is the signature ache before home. Avoid a continuous stock four-chord
loop — bass inversions and a two-bar harmonic answer are what give the hook its
own footprint.

**Four-hit emphasis.** Each of the four attacks carries different weight: HANDS
wide, HEARTS centred, WHOLE ROOM largest, RIGHT NOW shortest. Leave 30–60 ms of
human spread; don't grid every onset.

## What to submit

1. **Two genuinely different melodic settings** of that chorus, each 45–60
   seconds, with simple but convincing production. Two settings, not one setting
   and a variation — this is the single most important thing we're testing.
2. **Chord chart, scratch lyric, tempo and key.**
3. **Named writer list**, PRO/publisher status, and a signed originality
   declaration: no samples, no library melodic phrases, no cloned voices, no
   generative full-song output.
4. **A short note** explaining how the 12.5-second hero section works cold from
   silence — how you'd build the pickup and the exit.
5. **Fixed full-production quote**, schedule, revision policy, and confirmation
   of the rights assignment and deliverables in the full brief.

Production polish is not what we're scoring. A convincing rough with the right
hook beats a slick one without it.

---

## How we choose

Weighted against hook recall and crowd usability first, then originality, then
production feel, then how well the 12.5-second section works against picture.
Vocal casting, editability and rights readiness carry the remainder.

Every demo is similarity-screened by a panel of at least three listeners before
we decide. If a demo draws a consistent "that sounds like…", it doesn't proceed
— which is why the two settings matter.
