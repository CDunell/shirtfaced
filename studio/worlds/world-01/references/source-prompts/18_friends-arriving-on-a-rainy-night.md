# Source Prompt

Photorealistic documentary photograph.

Australia.

Saturday morning.

3:18am.

A quiet suburban lookout overlooking the city.

The kick-ons have paused for five minutes.

Nobody suggested it.

Everyone simply ended up here.

Seven ordinary Australians in their mid-twenties to mid-thirties.

Four men.

Three women.

Nobody is posing.

Nobody notices the camera.

Nobody is looking at the view because they've all seen it before.

The view is just somewhere to keep talking.

One bloke sits on the bonnet of the ute telling the second half of the story nobody let him finish earlier.

A woman is laughing so hard she's almost spilling her drink.

Another sits cross-legged on the concrete barrier eating leftover chips from the bag.

One mate is leaning against the tray of the ute holding a plain black cap in one hand.

Another has climbed into the tray and is lying on his back looking at the stars.

A couple quietly share earbuds listening to a song for a few seconds while everyone else talks over the top.

Someone has a hoodie draped over another friend's shoulders because the air has turned cold.

Nobody is on their phone.

Nobody is checking the time.

Nobody wants the night to end.

The city glows softly in the distance.

Orange streetlights stretch to the horizon.

A faint breeze moves loose hair and hood strings.

The road behind is empty.

A single car quietly drives past in the background.

The atmosphere feels peaceful.

Hopeful.

Content.

The best part of the night wasn't planned.

Everything feels completely ordinary.

Everything feels unmistakably Australian.

Photographed from beside another parked car by the last friend walking over with food.

35mm documentary photography.

50mm lens.

Kodak Portra 400.

Available light only.

Natural film grain.

Slightly underexposed.

No HDR.

No cinematic colour grading.

Natural motion blur from people laughing and moving.

Imperfect framing.

Part of another parked car occupies the foreground.

One person is partially cropped by the edge of frame.

The photograph feels completely accidental.

It feels like the photograph somebody keeps framed because it reminds them what friendship felt like in their twenties.
