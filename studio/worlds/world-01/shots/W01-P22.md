# 5. Negative constraints

Per `SHOT_SPEC.md` §11, plus the drift this specific frame invites.

- No fifth person anywhere in frame, including background and reflection.
- **No hand, arm or limb that does not resolve to one of the four bodies.** Count
  hands. Eight, and every one attached.
- No garment being pulled over a head; no two people inside one garment.
- No logos, graphics, print, embroidery or labels on any t-shirt, crop, hoodie,
  jumper, cap or hat, anywhere in frame.
- No American pickup, no enclosed pickup tub, no lifted 4WD, no cab-over truck.
- Nobody entering or leaving the car. Nobody leaning through a door opening.
  Nobody's head inside a cabin.
- The camera is not on the forecourt and not inside the shop.
- No studio light, no beauty light, no rim light, no fill.
- No teal-orange grade, no HDR, no clean glossy asphalt.
- Nobody looking at the lens.
- Price board and any other marked signage stays small, off-centre, out of focus,
  never first-read.
- Ground stays wet. No dry patches under the canopy.

# 6. Acceptance gate

The image passes only if all of these are true.

1. **Four people.** Count them.
2. **Eight hands, all attached.**
3. Both subjects in the handover have both feet on the ground and one clear
   silhouette each.
4. The hoodie is a single garment held by two people at two separate points.
5. Every garment listed in §4 is blank.
6. The canopy is doubled in wet asphalt.
7. Faces readable despite the frame being dark.
8. The photographer's position is believable — low, outside the forecourt.
9. `PI-04` reads as a big frame and `PI-05` reads as small. **The fit contrast is
   the entire commercial value of this frame.**
10. Success Test: would someone save this if nobody was wearing anything of ours.

# 7. Where this will break, and what it means

Read this before running it.

## 7.1 — This is the highest-risk frame in the night, on purpose

`CONTINUITY.md` records exactly one anatomy failure so far: W01-015, *"Phantom hand
gripping his forearm, belonging to nobody."*

A garment changing hands between two people at close range is the single most
reliable way to produce that failure. Two bodies, four arms, one object, overlapping
limbs, and a model that will happily add a fifth hand to make the geometry work — the
same mechanism that deleted the back of a van to fit people in it.

So this frame is the right test precisely because it is the hardest one. **A first
attempt failing here is expected and is not evidence the pipeline is broken.**

`CONTINUITY.md`'s own precedence rule covers this: a rejected attempt is not a
rejected scene. Two or three attempts before it lands would be a normal result.

The staging in §3 — hoodie already off, held at arm's length, one hand each, no
overlap — exists to reduce this specific risk. It is not a preference.

## 7.2 — Fallbacks, in order

If S02 fails on anatomy twice, do not rewrite the scene. Step down the staging:

1. **As specified.** Arm's length, one hand each on separate points of the garment.
2. **Hoodie mid-air.** He has let go, she has not taken hold. No contact at all
   between two people and one object. Loses a little warmth, removes the failure
   mode completely.
3. **Sequential.** Two frames — him taking it off alone, her pulling it on alone.
   The handover becomes an edit rather than a frame. Weakest, and still supplies
   the fit contrast, which is the commercial job.

Do not step down to 2 or 3 before 1 has had three attempts.

## 7.3 — The other three risks, ranked

- **The count.** Four people plus a background woman at the window is five human
  figures in frame. If the model merges them or adds one, the entire five-becomes-four
  mechanic is compromised at the exact shot where it starts. Consider generating the
  night window empty and adding the attendant only if the count reads cleanly.
- **The vehicle.** One car at a far bowser is enough for the model to produce an
  American pickup. It is background and expendable — if it drifts twice, remove it
  from the prompt entirely and let the forecourt be empty.
- **Wet ground.** Wet asphalt tends to come back glossy and cinematic. It needs to
  read as a servo after rain, not as a car commercial.

## 7.4 — What "it holds" actually means

Define it before running, so the result can be read honestly.

**It holds** if the acceptance gate in §6 passes within four attempts, and the two
attempts either side of it are consistent with each other on face, build, wardrobe
and light. Consistency across attempts is the real signal — a single lucky frame
proves nothing about a ten-scene night.

**It does not hold** if identity drifts between attempts at the same prompt, or if
the anatomy fails at fallback stage 2, where there is no contact between people and
no excuse for it.

# 8. Running it

The repo's own orchestrator is `studio/app/services/generation_orchestrator.py`
(`start_attempt` → `run_attempt` → `_store_assets`). It requires `OPENAI_API_KEY`
and `OPENAI_IMAGE_MODEL` in the environment or a local `.env`; without them
`app/adapters/factory.py` falls back to the fake adapter and nothing billable runs.

Every attempt, including rejected ones, must be retained with provider, model,
settings, prompt and reference provenance per `README.md` §6 Level 5. The rejected
attempts from this test are more useful than the successful one, because they are
the evidence about whether the pipeline holds.

## What this shot has no answer for

There are no character reference images yet. `CHARACTER_CONTINUITY.md` §10 is
explicit: *"A campaign shot involving a character cannot become generation-ready
unless the required character identity and applicable appearance/wardrobe state
resolve to persisted records and required references exist."*

By that contract this shot is **not** generation-ready, and the first real job is
not this frame at all — it is the four identity references for `PI-02` through
`PI-05`. This specification is written so those references have something exact to
be built against.
