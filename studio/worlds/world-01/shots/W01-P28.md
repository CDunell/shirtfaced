# 1. Scene state

The back room of an Australian pub is packed shoulder-to-shoulder while a four-piece plays on a low stage. The room is hot, loud and already fully alive before the camera finds any of the recurring cast. Low ceiling, condensation on the windows, PA stack, cables, layered gig posters, crowded bar, red neon spill and the cold pool-table lamp all remain part of the established room.

Damo is **on top of the full-size green-felt pool table**, rocking out with the crowd. He has the pool cue horizontal above his head in both hands. He is singing the chorus at full volume. He is not flat-footed or posing: his weight is moving, knees loose, body caught mid-beat. A proper full-size wooden pub barstool stands on the table beside him with a pint on it.

Brock is elsewhere in the same crowd, singing enthusiastically and not quite knowing the song. Emma is integrated into the crowd near the bar, screaming the chorus with everyone else. Gary remains back from the chaos, watching. Casey may be present as continuity requires, but the scene does not organise itself around any one cast member.

**Critical crowd rule:** Damo is an event inside a larger event. The crowd is not his audience. Some nearby people may naturally glance at him, laugh with him, gesture toward him or briefly react to what he is doing. That is ordinary pub behaviour. Attention remains distributed across the room: other people watch the band, sing with mates, talk, drink, move through the crowd or look elsewhere. Damo must never become the dominant collective focal point or replace the band as the performance source. No organised audience, semicircle, cleared viewing space or majority crowd attention converges on him.

# 6. Motion direction

- Damo is **rocking out**, not posing on furniture. His feet do not remain planted symmetrically. Use shifting weight, bent knees, torso movement and imperfect balance consistent with somebody yelling a chorus in a packed pub.
- Keep the cue securely in both hands above him. Do not introduce dangerous swinging into nearby heads.
- The stool and pint remain stable on the table. They are continuity anchors, not slapstick props.
- Crowd energy is distributed. Independent micro-events and occlusions are desirable.
- Nobody notices the camera.
- Nearby people may naturally glance at, laugh with, gesture toward or briefly react to Damo; attention must remain distributed across the room.
- No dominant crowd convergence on Damo. No organised audience, semicircle, cleared viewing space or majority attention directed toward him.
- No hero halo, clean negative space or stage-like isolation around Damo.
- Preserve red bar-neon versus colder pool/stage practical-light contrast.
- Preserve the master image's density. Do not simplify the room to improve character identity.
