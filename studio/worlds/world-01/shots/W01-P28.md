# 1. Scene state

The back room of an Australian pub is packed shoulder-to-shoulder while a four-piece plays on a low stage. The room is hot, loud and already fully alive before the camera finds any recurring cast. Low ceiling, PA stack, cables, layered gig posters, crowded bar, red neon spill and the pool-table/stage practicals remain part of the established room.

Damo is **on top of the full-size green-felt pool table**, rocking out with the crowd. He has an ordinary billiards cue horizontal above his head in both hands and is roaring the chorus. He is moving, not posing: weight shifting, knees loose, body caught mid-beat. A proper full-size wooden pub barstool stands on the table beside him with a pint on it.

Brock is elsewhere in the same crowd, singing enthusiastically and not quite knowing the song. Emma is integrated into the crowd near the bar, screaming the chorus with everyone else. Gary remains back from the chaos, watching. Casey may be present as continuity requires, but the scene does not organise itself around any one cast member.

**Critical crowd rule:** Damo is an event inside a larger event. The crowd is not his audience. Attention remains distributed across the room: people watch the band, sing with mates, talk, drink, move through the crowd or look elsewhere. Damo must never become the dominant collective focal point or replace the band as the performance source.

# 6. Motion direction

- Damo is **rocking out**, not posing on furniture. Feet do not remain planted symmetrically. Use shifting weight, bent knees, torso movement and imperfect balance consistent with yelling a chorus in a packed pub.
- Keep the cue securely in both hands above him. Do not introduce dangerous swinging into nearby heads.
- The stool and pint remain stable on the table. They are continuity anchors, not slapstick props.
- Crowd energy is distributed. Independent micro-events and occlusions are desirable.
- Nobody notices the camera.
- No dominant crowd convergence on Damo. No organised audience, semicircle, cleared viewing space or majority attention directed toward him.
- No hero halo, clean negative space or stage-like isolation around Damo.
- Preserve red bar-neon versus colder stage/pool practical contrast.
- Preserve the master image's density. Do not simplify the room.
- Veo may animate what is present in the source still; it must not invent new recurring characters, props, signage or geography.
- Camera motion stays small and physically plausible: handheld sway, crowd bump, slight push/reframe, obstruction. No teleporting camera or manufactured edit inside a take.
