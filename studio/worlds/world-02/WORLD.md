# What this is

World 02 is other people's houses.

Backyards, side driveways, sheds, front lawns, the gap in the fence. Saturday
afternoon running into Saturday night. Trestle tables, an esky, borrowed chairs,
a speaker somebody brought, forty people who were never invited.

Where World 01 is the street at night, this is the yard in daylight and after.
`UNIVERSE_PREMISE.md` makes a world a persistent setting, not a campaign — this
one holds birthdays, engagements, Christmas, grand final day, someone's kid's
first, a house that's about to be sold, and a hundred Saturdays that aren't
anything in particular.

The first story inside it is Brock's thirtieth.

# What changes

## Light

**World 01 is one hundred per cent night.** Ten scenes, sodium and fluorescent and
festoon and one dawn. That is the single biggest limit on the visual range and
this world is where it opens up.

The backyard runs from hard afternoon sun to string lights. Sources:

- Flat high sun with hard shadows, 2–4pm
- Long low gold through a fence, 5–6pm
- The blue twenty minutes after it goes, when nobody has turned a light on yet
- String lights on a wire, warm and too few
- A shed fluoro with the door open
- A phone torch in the dark down the side of the house
- A kitchen window from the yard
- Direct flash after dark

**The blue twenty minutes is this world's signature.** World 01 owns wet asphalt
under sodium. World 02 owns the moment the yard goes blue and the lights aren't
on yet.

## Colour

Dry grass yellow. Concrete grey. Colorbond green. Terracotta. Esky white and
faded blue. Warm bulb amber after dark. Sunburn.

No HDR, no grading, same as World 01.

## Locations inside the world

- The backyard — patchy dead lawn, a concrete strip down the side, a shed with the
  door open, a trampoline nobody is using, a fence with a gap in it to next door
- The side driveway and the gate
- The kitchen, photographed from the yard through the back door
- The front lawn and the kerb
- The street the house is on

## The camera

Same observer rule. The useful difference: a backyard has a **fence**, a **back
door** and a **shed doorway**, so the observer's natural positions are the far
side of the yard, the kitchen window from outside, and the gap in the fence.

The kitchen is photographed from the yard. Never from inside it.

## Australiana

`POSITIONING.md` §2 permanently rules out hills hoists, water tanks, windmills and
corrugated iron **as depicted subject matter**. That is about artwork, not
photography, but this world is where the temptation returns, so the rule here is
simple and stricter than it needs to be: **nothing in that list is ever the reason
a frame exists, is ever centred, and is never the first thing the eye finds.**

An ordinary backyard is an ordinary backyard. It does not need dressing to be
Australian, and the moment it gets dressed it becomes a tea towel.

# Product

Rotation continues from the World 01 ledger rather than restarting.

The daylight half of this world is the first real opportunity in the universe for
**true-colour garment frames** — World 01 has exactly one, at 7:20am. A backyard
at 3pm shows what black actually looks like, which is the thing a customer
receiving a parcel is comparing against.

Caps and bucket hats are naturally load-bearing in afternoon sun and should not be
over-used for that reason. Hoodies and jumpers arrive as the temperature drops,
which gives this world a wardrobe clock as strong as World 01's fatigue clock.

Crops belong to the afternoon.

# The world's own test

World 01 asks whether someone would share the photograph without the product.

World 02 adds one, because a party is where staged photography goes to die:

> **Does this look like a party somebody is at, or a party somebody is
> photographing?**

Nobody holds a drink up. Nobody toasts to camera. No arms around shoulders in a
line. If a frame could be a hire company's website, it is dead.
