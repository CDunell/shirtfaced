# ONE — THE ARC

## The premise

**Twelve people were asked. One of them goes next door. By seven there are forty,
none of the chairs are ours, and the food is cooking in three backyards.**

## The human truth

**One bloke cooks and four blokes supervise.**

By twenty to five there are five men at that barbecue. One is holding tongs. The
other four have their arms folded and every one of them has a view, including the
chef, who is explaining heat to a twenty-year-old.

## The rule the whole day runs on

**Shit goes down, and it leads to wilder better shit nobody saw coming.**

Same engine as World 01. Every shortage produces something larger than the thing
that ran out.

| Goes wrong | Leads to |
|---|---|
| Four chairs and twelve people coming | A woman goes next door and comes back with the street |
| Nowhere near enough seats | By eleven there are more chairs than there are people |
| The gas runs out with twenty waiting | Food cooking on three barbecues in three backyards |
| Not enough string lights | Twenty minutes of a blue yard nobody turns a light on in |
| More food arrives than the table holds | The table gets rebuilt in four minutes by somebody who says nothing |

**Nobody at this party goes without anything for more than ten minutes**, and every
fix is bigger than the problem.

## The day

Brock is turning thirty and has organised it himself, which means he has booked
things and paid for almost none of them. It's a rented weatherboard two streets
back from the flats. The lawn has been dead since December. Twelve people were
asked.

At 2:40pm there's one trestle table, an esky, and four chairs from four different
houses. One of them is a dark timber pub stool with no back. A tray-back ute is
reversed up the side driveway with the tray down. **Nobody is here yet and the yard
is the loudest thing in the story.**

Ethan lights the barbecue at three because somebody has to and he's the one who
won't say no. Nineteen months into being Grace's boyfriend and still standing like
it's an interview. Grace hands him the tongs and goes and sits down.

At 4:15pm Sophie goes next door. Not to warn them — to ask them. She comes back
through the gap in the fence with four neighbours behind her and every one of them
carrying a chair. Twenty minutes later she's been two doors further down. Twelve
becomes forty across ninety minutes and it isn't an accident, it's one woman going
door to door, and nobody stops her because nobody thinks to.

At 4:40pm there are five men at the barbecue and one of them is cooking. Mitch is a
chef and is telling a twenty-year-old about heat with his arms folded. Nobody
leaves.

By five there are six people horizontal on the tray of the ute and nobody on it is
talking. Gary is down the side fence with a beer, talking to somebody's father.
Nobody says anything about that either.

At 6:30pm the gas runs out with twenty people still waiting and nobody has a spare
bottle. Sophie goes back through the gap. By ten past seven there's food going on
three barbecues in three backyards and two of them belong to people nobody here has
met. Ethan will not leave his. The string lights go on and there aren't enough of
them, so the yard goes blue for twenty minutes and nobody turns a light on in the
house.

At 7:45pm Charlotte finds the hire docket taped to the thing Brock hired, reads the
name on it, and puts it back down.

At 8:50pm more people keep arriving and there's more food than there was at four.
SK looks at the table for four seconds, then moves everything on it, without asking
and without telling anybody she's doing it. Twice the room, and nobody noticed there
was a problem.

At 10:30pm Gary walks over with two beers, hands one to Ethan and holds out his
other hand. Ethan gives him the tongs. He wouldn't give them to a chef. He sits down
for the first time since three o'clock.

By 11:40pm there are more chairs than people and not one of them came from this
house.

Sunday about nine, four of them are standing in the middle of a dead yard holding
chairs and nobody knows whose is whose.

## What changes from the first frame to the last

At 2:40pm there are four borrowed chairs and twelve people coming.

At 11:40pm there are more chairs than people and nobody knows whose any of them are.

**The party outgrew its own guest list and no individual person let it happen.**

## The registers this world runs on

`DENSITY.md` §1. Three settings, nothing in between, and **no frame in this world is
ever a pleasant afternoon.**

| Register | What it is | Scenes |
|---|---|---|
| **Anticipation** | Charged and empty. Everything ready, nobody here, and the yard knows what's coming | 1, 2 |
| **High energy** | Loud, crowded, moving, food and noise and forty people who weren't asked | 3, 4, 6, 7, 8 |
| **Horizontal** | Totally relaxed. Heat, nobody standing up, the afternoon has won | 5, 9, 10, 11 |

## The loud one

**4:15pm. The gap in the fence.**

She goes door to door and comes back with the street. It gets louder for ninety
minutes, it's legible in a second with the sound off, and it's the recruiter rather
than a phase transition. **Slot 1. Nothing else opens this cycle.**

The peak at 10:30pm stays silent on purpose. A world can have a quiet climax once it
has already earned the attention somewhere else.

## Where the joke and the tension live

The joke is the barbecue. Five men, one set of tongs, four opinions, and the chef is
one of the four. Never remarked on, and funnier every time the camera finds that
group and the number has gone up.

The tension is Ethan, and it's entirely one-sided. He's the only person here who
thinks he's being assessed. Everyone else stopped a year ago and nobody told him.

The release is Gary taking the tongs — the closest thing to a welcome anybody in
this family produces, and Ethan will completely fail to recognise it as one.

The engine is Sophie, who isn't tense about anything and never has been.

## The six phases

| Phase | Time | What |
|---|---|---|
| Setup | 2:40pm–3:04pm | An empty yard, four borrowed chairs, twelve people coming. Ethan lights it because nobody else will. |
| Commitment | 4:15pm | **The gap in the fence.** Four neighbours, four chairs, then two doors further down. |
| Escalation | 4:40pm–6:30pm | Five men at one barbecue. Then **the gas runs out** and by ten past seven the food is on three barbecues in three backyards. |
| Complication | 7:45pm–8:50pm | A name on a docket that isn't his. More food than table, and somebody fixes it without telling anyone. |
| Peak | 10:30pm | Gary holds out a hand and Ethan gives him the tongs. |
| Aftermath | 11:40pm and Sunday | More chairs than people. Then four people in a dead yard holding furniture nobody can identify. |

# FOUR — THE PLACE

A rented weatherboard two streets back from the walk-up in World 01. Same town, never
named. Built to `DENSITY.md` — nothing here has one of anything.

**THE YARD** — dead since December, straw-coloured, green only along the fence line
where the hose reaches and in one rectangle where a pool sat two summers ago. A
concrete strip down the side with a crack running its length and moss in the crack.
Two previous fence repairs in different timber, neither stained to match. A wall the
mower has hit forty times. A trampoline nobody uses with the mat gone grey. A rotary
line with three pegs on it and nothing else — **never centred, never found first,
never the reason a frame exists.**

**THE FENCE AND THE GAP** — palings, one panel down, and a gap two people wide that
existed before anybody in this story moved in. Through it: the next yard, and past
that a third. **The deep layer of half the frames in this world is somebody else's
backyard.**

**THE BARBECUE** — a four-burner on a trolley with a lid that has never been cleaned,
one wheel that doesn't turn, a gas bottle with the safety tag gone, and a bench beside
it made of an old door on two crates. Everything within arm's reach belongs to
somebody else.

**THE SHED** — door open all day, fluoro tube inside that goes on at six and is the
only cold light in the yard. Everything on the wall and nothing on a hook. Becomes the
bar because that's where the second esky ends up.

**THE SIDE DRIVEWAY** — concrete, oil-stained, a tray-back ute reversed up it with the
tray down. Bins at the gate. Where the smokers and the phone calls go, and there are
always two people down there.

**THE KITCHEN** — photographed from the yard through the back door, never from inside.
A window over the sink lit warm from six, and it is the warmest thing in every
blue-hour frame.

**THE STRING LIGHTS** — on a wire from the shed corner to the fence to the house,
sagging, twelve bulbs where the yard needs forty, four of them out. **Warm against
blue air, lighting about a third of what's happening.**

**Australiana discipline.** `world-02/WORLD.md` is stricter than it needs to be and
stays that way: nothing on the ruled-out list is ever the reason a frame exists, is
ever centred, or is the first thing the eye finds. An ordinary backyard is an ordinary
backyard. The moment it gets dressed it becomes a tea towel. **Density is what
accumulates when nobody tidies; dressing is what somebody put there this morning.**
