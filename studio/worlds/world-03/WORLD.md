# SHIRTFACED — WORLD 03

## THE FUNCTION ROOM

> **Upstairs, carpet you can hear, a bar with a roller shutter, and forty
> stackable chairs in rows nobody wants to sit in.**

**Status:** Draft world bible. Not approved.
**Date:** 14 August 2026.

# What this is

The room above the bowlo. The one at the RSL with the honour boards. The one you
book by ringing the club on a Tuesday.

Twenty-firsts, engagements, presentation nights, trivia, a christening, a
retirement do, a wake. Weekday afternoons that go longer than anybody planned,
because the bar is right there and nobody has to drive past it to get home.

World 01 is the street at night. World 02 is the yard in daylight. **This one is
indoors, under fluorescent, on a weekday** — and that third register is the point
of it.

The first story inside it is a wake for a bloke nobody in the room was related to.

# What changes

## Light — and this is the hard one

Overhead fluorescent troffers in a suspended ceiling. Flat, green-ish, from
directly above, with nowhere to hide.

It is the least flattering light in the universe and it is not a problem to be
solved. Every ordinary Australian function room looks exactly like this and any
attempt to warm it up turns the frame into a venue's marketing photo. **The
fluorescent stays.**

Sources:

- Ceiling troffers, on from two in the afternoon whether they are needed or not
- High windows down one side, blown out, no view
- The bar back-lit once the shutter is up
- A bain-marie
- An illuminated club sign in the stairwell
- Outside, later, sodium over a bowling green

**This world's signature is a room lit from directly above with daylight still
outside.** The contrast between the two — 4pm sun in the windows and the lights on
anyway — is the whole visual identity.

## Colour

Patterned carpet, red and gold and worn. Wood veneer. Beige walls. Honour boards
in dark varnish with gold leaf. Green baize. Stackable chairs in maroon vinyl.
The only black in the room is what people are wearing.

## The camera

The observer stands where you would stand: at the back near the doors, at the far
end of the bar, in the stairwell looking in, from the servery hatch, out on the
balcony looking through glass.

The room is large, so it is not an enclosure. The servery and the stairwell are
the two natural openings and they earn their place the way doorways did in
World 01.

## Locations inside the world

- The function room itself, and the bar along one wall with its roller shutter
- The servery and the kitchen behind it, shot from the room
- The stairwell and the landing
- The car park
- The bowling green, and the green at night

## Sound

This world has a sound identity the other two don't: a room with a low ceiling and
hard surfaces, forty people, and the specific noise a roller shutter makes going
up. Motion assets should be built with that in mind even though most viewing is
silent.

# Product

**This is the world where black stops being a brand decision and becomes a fact
about people.**

Most Australian men do not own a suit. What they own is one pair of dark jeans and
a plain black t-shirt, and that is what they wear to anything that matters. A
function room on a weekday afternoon is full of people wearing the most formal
thing they have, and the most formal thing a lot of them have is a blank black
tee.

That gives the range an emotional reason to be present that neither other world
can give it, and it has to be handled carefully, because the moment a frame is
*about* the garment it dies. The rule is unchanged: the image has to be worth
saving with nobody wearing anything of ours. The world simply supplies the most
honest reason black is everywhere it has ever had.

Caps come off at the door and go back on in the car park. Hoodies are tied at the
waist indoors because the room is warm and go on outside after eight. Jumpers over
shoulders. Crops are wrong in this room and should be absent rather than forced —
this is the one world where a garment sits out, and that is more useful than
pretending otherwise.

# What this world will not do

- Nobody is shown crying to camera. One person goes at the edge of a frame, once,
  and the frame is not about him.
- No coffin, no hearse, no graveside, no service. **The story starts in a car park
  afterwards.** Everything that would make this solemn happens off-page.
- The deceased is never named, never shown clearly, never described, and is not
  related to a single person in `CHARACTERS.md`. Nobody in the cast is bereaved
  and nobody's state changes because of him.
- Kids are present, in motion, mid-distance and background. They are never the
  subject of a frame and never named.
- Nothing here is sad for effect. `CHANNEL_TRANSLATION.md` §9 already governs it:
  optimistic, never misery or humiliation. An Australian wake is one of the better
  days of the year and that is what is being photographed.
