# THE ROOM WAS FULL

*World 03. One Thursday. Gary, Damo, SK, Brock, Charlotte, Mitch, Casey.*

*The readable cut. `STORY_ARC.md` is the production version.*

---

### 1:50pm — the car park

Thirty-odd people standing around in a bowls club car park in black, in the heat.

Nobody is going in first.

Charlotte is writing something on her phone with her back to a car.

Casey knows four of the older women by name and has stopped with all of them.

Gary is at the edge with his hands in his pockets.

Damo has already walked to the end of the car park and back twice. There is
nothing for him to do out here and it's killing him.

---

### 2:15pm — the honour board

Upstairs. The lights are on and it's the middle of the afternoon.

Forty stackable chairs in rows and about six people sitting in them.

Everybody else is standing up reading the walls, because there's nothing else to
do yet.

Life members. Past presidents. Forty names in gold going back to 1961.

One of them has KEVIN in the middle of it.

Nobody says anything about it.

---

### 2:40pm — the shutter

Brock puts the roller shutter up on the bar.

He doesn't work here.

Forty people in black turn around at once.

That's it. That's the moment. Everything after this is a different afternoon to
everything before it.

Somebody behind the bar comes out, sees what's happened, and starts pouring.

---

### 3:20pm — the microphone

Charlotte gets up because somebody asked her on Tuesday and she's a solicitor and
they thought she'd be good with words.

She read it off her phone. She wrote it in the car park.

It's four sentences.

It's the best thing anyone says all day.

When she stops, nobody claps, because nobody knows if you're supposed to.

Then somebody at the back does, and then everybody does.

---

### 4:15pm — the chairs

The rows are gone. Damo started it and then everybody was doing it, and now every
chair in the room is against a wall.

Three kids are doing laps of the room in their good clothes, sliding on the
carpet.

Nobody stops them.

The club put out four platters for forty people.

Mitch goes into the servery. Nobody asked him and nobody stops him.

By five there are nine platters and none of them came from the club.

---

### 5:30pm — the meat raffle

The club runs a meat tray on Thursdays and nobody cancelled it.

So at half past five a bloke comes in with the barrel and the book and does it,
in the same room, at a wake, completely straight.

Brock wins it.

He gives it to one of the older women Casey was talking to in the car park.

She tries to give it back for about a minute and a half.

---

### 6:10pm — booked at seven

A bloke from downstairs comes up and says the room's booked from seven.

He says it twice.

Nobody moves.

He goes back downstairs.

---

### 7:00pm — the twenty-first

The doors go and it's a twenty-first.

Balloons. A cake in a box. Thirty people dressed the exact opposite of everybody
already in here.

They stop in the doorway.

Forty people in black turn around and look at them.

Nobody leaves.

Damo is across the room and shaking hands before anyone has decided anything.

By ten past, the balloons are up.

By half past, there's a cake on the same trestle the sandwiches were on.

Nobody has explained anything to anybody and nobody is going to.

---

### 8:00pm — the servery

Mitch is still in there.

He's making toasted sandwiches for about thirty people now, on a press he found,
at eight o'clock at night.

Two months ago at his mate's thirtieth he stood at somebody else's barbecue with
his arms folded for six hours and never touched it.

Nobody mentions that either.

---

### 8:30pm — the photo

Somebody's auntie decides there should be a photo.

It takes eleven minutes, because people keep leaving the frame to get somebody
else who should be in it.

By the time she takes it there are about fifteen people in it who walked through
the door at seven.

They don't know whose wake this is.

They're in the photo.

---

### 9:20pm — the green

Downstairs, out the back, in the dark.

Thirty of them on the bowling green, which you are not supposed to walk on.

Shoes off. The grass is wet.

Damo is explaining bowls to four people who turned twenty-one today. He has never
played bowls.

SK is on the edge of the rink giving one of them serious career advice at half
past nine on a Thursday.

Gary is further along with a beer, talking to somebody's father.

Casey has her jumper on and somebody else's over her knees.

Upstairs, the meat tray is on the birthday table.

Not one person out here was family.

The room was full anyway.
