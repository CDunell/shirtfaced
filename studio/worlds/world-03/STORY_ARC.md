# ONE — THE ARC

## The premise

**The room's booked from seven and nobody leaves, so a wake and a twenty-first
become the same party.**

## The human truth

**The room was full and not one person in it was family.**

An old bloke from the club died. He was always there. Nobody could tell you what he
did for work or whether he had kids. Forty people took a Thursday off, the room was
full anyway, and by six o'clock it was one of the better afternoons any of them had
had that year.

**The disproportion is that this is the largest event in the universe so far and
nobody in it has any standing to feel it.**

## The rule the whole day runs on

**Shit goes down, and it leads to wilder better shit nobody saw coming.**

| Goes wrong | Leads to |
|---|---|
| Forty people who can't work out how to start | Somebody opens a bar he doesn't work at |
| Nobody knows whether you're supposed to clap | One bloke at the back does, and then everybody does |
| Four platters for forty people | A stranger's kitchen, nine platters, and seventy fed by eight |
| The room is booked from seven | Two events become one and nobody explains anything to anybody |
| Nowhere left to be at half nine | Thirty people on a bowling green in the dark with their shoes off |

**Nothing goes short here either.** The club under-caters and a chef takes the
kitchen. The room is double-booked and the room simply holds both.

## The day

The funeral is over before the story starts. Nothing about the service is
photographed, described or referred to. It begins in a car park at ten to two with
thirty people in black not going inside.

At 2:15pm they're upstairs reading the walls, because the lights are on, it's the
middle of the afternoon and there's nothing else to do yet. Life members, past
presidents, forty names in gold going back to 1961, and one of them has KEVIN in the
middle of it.

At 2:40pm Brock puts the roller shutter up on the bar. He doesn't work there. Forty
people in black turn around at once and the afternoon becomes a different afternoon.

At 3:20pm Charlotte says four sentences off her phone, which she wrote in the car
park, because somebody rang her on Tuesday and thought a solicitor would be good with
words. When she stops nobody claps, because nobody knows if you're supposed to. Then
one person at the back does.

By 4:15pm the chairs are all against the walls and nobody moved them. The club put out
four platters for forty people, so Mitch goes into the servery — nobody asked him and
nobody stops him — and by five there are nine platters and none of them came from the
club.

At 5:30pm the club's Thursday meat raffle goes ahead, in that room, at that wake,
completely straight, because nobody cancelled it. Brock wins it. He gives it away
inside ten seconds and spends the next ninety refusing to take it back.

At 6:10pm a bloke from downstairs comes up and says the room is booked from seven. He
says it twice. Nobody moves. He goes back downstairs.

At seven the doors go and it's the twenty-first. Balloons, a cake in a box and thirty
people dressed the exact opposite of everybody already in the room. They stop in the
doorway. Forty people in black turn around and look at them.

Nobody leaves. Damo is across the room shaking hands before anyone has decided
anything. By ten past, the balloons are up. By half past, there's a cake on the same
trestle the sandwiches were on. **Two events merge in one room and nothing is
explained to anybody.**

At eight Mitch is still in the servery, now feeding thirty more. At half past,
somebody's auntie finally gets her photograph, and about fifteen people in it walked
through the door at seven and have no idea whose wake this is.

At 9:20pm thirty of them are on the bowling green in the dark with their shoes off.
Damo is explaining bowls to four people who turned twenty-one today. He has never
played bowls. Upstairs the meat tray is on the birthday table.

## What changes from the first frame to the last

At 1:50pm nobody will go in the door.

At 9:20pm there are twice as many people and half of them have never met anybody.

## The two loud ones

**2:40pm — the shutter.** A roller shutter goes up and a room full of people in black
turns around at once. One movement, one sound, forty heads, and the day pivots.
Legible in under a second with the sound off, and it's Brock doing something he has no
authority to do for the third world running without anybody commenting on it. **Slot
1. Nothing else opens this cycle.**

**7:00pm — the twenty-first arrives.** Thirty people in party clothes stop in a
doorway in front of forty people in black, and instead of anybody leaving, the two
events simply become one. Loud, absurd, completely true of both function rooms and
Australians, and not one line anywhere acknowledges what has happened. **This is the
peak and it is not quiet.**

## Where the joke and the tension live

The joke is the meat raffle. It goes ahead. Nobody stops it, nobody apologises for it,
and it's played completely straight by everyone including the bloke with the barrel. A
wake with a meat tray in the middle of it is the most Australian sentence in this
universe and it doesn't get a single line of comment.

The tension is the first fifty minutes — a car park, a room with the lights on at two
in the afternoon, and forty people who have known each other for twenty years and
cannot work out how to start.

The tenderness is a room that doesn't throw anybody out, and it's never named.

## Story quality gates — `STORY_ENGINE.md` §5

- **World fit** — Pass. No logo explains a meat raffle at a wake.
- **Story change** — Pass. Nobody will go in the door; six hours later nobody will go home.
- **Character motive** — Pass. Brock opens the bar because somebody had to, Charlotte speaks because she was asked, Mitch cooks because he isn't going to let the food be bad, nobody leaves at seven because leaving would end it.
- **Garment naturalism** — Pass, and it's the strongest garment logic in the universe: everyone is wearing the most formal thing they own and for a lot of them that's a blank black tee.
- **Australian grounding** — Pass. Bowls club, honour boards, meat tray, stackable chairs, the green you're not allowed to walk on.
- **Originality** — Pass. No named director, no reproduced work, and the deceased is unnamed and unrelated to anybody.
- **Social extractability** — Pass. The shutter, the raffle and the doorway at seven are three genuinely different posts and any one recruits alone. Two of the three are loud.
- **AI producibility** — Hardest in the universe. A full room under fluorescent is World 02's crowd problem plus a new lighting problem. See §10.
- **Ending value** — Pass. Two events, thirty people on a green, and a meat tray on a birthday table. The audience arrives at "nobody here was family" without being told.
- **Register** — Pass. Eleven declared, none of them middle of the road.

**Every scene owes one frame under two metres.** Lens by size: WS 24–35mm · MLS 35mm ·

**The function room trap.** Under flat overhead light a wide of a full room is
featureless — no modelling, no separation, forty identical black shapes. **The room is
only legible close.** One face at 85mm with sixty out-of-focus people stacked behind it
carries the count better than a wide that contains all of them.

**Both wides are spent on purpose:** the doorway at seven, and the green at half nine.
Nowhere else.

# FOUR — THE PLACE

The room above the bowlo, ten minutes' walk from the corner pub. Same town, never named.
Built to `DENSITY.md`.

**THE CAR PARK** — cracked asphalt with the line marking worn to ghosts, a kerb painted
yellow twenty years ago, a shade tree that covers four bays out of forty. A club sign on
a pole with a fluorescent tube behind perspex gone yellow. Wheelie bins in a cage.
**Thirty-degree heat, no shade, everybody in black.**

**THE STAIRWELL** — vinyl treads with the nosing worn through to the ply on the traffic
side only, a handrail rubbed pale where hands go, an illuminated club sign on the
landing that is the only light source between two doors, and a fire door held open with
a wedge that has been there for years.

**THE FUNCTION ROOM** — patterned carpet, red and gold, worn to a pale track where
everybody walks and nowhere else. Suspended ceiling, **fluorescent troffers on from two
in the afternoon whether they're needed or not**, two tubes flickering at different
rates. High windows down one side, blown out, no view, and the whole visual identity of
this world is that **the lights are on at four o'clock with daylight in the glass.**

Beige walls with a blu-tack constellation and four rectangles of tape residue. Honour
boards in dark varnish, gold leaf gone off two thirds of one of them, forty names going
back to 1961 — **one has KEVIN in the middle of it, never centred, never in focus,
never read aloud.** Scuff arcs on the wall where the chairs stack. Forty maroon vinyl
stackable chairs, then none of them where they started.

**THE BAR** — along one wall, roller shutter down, **and when it goes up the whole
back-lit warm interior of it fights the ceiling fluoro for the rest of the day.**
Beer mats, a Thursday raffle sign, a laminated notice curling at one corner.

**THE SERVERY** — a hatch to a stainless kitchen, shot from the room, never entered by
the camera. Bain-marie glow, a sandwich press that was found rather than provided.

**THE BOWLING GREEN** — flat, dark, dew coming down, one sodium light on a pole at the
far corner doing about a quarter of the job, and the room's own lit windows behind and
above it. **The green is the only place in this world with two light sources more than
twenty metres apart**, and it's why the ending looks different from everything before
it.
