**Status:** Open register. Not canon, not a contract.
**Scope:** State that persists between worlds, and decisions not yet made.
**Created:** 14 August 2026.

---

## 0. What this is for

`CHARACTER_CONTINUITY.md` covers who a character is and what they look like across
campaigns. `CONTINUITY.md` covers what has been shot inside World 01. Neither has a
place for *what state a world left the crowd in* — and the universe is a series of
worlds with largely the same crowd, so that state is real and currently homeless.

This document is that home. It also holds decisions that are open, so they get made
deliberately rather than by whoever writes next.

Nothing here decides a creative question. Where a decision would constrain the work,
it is recorded as open and left open.

---

## 1. Carry-forward record

One entry per world, written **after** that world's story is approved, never before.

Per world:

- **Cast state on exit** — where each character is left. Not a summary of what they
  did; the state the next world inherits.
- **Relationships as left** — what moved, what deliberately didn't.
- **Planted devices** — running bits, objects, phrases, absences intended to recur.
  Marked as planted-deliberately, so a later world knows it is picking something up
  rather than inventing it.
- **Locations now canon** — places established well enough to return to.
- **Held back** — what was available and deliberately not spent, with the reason.
- **Spent** — what this world used up and cannot be used again the same way.

## 2. Open decisions

Recorded so they are not lost. Not resolved here.

### 2.1 — Does The Big Night collide with We Lost Gary?

**The problem.** `docs/launch-research/06-we-lost-gary-campaign-plan.md` describes a
campaign that is not a separable device. It is a Friday night, running from 4:03pm
through 8:52am Saturday, following one group across knock-off, deep night, a food stop,
a servo and the morning after. That is the same night, the same window and the same
shape as World 01 — The Big Night.

Its mechanic is that five mates become four with no explanation, and the audience
notices the absence before the brand mentions it.

**Why it matters.** The absence only lands once. If The Big Night is written as an
ordinary complete night, it either pre-empts the Gary mechanic — the audience has
already seen this crowd do this night — or it has to be carefully written not to,
which is a constraint the writer needs stated rather than inferred.

**The options, unresolved:**

- **The Big Night *is* the Gary campaign.** The disappearance is the complication and
  peak phase. One night, one arc, the mechanic built in.
- **The Big Night is a different night, and protects Gary.** The Gary campaign stays a
  later world. The Big Night must then avoid consuming its shape: no unexplained
  absence, no five-becomes-four, and Gary present throughout or absent for a stated
  ordinary reason.
- **The Big Night plants, Gary pays off.** Something small and unexplained here that
  the later campaign collects. Cheapest option, but only works if what is planted is
  genuinely inert on its own.

**Owner decision — made, 14 August 2026.** It is all one night. The Big Night is the
night, and the Gary disappearance happens inside it rather than in a later world. The
first option above is selected; the other two are closed.

Consequence for the writer: five-becomes-four is available and should be built into the
complication and peak phases rather than protected for later. The absence still only
lands once, so it is spent here deliberately and well, not scattered.

### 2.1a — The town is unnamed

**Decided by the owner, 14 August 2026.** Everything happens in one town, and the town
is deliberately not identified. It is *your* town, wherever that is.

This is a rule, not an absence of one. No named city, no identifiable skyline, no
landmark, no recognisable bridge, stadium, tower or harbour, no state-specific signage
or number plates legible enough to place it. The viewer should be able to believe it is
theirs.

It follows directly from the creative brain's line that ordinary Australian reality
beats Australian iconography, and it is stronger than that rule rather than a
restatement of it: the pub, the servo, the takeaway and the side street have to read as
generically, unmistakably Australian while placing nowhere in particular.

Everything is walkable or near enough. One night, one town, one crowd.

**Unnamed is not the same as unspecific.** The town has no name and a very definite
identity. It is the same pub every time, the same servo on the same corner, the same
takeaway with the same broken sign, the same stretch of side street between them. A
returning viewer recognises the place without ever being told what it is called. That
consistency is what makes it a world rather than a backdrop, and it is recorded per
world in §1 under locations now canon.

So the writer's job is not to keep the town vague. It is to build somewhere real and
withhold the label.

The town has no name at all — not a real one, not an invented one. Nobody says where
they are, because nobody in a real night out ever does.

## 3. Rule for adding to this document

Add an entry when something would otherwise be lost: a state the next world needs, a
decision deferred, a device planted. Do not add creative preferences, and do not use
an open decision here to constrain a draft — an open decision is a flag, not a rule.
