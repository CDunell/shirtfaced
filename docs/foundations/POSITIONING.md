# SHIRTFACED — POSITIONING

**Status:** Canon.
**Date:** 8 August 2026.
**Decided by:** the owner, in session, after twelve illustrations were commissioned
against the wrong register.

This document exists because the register was never written down, and something had
to fill the gap. What filled it was a guess, and the guess was wrong.

---

## 0. Scope & authority

Canon for what Shirtfaced *is* in the market, and therefore for what its artwork may
depict. It governs the sourcing briefs in `studio/docs/`, and it constrains System B
subject matter. It does not govern voice — see `BRAND_VOICE.md` — and it does not
govern the universe, the worlds or the photography, which are System A.

Like `BRAND_VOICE.md`, this is not gated by `PLANNING_CANON_HEADINGS`. It is
enforced by review.

---

## 1. The position

**Shirtfaced is high-end streetwear.**

Australian, humour-led, premium. In that order of adjectives but not that order of
importance: the garment has to be desirable before the joke lands. That is already
the first principle of the product design constitution — *a SHIRTFACED garment must
succeed as a desirable product before brand context, humour, story or marketing is
applied* — and this document is the register that principle implies.

Nothing else about market tier, price point, distribution or competitive set has
been decided. Do not infer any of it from this document.

---

## 2. Where the Australian part lives

**In the words and the characters. Not in depicted subject matter.**

This is the distinction the twelve illustrations got wrong. Shirtfaced being
Australian does not mean Shirtfaced draws Australia. It means the language is
Australian, the humour is Australian, and the people in the universe are
Australian.

Out, permanently:

- native wildlife — magpie, kookaburra, cockatoo, galah, kangaroo, wombat, goanna,
  blue heeler, redback, cane toad;
- native flora — gum leaf, wattle, banksia, bottlebrush;
- national food — meat pie, lamington, Chiko roll, snag on bread;
- domestic infrastructure — hills hoist, water tank, windmill, corrugated iron.

Not because Australia is off-limits. Because depicting it literally is the souvenir
register, and a souvenir shirt is bought once, by a visitor, as a joke about the
country rather than a joke from inside it.

---

## 3. The evidence

We hold 12,151 product images from 188 apparel brands in
`studio/var/design_corpus/`, tagged by tradition — thirty streetwear, twenty skate,
ten Australian streetwear, three Australian humour.

**No brand in that corpus depicts its own country's wildlife or food.** Not the
Australian streetwear brands. Not the Australian humour brands, which run on slogans
and licensed characters instead.

What they do run on, from 1,166 mined designs and roughly 180 products examined
across 36 brands:

- 577 of 1,166 designs are a single element; 899 are one or two;
- the median design carries four words, at every element count;
- the dominant object is a wordmark, and the craft is in the lettering;
- the volume product is a plain blank with a small mark at left chest;
- illustration, where it appears, is a brand-owned character, tattoo or occult
  flash, an institutional crest, or a halftone panel.

The full derivation is in `studio/docs/ASSET_SPECIFICATION.md`.

## 4. What this does not settle

Recorded so nobody mistakes silence for a decision:

- price point, margin and positioning against named competitors;
- distribution — direct, wholesale, or both;
- the range architecture beyond what the constitution already says about
  collection roles;
- whether drinkware follows the same register as apparel.

`CHARACTERS.md` is still a name list. Section 2 puts real weight on characters
carrying the Australian identity, and nothing is formed yet to carry it.
