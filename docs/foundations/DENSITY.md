# SHIRTFACED — DENSITY AND REGISTER

**Status:** Production doctrine. Applies to every world, every scene, every frame.
**Date:** 15 August 2026.

---

# The failure this exists to kill

Two failures, and they compound.

**The places were written as one of each thing.** One flickering light. One stack
of milk crates. One roller door. One stencil on one wall. A side street with five
people on it and nobody else. That is a film set at six in the morning before
anyone has arrived — tidy, legible, empty, and **nothing could possibly happen
there.**

**The frames were written in the middle of the road.** Warm, pleasant, a nice
evening, people having a good time. Every scene sat at the same mild temperature
and no frame was ever at either end of anything.

The characters were written right and the garments were written right and then
they were put in a tidy place at room temperature.

The site already knows what this looks like. Laneways painted over four times.
Neon on one side and cold white on the other. Wet ground doubling all of it.
Posters over posters over torn posters. Other people's nights going on behind the
shoulder of the person in frame.

**The world has to be worth being in, and every frame has to be at one end of
something.**

---

# One — no frame is ever middle of the road

Every frame sits in exactly one register, and the register is at an extreme.
There is no neutral setting in this universe and there is no such thing as a
pleasant scene.

| World | The registers available | Nothing else |
|---|---|---|
| **01 — night** | **About to go off** (charged, waiting, nobody has decided anything) · **Gone off** (loud, hot, unrecoverable) · **Wrecked and happy** (three in the morning, done, still going) | Never "a nice night out" |
| **02 — the yard** | **Anticipation** (charged and empty, everything ready, nobody here yet) · **High energy** (loud, crowded, moving, food and noise) · **Horizontal** (totally relaxed, heat, nobody standing up, the afternoon has won) | Never "a pleasant afternoon" |
| **03 — the room** | **Can't start** (forty people who cannot work out how to begin) · **Gone off** (two events, one room, nobody leaving) · **Shoes off** (the end of it, out the back, in the dark) | Never "a nice do" |

**The three-word block in every scene prompt is the register declaration.** World
01's *Warm / Loud / Easy* and *Wet / Absurd / Committed* are doing this job
already. Every scene in every world owes one and it has to name an extreme.

**The banned words, in any prompt, any block, any note:** pleasant, nice, lovely,
enjoyable, a good time, people enjoying themselves, relaxed atmosphere, festive.
If the honest one-word summary of a frame is *nice*, the frame is dead and no
amount of texture saves it.

**Anticipation counts as an extreme.** An empty yard at two in the afternoon with
one trestle table, an esky, forty chairs that aren't there yet and nobody in it is
not a neutral frame — it is a loaded one, and it is one of the strongest images in
World 02. The test is charge, not volume.

**Relaxed counts as an extreme, at the far end.** Six people horizontal on a ute
tray in the heat with nobody talking is not middle of the road. Middle of the road
is four people standing up holding drinks at a comfortable distance from each
other having a fine time.

# Two — every frame is four layers deep

A frame with a subject and a background is flat and reads as staged, because real
photographs of real nights are taken from inside the night, with things in the
way.

| Layer | What is in it | Non-negotiable |
|---|---|---|
| **Crop** | Something between lens and subject — a shoulder, a post, a wing mirror, a bin, a rail, a doorway edge, rain, a fence | Most frames |
| **Subject** | Our people, 1.5–4m, garment legible | Every frame |
| **Middle** | Other people's day or night. Unrelated, in motion, out of focus, not looking | Every frame except where emptiness *is* the event |
| **Deep** | Light or space going away — a run of lamps, a lit doorway a hundred metres down, neon round a corner, three more backyards, the length of a room | Every exterior and every large interior |

An empty frame is permitted **twice per world** and it has to be the point: the
locked wide at the side door before five people cross it, the shut pub at twenty
past seven, the yard before anybody arrives. Everywhere else is populated.

# Three — every surface carries history

Nothing in this universe is new, clean, or the first one.

**Street.** Paste-up over paste-up over torn paste-up, corners lifting · sticker
archaeology up a pole, six deep, all faded · repaint over graffiti over repaint,
three greys that don't match · tape residue in rectangles where something used to
be · rust runs under every bracket · cable and conduit and downpipe on every wall,
none of it planned · gaffer tape on a step edge, half gone.

**Yard.** Two previous fence repairs in different timber · a paver path that has
moved · a dead patch where a pool was · a wall the mower has hit forty times · a
shed with everything on the wall and nothing on a hook · plastic gone chalky in
the sun · a barbecue lid that has never been cleaned.

**Room.** Carpet worn in the traffic line and nowhere else · blu-tack constellation
on the beige · scuff arcs where chairs stack · gold leaf gone off two thirds of an
honour board · a laminate corner lifting · a fire door held with a wedge that has
been there for years.

**Everything in this layer is illegible.** Torn, oblique, out of focus, cropped,
overpainted. No real-world mark resolves anywhere in it. The Global Production
Rule is unchanged — nothing we sell carries a mark, and no unsold brand is ever
readable, centred or found first.

**Density is not dressing.** Ask of any object: *who put that there and when?* If
the answer is "somebody, years ago, and nobody has taken it down", it stays. If
the answer is "us, this morning, because the frame needed something", cut it.
Dressing is what turns a backyard into a tea towel.

# Four — light is contaminated

**Minimum two colour temperatures in every frame, and at least one practical light
source physically in the frame** — not implied from off-camera.

| World | The contamination |
|---|---|
| 01 — night street | Sodium orange against fluoro green-white. Red neon out of a doorway on one side, cold shopfront on the other. Festoons warm against street light cold |
| 02 — the yard | Hard sun against hot shade at three. Then the blue twenty minutes with a kitchen window warm through the back door and a shed fluoro cold down the side. String lights warm against blue air. Direct flash after dark |
| 03 — the room | Ceiling fluorescent green-white against blown-out daylight in the high windows — **the whole visual identity is that the lights are on at four in the afternoon.** Bar back-light warm once the shutter is up. Later, sodium over a green with the room's windows lit behind |

Flat single-source light is the sitcom. Two sources fighting is the picture.

---

# Five — wet whenever it can be

Wet ground doubles every light source in frame and halves the emptiness. World 01
is wet from 11:32pm by story; before that, a gutter running from somewhere else, a
hosed footpath outside a shopfront, condensation, a puddle nobody has driven
through. World 02 has a hose, an esky melting through, a wet concrete strip down
the side. World 03 has a bowling green with dew on it at half past nine.

Never a mirror. Broken, moving, partial.

---

# Six — other people are having their own day

In every populated frame there are people who have nothing to do with us and are
not aware of us. In motion, mid-conversation, cropped, backs, out of focus.
**Never extras standing still.**

Two or three minimum in any street or room frame. They are the difference between
a night and a rehearsal. They never look at the lens, and neither do we.

---

# Seven — the shot consequence

Density only works **compressed behind a close subject.**

A wide shot of a textured street is busy and unreadable and the garment is forty
pixels. The same street at 85mm from two metres — subject filling the frame, forty
metres of neon, poster, cable and other people's night stacked and soft behind
their shoulder — is the picture.

**Long lens, close, layers falling away.** `world-01/STORY_ARC.md` §3 holds the
size distribution; this says what the background is doing while that happens.

---

# Eight — the test

Three questions on every frame, on top of the Success Test and the World 02 party
test:

> **Which end is this frame at?**

Name the register in three words. If the honest answer is *nice*, *pleasant* or
*fine*, the frame is dead and no amount of texture saves it.

> **Could anything happen here?**

If the location is tidy, legible, empty and has one of each thing, the answer is
no.

> **Who else is in this photograph, and what are they doing?**

If the honest answer is "nobody" or "standing there", the frame is not finished.
