## 0. Scope & authority

This is the canonical authority for **who the recurring cast are, how they relate, and what is true about them as characters**. It sits alongside `UNIVERSE_PREMISE.md` — characters are rung 3 of the hierarchy, above products and below the worlds they appear in.

It does **not** attempt to duplicate every visual-reference detail or every scene wardrobe state. Production identity is deliberately split across three authorities:

1. **Narrative character canon — this document.** Who the person is, relationships, established age/role/background/personality facts, and facts explicitly settled by the owner.
2. **Canonical visual identity — `CAST_REFERENCE_USE.md` + the approved files under `studio/var/cast/<slug>/`.** The actual approved face/body reference is the visual authority. A generation prompt does not get to redraw or embellish that person from prose.
3. **Scene appearance state — `CHARACTER_CONTINUITY.md` and the resolved scene/shot appearance.** Wardrobe, headwear, wetness, damage, layers, carried objects and deliberate appearance changes belong here.

When these layers disagree, do not improvise a reconciliation. Resolve the conflict against owner-approved canon/reference state before generation.

### 0.1 Canon supremacy

For an established recurring character, the production hierarchy is:

**owner-set story canon > approved canonical visual reference > approved scene appearance/wardrobe > shot instruction > generator interpretation.**

The generator has no authority to create a new distinguishing feature merely because a prompt omitted it.

Examples of unauthorised identity drift include invented tattoos, scars, piercings, jewellery, facial-hair changes, hairstyle/colour changes, glasses, body-shape changes or distinctive accessories where none exist in approved canon/reference/scene state.

Natural scene effects such as sweat, flushed skin, wet hair, rumpled clothing, dirt or fatigue are allowed only when the story/scene makes them plausible and they do not rewrite identity.

### 0.2 Character names in production prompts

The old rule that recurring characters must never be named in provider-facing generation prompts is **retired**.

Use canonical names/slugs freely throughout planning, orchestration, production prompts and review when they make the instruction clearer. For example, `Damo stands on the pool table` is preferable to an ambiguous `one bloke stands on the pool table` when the attached reference manifest already resolves Damo's identity.

Names are labels for role resolution and continuity. They are **not** a substitute for visual references. The model should not be expected to know what `Damo` looks like from the name alone.

Production must still:

- persist exactly which canonical character a scene/shot contains;
- resolve the correct approved identity reference/version;
- resolve the current appearance/wardrobe state;
- attach or otherwise bind the relevant reference assets where the provider supports them; and
- review the returned asset against that same identity.

Provider-facing prose may use whichever wording is clearest for the selected model and reference mode: `Damo`, `Damo from reference 1`, `the man from reference 1`, etc. There is no stylistic prize for anonymising a character we already know.

The retired anonymous-name rule must not be reintroduced through old World 01 prompt templates, handover notes or archived generation doctrine.

### 0.3 Canonical visual identity is the file, not a re-description

A prompt paragraph is not a substitute for a character reference.

If a canonical reference exists, scene generation must use it where the provider/mode supports references and the character is important enough for identity continuity. The prompt should not freely reinterpret the reference into a "similar" person.

A new reference version becomes canonical only by deliberate approval. Failed or superseded generations do not silently rewrite the character.

### 0.4 Background cast

Anonymous background people are not constrained by this document unless a recurring background figure is deliberately promoted into canon.

They may be invented to make environments credible, but must not be mistaken for or visually overwrite established recurring characters.

## 1. The shape

Not a flat friend group. A family, with a friend web attached to it.

**Gary** is the centre — brother to Damo, father to Grace and Emma — and the rest of the cast connects through one of those three, or through Emma and Grace's own school and work circles. This is the strongest possible fit for the brand's own doctrine: nobody gets shirtfaced alone, and here the group isn't assembled for the photo, it's just who's actually related to who.

## 2. The family

**Gary** — real name Chris; goes by Gary. Smart, quiet, sarcastic as fuck, irreverent, an encyclopaedic knowledge of useless shit. The dry centre everyone else orbits. Brother to Damo. Father to Emma and Grace.

**Damo** — Gary's younger brother. SK's other half. The chaos agent — something ill-advised happens to or because of Damo every single time. Exhausting, beloved, always the first name in the retelling.

A mathematical genius. Ran his own finance company and consulted for some of the largest companies in Australia. The chaos is not stupidity — it is a very clever man who has stopped performing seriousness. Separated about five years ago after eighteen years with the same woman — together from around fifteen, so she is most of his life to date. The divorce was messy and ugly, and it is where his view comes from: he came out of it having lost years and decided not to lose any more. "Living his best life" is not a personality, it is a position he arrived at the hard way. No children appear in any world. *(Owner, 15 August 2026.)*

**Damo and SK together** — the power couple who are suburban Aussies at heart. They swear a lot and laugh more. Neither is impressed by status, including their own. *(Owner, 15 August 2026.)*

**SK** — Damo's partner. Former New York/London L'Oréal marketing executive. Goes by initials, not a full first name. Sharp, low patience for excuses, has run campaigns bigger than this one — which either makes her the best-qualified person in the group to say an idea is bad, or the least impressed by any of it.

Born and raised in Brisbane to high school teacher parents. Local girl made good — the international career is something she went and did, not something she came from, and she wears it lightly. *(Owner, 15 August 2026.)*

**Grace** — 18+, Gary's daughter, Emma's half-sister. Strawberry blonde, from her mother's side — she has her father's brow and her mother's colouring. *(Owner, 15 August 2026.)* Computer science and engineering student. The driest humour in the entire cast. Ethan's girlfriend.

**Ethan** — uni student, IT. Grace's boyfriend. Still slightly on his best behaviour around the rest of the family, which everyone finds hilarious and periodically tests on purpose.

**Emma** — Gary's eldest, nursing graduate, wild child. Grace's half-sister. Casey's best mate.

Not a character. In the owner's real family, every person and dog carries Kevin as a secret middle name. That's too good to spend on one background character with a pub-trivia backstory — it plays better as a running device across the universe: a name that keeps turning up on paperwork, tags, forms, never explained. ## 7. Character generation/review invariant

For any shot containing an established recurring character, the production package must be able to answer:

- which canonical character is present
- which approved identity reference/version is authoritative
- what appearance/wardrobe state applies in this scene
- what distinguishing features are allowed
- what distinguishing features are forbidden
- which scene action and blocking are locked
- what returned identity drift would cause automatic rejection

A generated image that contains the wrong person, invents an identity-changing feature, or contradicts locked appearance state is a **failed generation**, even if the environment/composition is otherwise strong.

Do not present that output as a candidate requiring the owner to rediscover the continuity error.
