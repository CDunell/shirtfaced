# SHIRTFACED — CHARACTERS

**Status:** Canon.
**Promoted from:** Hot List item 3 (`docs/shirtfaced-audit.md`, Area 1.1) — "there are no
characters, the biggest hole in the repo."

## 0. Scope & authority

`UNIVERSE_PREMISE.md`'s hierarchy puts characters above any single world: *"recurring
ordinary Australians whose lives continue across worlds."* This document is where
they live — portable, like `CARRY_FORWARD_CANON.md`, not World 01-specific.

**This is a story and continuity document, not prompt text.** `WORLD.md`'s own Form
rules say it directly: *"Name nobody. 'One bloke.' 'A woman.' 'Another mate.'"* That
rule doesn't change. A character here is never typed into a generation prompt by
name — the planner still gets "one bloke," "another mate." What this document governs
is which recurring person a given "one bloke" *is*, for continuity notes, captions,
and Identity-voice copy, tracked going forward via `CONTINUITY.md`'s new Cast
Rotation table (§4).

Visual identity consistency for a named character across independently generated
images is a harder, separate problem this document doesn't solve — see §5.

## 1. Shape of the cast

Not a fixed roster. Per `BRAND_VOICE.md` §1: *"the boundary is the occasion, not the
demographic."* Real friend groups work the same way — a core who are basically always
there, and a wider circle who show up depending on which night it is. A buck's do
pulls a different subset than a girls' trip to the same group of fifteen.

**The Core Eight** anchor most scenes and carry the recurring bits. **The Wider
Circle** are named, real, and appear situationally — the ones who make "whole
street's here now" true rather than a slogan.

## 2. The Core Eight

### Brock
The one who keeps trying to finish the same story all night — never quite lands it,
never stops trying. Main-character energy without being the one anyone follows.
Refuses to be the one who calls it.

### Charlotte
Carries everyone's drinks, unasked. The organiser nobody elected — group chat,
group order, the one who knows what time it actually is. Doesn't perform the role;
just does it.

### Tommy
Steals chips off someone else's plate before they've noticed. Youngest-energy of the
group regardless of actual age. Always hungry, always first to say yes.

### Emma
Says "just one" and means it for about forty minutes. The bit everyone lets her keep
running. Most reliably still out at sunrise despite having protested the loudest at
9pm.

### Mitch
The missing mate. Wanders off mid-conversation, group has to go find him, always
comes back with a story nobody can verify. Grace's other half — see §3.

### Sophie
Always in someone else's hoodie by midnight. The sentimental one — the who's-got-the-
photos, the one who says "wait, everyone get in." Cold regardless of season.

### Damo
Phone's always on 4%. Somehow still the one everyone trusts to navigate. Mild,
constant, good-natured chaos — the borrowed charger is always his to lend or need.

### Grace
Drifts ahead of the group in a pair sometimes, laughs before the punchline lands.
Dry, quiet, the one whose approval actually registers. Mitch's other half.

## 3. Relationships worth carrying

- **Mitch and Grace** are the actual couple — the two who drift ahead of the group,
  per `WORLD.md` Level 2. Not performed for anyone; just where they end up.
- **Brock and Charlotte** run the group's oldest joke — "if they ever got together
  the group chat would implode" — and it's never gone anywhere and probably never
  will. That's the joke.
- **Tommy** is Brock's unofficial little brother — no relation, just the dynamic.
  First to copy him, first to take the fall for it.
- **Damo and Sophie** end up on op-shop-hoodie duty together more often than makes
  sense. Neither has noticed.

## 4. The Wider Circle

Named, real, occasional — pulled in by the occasion rather than always present:
**Lucas, Casey, Ethan, Oliver, SK, Kevin, Gary.**

SK goes by initials, not a full first name — the one exception to the group's naming
pattern, and nobody remembers why anymore, including her.

These eight aren't sketched into roles yet. Do that when a specific world or scene
actually needs one of them to carry a bit — a role invented ahead of the occasion
that calls for it tends to feel assigned rather than discovered.

## 5. What this doesn't solve

Two things this document deliberately leaves open:

- **Visual consistency.** Nothing here guarantees "Mitch" looks like the same person
  across two independent generations. That's a reference-image-conditioning problem
  (see `reference_active_limit` in Studio's config), not a story problem, and it
  isn't solved by naming him.
- **Casting.** `docs/SESSION_HANDOVER_2026-08-07.md` §9 names casting rules and
  "designing for the photograph someone else takes" as live, unresolved strategic
  questions. This document gives future casting a cast list to cast *for* — it
  doesn't answer who plays them.
