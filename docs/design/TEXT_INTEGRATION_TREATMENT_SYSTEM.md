# shirtfaced — Text Integration Treatment System

Status: Working design system

## Purpose

Define a stable but rotating family of ways that slogans, observations, captions, wordmarks and secondary language can be integrated into shirtfaced artwork without collapsing into one repeated house layout.

The goal is recognisable authorship without rinse-and-repeat composition.

This system sits downstream of the concept. It must never invent the joke, explain the joke or rescue a weak concept.

The concept comes first. The treatment is selected because it best serves the concept.

---

## 1. New canon finding — ordinary people, outrageous labels

Older people are an approved recurring subject family in shirtfaced.

This is not a generic `unexpected old person doing something wild` recipe.

A distinct shirtfaced mechanism is:

**ordinary subject + sincere documentary image + outrageously disproportionate caption**

Examples:

- old woman waiting at a bus stop → `LOOSE UNIT`
- older man playing lawn bowls → `ABSOLUTE WEAPON`
- Victorian gentleman holding a cabbage → `ITSBULLSHITMATE`
- kebab worker doing an ordinary shift → `BLOODY OATH`

The subject does not perform the caption.

The humour comes from the confidence of the false characterisation. The image supplies little or no evidence for the claim; the caption states it as settled fact.

This is structurally different from the nun cannonballing into the pool:

- the nun actually performs the outrageous act;
- the old woman at the bus stop does nothing outrageous;
- both retain dignity;
- neither image should wink at the audience.

### Guardrail

Do not reduce older people to novelty props. Age is not itself the joke.

The person must be photographed or illustrated with sincerity, dignity and enough visual interest to carry the design before the caption is read.

### Transferable principle

`false characterisation delivered with absolute confidence`

### Protected lesson

Do not turn this into `random ordinary person + aggressive slang` at scale. Subject, phrase and image must have enough tension to feel discovered rather than generated.

# 2. The treatment problem

shirtfaced needs recurring text behaviours that create recognition while leaving room for radically different artwork.

A single fixed lockup would become predictable.

Unlimited treatment freedom would lose authorship.

The proposed answer is a **rotating stable**:

- finite enough to become recognisable;
- broad enough to avoid formula;
- deterministic enough for a future design engine;
- flexible enough to work across photography, illustration and pure type;
- independent of garment placement.

Initial recommendation: maintain **12 active treatment families**.

Not every drop must use all 12.

No treatment receives a mandatory quota.

A treatment may be retired, revised or added only when repeated approved work shows the need.

---

# 3. Treatment roles

Text entering the artwork must first be classified by role.

## T0 — None

Image stands alone. No copy required.

## T1 — Observation

A phrase that labels or observes the image without explaining it.

Examples: `LOOSE UNIT`, `ABSOLUTE WEAPON`, `BLOODY OATH`.

## T2 — Contradiction

Language deliberately conflicts with what the image appears to show.

## T3 — Dialogue fragment

A line that feels overheard rather than written as ad copy.

Examples: `you're not welcome`, `what the ever loving fuck`.

## T4 — Proposition

The words themselves are the main concept.

Examples: `itsbullshitmate`, `come as you are. don't be a dickhead.`

## T5 — Identity

`shirtfaced`, `sf`, club marks or other permanent brand identity.

## T6 — Secondary sting

A small second line that changes or sharpens the first reading without explaining it.

Use sparingly.

## T7 — Provenance / micro-copy

Tiny secondary identity, edition, location, club, collection or nonsense metadata.

It must never be required to understand the design.

# 4. Rotating stable — 12 treatment families

## R01 — The Blunt Caption

A strong image with a short caption sitting outside the image field or immediately below it.

Characteristics:

- 1–4 words preferred;
- high confidence;
- caption does not explain the action;
- strongest for false characterisation and observational labels;
- image remains visually complete without it.

Canonical territory:

`LOOSE UNIT`
`ABSOLUTE WEAPON`
`BLOODY OATH`

Recognition cue: the emotional distance between sincere image and unreasonable label.

Risk: becoming a meme format. Reject if image + caption can be generated by simple noun/adjective substitution.

---

## R02 — The Intrusion

Text physically intrudes into the artwork rather than sitting politely around it.

Examples of behaviour:

- clips part of the image;
- crosses an edge;
- interrupts a frame;
- is partially obscured by the subject;
- appears to have arrived after the image existed.

Recognition cue: tension between visual field and language.

Risk: fake edginess. Intrusion must improve hierarchy or meaning, not just look streetwear.

---

## R03 — The Dead-Centre Verdict

One short phrase placed with unreasonable certainty in a visually calm composition.

No decoration needed.

Best for verdict language:

`itsbullshitmate`
`what the ever loving fuck`
`yeah righto`

Recognition cue: restraint plus conversational language.

Risk: slogan merch. Requires exceptional wording and proportion.

---

## R04 — The Formal Label

The language is treated with more institutional dignity than it deserves.

Possible visual registers:

- museum caption;
- formal portrait title;
- club inscription;
- luxury product naming;
- award title;
- commemorative line.

The formality is visual, not bureaucratic parody.

Recognition cue: good manners handling inappropriate or absurd content.

Risk: drifting into fake certificates, technical documents or novelty seals. Keep it visually disciplined.

---

## R05 — The Oversized Interruption

A very large word or short phrase becomes a graphic mass rather than a conventional caption.

It may interact with the image but must not reduce the image to background filler.

Recognition cue: scale shock.

Risk: every generic streetwear brand does giant type. The wording/image relationship must remain distinctly shirtfaced.

---

## R06 — The Quiet Sting

The obvious image or dominant wording gets a tiny secondary line that changes the reading.

The small line must reward discovery, not provide explanation.

Good secondary sting:

- contradiction;
- undercut;
- affectionate insult;
- unnecessary correction;
- tiny act of self-sabotage.

Bad secondary sting:

- explaining the joke;
- restating the image;
- generic brand boilerplate.

Recognition cue: delayed second hit.

---

## R07 — The Overheard Line

Text is treated as something somebody genuinely said, not as a slogan.

It can be fragmentary, grammatically imperfect or oddly specific.

Examples:

`you're not welcome`
`what the ever loving fuck`
`itsbullshitmate`

Recognition cue: lived language.

Risk: trying too hard to write Australian slang. If it sounds written for a T-shirt, reject it.

---

## R08 — The Wordmark Cameo

The design is not about the brand, but `shirtfaced` appears as a quiet author signature.

Possible behaviours:

- very small edge mark;
- integrated into a border;
- tiny secondary line;
- visual easter egg;
- micro signature separate from the principal caption.

`shirtfaced` is always lowercase when the wordmark is used.

Recognition cue: authorship rather than advertisement.

Risk: using the wordmark to rescue generic artwork.

---

## R09 — The Brand Hero

`shirtfaced` itself is the principal graphic proposition.

Use selectively while brand equity is still being earned.

The treatment must make the object desirable through typography, material, scale, composition or secondary idea — not rely on name recognition alone.

Recognition cue: pure identity.

Risk: premature logo worship.

---

## R10 — The Split Read

Two pieces of language are spatially separated so the second completes, reverses or damages the first.

The viewer should encounter them sequentially.

This can work across one artwork field without defining garment placement.

Recognition cue: timing.

Risk: setup/punchline comedy. Avoid writing conventional jokes with obvious punchlines.

---

## R11 — The Embedded Language

Words exist naturally inside the image world rather than being laid over it.

Examples:

- sign;
- hand-painted board;
- label;
- club banner;
- packaging;
- patch;
- handwritten note;
- environmental typography.

The text must belong to the scene.

Recognition cue: photographic or illustrated evidence rather than graphic overlay.

Risk: forcing signage into every scene. It must be plausible and conceptually necessary.

---

## R12 — No Words, Brand Still Present

No caption. No slogan. Potentially no visible wordmark.

Authorship comes from concept selection, casting/subject, emotional mechanism, art direction and treatment.

This is a required part of the stable because it prevents shirtfaced from becoming dependent on copy.

Recognition cue: worldview alone.

Risk: generic cool image. Apply the logo-removal and competitor-substitution tests aggressively.

---

# 5. Rotation rules

The stable is **not a template carousel**.

The engine must not choose treatment by simple round-robin or equal distribution.

Selection order:

1. determine whether text is required at all;
2. classify the text role;
3. identify the emotional mechanism of the concept;
4. select candidate treatment families that preserve ambiguity and dignity;
5. reject any treatment that explains the concept;
6. compare at least three viable treatments when the concept supports them;
7. select the one that creates the strongest product, not the one least recently used;
8. use recent-range history only as a diversity constraint after conceptual fit.

### Diversity constraint

A release or range should avoid obvious repeated use of the same treatment family when another treatment is equally strong.

Do not weaken a hero concept merely to satisfy treatment diversity.

---

# 6. Recognition without repetition

Recognition should accumulate from combinations of cues rather than one fixed device.

Potential cue stack:

- conversational wording;
- restraint;
- outrageous false characterisation;
- formal treatment of inappropriate language;
- image/text contradiction;
- confident negative space;
- delayed secondary sting;
- lowercase `shirtfaced` signature;
- dead-serious image treatment;
- refusal to explain;
- subject dignity;
- unexpected scale relationship.

A design does not need all of these.

The stable works when different designs share **two or three behavioural cues** while using different visual architectures.

That creates family resemblance rather than cloning.

# 7. Engine representation

Each approved design should eventually record:

```yaml
text_system:
  required: true|false
  roles:
    - observation
    - identity
  treatment_family: R01
  primary_copy: "LOOSE UNIT"
  secondary_copy: null
  wordmark_present: false
  copy_source: observed|canonical|generated|campaign
  explanation_risk: low|medium|high
  image_text_relationship: contradiction|reinforcement|independent|sequential|embedded
  text_dominance: none|micro|secondary|coequal|primary
  protected_elements: []
  prohibited_variations: []
```

The 32k+ reference corpus should later inform typography selection, scale, framing, position and production treatment **inside the selected family**.

It should not decide the copy, emotional mechanism or treatment family solely from statistical frequency.

---

# 8. Initial recommendation

Start with these 12 families as a working stable, then stress-test them against the existing concept library.

For every strong concept, generate **3 treatment candidates** without garment placement:

- safest natural fit;
- strongest alternative;
- deliberately unexpected but still valid treatment.

Review which treatment wins and why.

After 30–50 approved designs, analyse actual usage.

Then:

- merge families that behave identically;
- split families that are doing too much;
- retire treatments that repeatedly generate generic streetwear;
- promote repeated winning behaviours into stronger brand signatures;
- keep R12 no-text work permanently represented.

The stable should emerge from evidence, not be frozen because twelve sounded tidy.

# 9. Working principle

**Same worldview. Different delivery.**

The audience should recognise the bastard without knowing exactly what trick it is about to pull.
