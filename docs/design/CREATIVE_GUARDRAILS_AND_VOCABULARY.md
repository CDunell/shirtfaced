# shirtfaced — Creative Guardrails & Vocabulary

Status: Working design canon supplement

## Naming

`shirtfaced` is always lowercase in customer-facing use.

Uppercase may be used for initials/monograms such as SFC, SNC, SCC or SBC, but the written brand remains lowercase.

## The personality underneath the brand

shirtfaced is not primarily a drinking brand, profanity brand, Australian-joke brand or collection of funny slogans.

The deeper character is a hard exterior with soft actions.

It is warm-hearted, loyal, mischievous, defiant, self-deprecating, occasionally inappropriate and completely committed once the stupid idea has been proposed.

The recurring emotional mechanisms are:

- sincerity through insincerity
- affection through shit-giving
- loyalty through action
- vulnerability without performance
- humour without trying to be funny
- apparent hostility contradicted by obvious care

A useful real-world example is the ritual: someone brings their daughter a coffee every morning; she says `thank you`; the answer is `you're not welcome`. The action is caring. The language refuses sentimentality. That contradiction is shirtfaced.

Another is doing anything for a mate when they need it, then giving them shit for requiring the help. The care is demonstrated rather than announced.

A partner can puncture the hard exterior with a look, including the look that means an apology is now required. The toughness is real, but it is not emotional emptiness.

### Core behavioural rule

**shirtfaced should rarely tell you its personality. It should behave like it.**

The idea should feel like shirtfaced before the wordmark is added.

## Manners and profanity

Swearing is not the personality. It is punctuation.

shirtfaced may swear freely, but constant profanity turns the brand into a profanity brand and destroys the impact.

The stronger mechanism is restraint followed by profanity at precisely the point where it is least appropriate.

**Mostly restrained. Occasionally spectacularly inappropriate.**

Canonical expression:

> **shirtfaced has manners. It just can't be trusted with them.**

A beautiful, formal or polite visual treatment can make an unexpected swear significantly more shirtfaced than simply making the swear louder.

## Gleeful defiance

Another core mechanism is gleeful defiance of the expected.

The reference behaviour is:

> `You can't do that.`
> `Hold my fucking beer.`

This is not recklessness for its own sake. It is the instinct to hear a convention, expectation or unnecessary restriction and ask why it should be obeyed.

Canonical expression:

> **When convention says “you wouldn't,” shirtfaced asks “why the fuck not?”**

The nun cannonballing into the motel pool is a defining example. The nun is not the target of the joke. She is the hero. The expectation is restraint; she commits completely to the bomb into the deep end.

The lifeguard sitting earnestly in a toddler high chair works through a related mechanism: authority, dignity and the completely wrong situation, played absolutely straight.

`DO SOMETHING STRANGE FOR A CHANGE` belongs naturally to this part of the personality.

Absurdity should therefore usually come from breaking an expectation with conviction, not from randomness for its own sake.

## Subject vocabulary

### Dogs

If a dog is deliberately used as a shirtfaced design subject, the canonical dog vocabulary is:

- Australian Cattle Dog / Heeler
- Koolie

Do not default to fashionable generic streetwear dogs such as French bulldogs, Dobermanns, pitbulls or arbitrary breeds simply because they suit an aesthetic.

The Heeler/Koolie rule is not Australiana. These dogs belong naturally to the lived shirtfaced world.

Dog humour comes from behaviour, situation, expression and relationships — never costume or national symbolism.

A Heeler or Koolie may carry an entire image without text. Useful characteristics include intelligence, alertness, strong working-dog presence, total commitment to rest when resting, and the visual language of companionship without sentimentalising it.

Two dogs sharing a couch, stealing blankets, leaning on each other or simply occupying the same space can express mateship without announcing `mateship`.

### Subject selection principle

Use real things from the shirtfaced world, not Australian symbols.

A Heeler asleep on someone's washing may be shirtfaced.
A Heeler dressed in stereotypical Australian costume is souvenir content.

Subject vocabulary is a distinct future design-engine layer. Reference libraries may inform treatment, composition, placement and production, but they do not decide what belongs in the shirtfaced universe.

Conceptual pipeline:

`shirtfaced concept → subject vocabulary → treatment → composition archetype → garment/placement → production constraints`

## Australian identity

Australia is the origin, attitude, humour, language and standard.

It is not the clip-art library.

Do not default to native wildlife, meat pies, thongs, Hills Hoists, servos, wheelie bins, national foods, bush clichés or other tea-towel Australiana as a shortcut to identity.

Any Australian subject may appear when the concept genuinely demands it and the image succeeds without relying on national recognition. The Australianness itself must not be the joke.

## Retired visual lane

Scientific/specimen plates, engineering diagrams, technical drawings, blueprints, exploded views, numbered callouts, pseudo-academic charts and fake technical documentation are not a core shirtfaced design lane.

They may be revisited only if a future concept is exceptional enough to justify breaking this rule. Cleverness alone is not enough.

## Garment test

Every concept must answer: would someone actually pay to wear this if they encountered the garment before the explanation?

A design must succeed as a desirable product before the joke, story or brand context is applied.

A second test is now required:

**Would this still feel like shirtfaced if the wordmark were removed?**

If the answer is no, the concept or execution is relying on branding to manufacture authorship.

## Range behaviour

Mix subjects and visual languages aggressively.

Use text-only, image-only and combined designs.
Use photography, illustration, typography, polished graphics, crude graphics, sporting language, fashion language, poster language, editorial language and unexpected visual collisions.

Do not let one successful treatment become the house style for every design.

Whatever style or content is used, the authorship must remain recognisably shirtfaced.

Crops, tanks, crews, hoodies and headwear are their own products, not resized tees.

## Tone

Bold, funny, irreverent, optimistic, willing to give anything a go, attracted to the absurd, happy to poke fun at stereotypes, and punching up rather than down.

Dead-serious execution against a ridiculous premise remains a strong recurring mechanism, but not the only mechanism.

Targets of humour matter. Characters who violate expectations are often the heroes, not the victims of the joke. The nun bombing the pool is funny because she owns the moment, not because she is a nun being ridiculed.

## Favourite vocabulary

### itsbullshitmate

Preferred form: `itsbullshitmate`

One word. No apostrophe. No comma. Lowercase when used in normal brand styling.

It works because crushing the sentence together turns it from dialogue into a verdict.

Potential uses:
- tiny chest
- huge back
- cap embroidery
- hoodie sleeve
- neck-label easter egg
- tonal premium version
- giant condensed type
- understated country-club treatment
- `shirtfaced / itsbullshitmate`

Treat it as a recurring phrase/device rather than a one-off gag.

## Emerging shirtfaced test

A concept is moving toward shirtfaced when several of these are true:

1. The underlying action reveals more warmth than the language admits.
2. The joke is played straight rather than performed for the audience.
3. Someone breaks an expectation and becomes more admirable for doing it.
4. The humour punches up, inward or sideways rather than down.
5. Profanity appears because it belongs in the moment, not because the design needs edge.
6. The design remains desirable before its joke is explained.
7. Removing the logo does not remove the authorship.
8. The subject feels observed from real life rather than selected from an Australian-symbol checklist.
9. The design can be beautiful, classy, crude, loud, quiet or absurd without losing the underlying character.
10. There is enough confidence to leave part of the joke unsaid.
