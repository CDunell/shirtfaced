# shirtfaced — Creative Guardrails & Vocabulary

Status: Working design canon supplement

## Naming

`shirtfaced` is always lowercase in customer-facing use.

Uppercase may be used for initials/monograms such as SFC, SNC, SCC or SBC, but the written brand remains lowercase.

---

## 1. What shirtfaced actually is

shirtfaced is not primarily a drinking brand, profanity brand, Australian-joke brand, slogan brand or collection of funny shirts.

**shirtfaced is a worldview.**

The deeper character is a hard exterior with soft actions: warm-hearted, loyal, mischievous, defiant, self-deprecating, occasionally inappropriate and completely committed once the stupid idea has been proposed.

The recurring emotional mechanisms are:

- sincerity through insincerity;
- affection through shit-giving;
- loyalty through action;
- vulnerability without performance;
- humour without trying to be funny;
- apparent hostility contradicted by obvious care;
- confidence without taking yourself too seriously;
- inclusion without performance;
- defiance without cruelty;
- optimism that survives knowing things will probably go sideways.

### Core behavioural rule

**shirtfaced should rarely tell you its personality. It should behave like it.**

The idea should feel like shirtfaced before the wordmark is added.

The brand is not trying to manufacture an edgy character. The strongest source material comes from observed behaviour, relationships, rituals, contradictions and moments that already contain the worldview.

---

## 2. Hard outside, marshmallow inside

The toughness is real, but it is not emotional emptiness.

A useful character description is:

> **Sarcastic prick on the outside. Fucking marshmallow underneath.**

This is not necessarily customer-facing copy. It is an internal character specification.

The softness is normally shown through behaviour rather than declared. Someone brings their daughter a coffee every morning; she says `thank you`; the answer is `you're not welcome`. The action is caring. The language refuses sentimentality. That contradiction is shirtfaced.

Someone will do anything for a mate when they need help, then give them shit for requiring the help. Again, care is demonstrated rather than announced.

A partner can puncture the hard exterior with one look, including the look that means an apology is now required. Strength does not depend on size, volume or theatre.

### Strength without theatre

The toughest person in the room is not necessarily the person acting tough.

Authority can be quiet. Love can have more power than swagger. A small person can completely disarm a much larger smartarse without saying a word.

This is useful territory for both character writing and visual concepts because it reverses expected power without humiliating either person.

---

## 3. Inclusion and boundaries

Canonical expression:

> **Come as you are. Don't be a dickhead.**

This is both viable customer-facing language and a genuine brand principle.

shirtfaced is inclusive without making inclusion a performance.

Difference is ordinary. Sexuality, gender, background, age, appearance, family structure and other identity characteristics are not automatically the story, the joke or evidence of virtue.

Nobody should be used as a diversity prop.

Acceptance should normally be assumed rather than announced. Once someone is family, a mate or part of the group, they are allowed to participate fully in the same affection, teasing and shit-giving as everyone else.

The boundary is behaviour, not identity.

### Open door, firm boundary

- Everyone is welcome.
- Nobody is entitled to dominate the room.
- Respect gets respect.
- Difference does not require permission.
- Inclusion does not require submission to bad behaviour.
- Barging across other people's boundaries is not belonging.
- Punch up, inward or sideways; do not build humour by making vulnerable people the target.

A useful internal shorthand is:

> **Everyone welcome. No dickheads.**

The open door and the boundary must coexist. Remove the open door and the brand becomes exclusionary. Remove the boundary and it becomes performatively agreeable. Neither is shirtfaced.

---

## 4. Manners and profanity

Swearing is not the personality. It is punctuation.

shirtfaced may swear freely, but constant profanity turns the brand into a profanity brand and destroys the impact.

The stronger mechanism is restraint followed by profanity at precisely the point where it is least appropriate.

**Mostly restrained. Occasionally spectacularly inappropriate.**

Canonical expression:

> **shirtfaced has manners. It just can't be trusted with them.**

A beautiful, formal, polite, institutional or unexpectedly tasteful visual treatment can make an inappropriate phrase significantly more shirtfaced than simply making the swear louder.

Profanity should feel earned by the moment. If removing the swear destroys the entire concept, ask whether the concept was strong enough in the first place.

---

## 5. Gleeful defiance

Another core mechanism is gleeful defiance of the expected.

The reference behaviour is:

> `You can't do that.`
> `Hold my fucking beer.`

This is not recklessness for its own sake. It is the instinct to hear a convention, expectation or unnecessary restriction and ask why it should be obeyed.

Canonical expression:

> **When convention says “you wouldn't,” shirtfaced asks “why the fuck not?”**

The nun cannonballing into the motel pool is a defining example. The nun is not the target of the joke. She is the hero. The expectation is restraint; she commits completely to the bomb into the deep end.

The lifeguard sitting earnestly in a toddler high chair works through a related mechanism: authority, dignity and the completely wrong situation, played absolutely straight.

`DO SOMETHING STRANGE FOR A CHANGE` belongs naturally to this part of the personality.

Absurdity should therefore usually come from breaking an expectation with conviction, not from randomness for its own sake.

### Commitment is funny

Half-hearted absurdity is weak. Once the stupid idea is accepted, the character, composition and art direction should commit to it as if it were completely reasonable.

The visual should not wink at the audience to explain that it knows it is funny.

---

## 6. Self-deprecation without self-pity

shirtfaced can be confident and still admit failure, incompetence, bad judgement or inevitable embarrassment.

Canonical expression:

> **I'll take the rare win before I fuck it up again.**

The mechanism is not low self-esteem. It is being secure enough to make yourself part of the joke.

Failure should feel survivable. Mistakes become stories. Embarrassment is not tragedy. The worldview tends toward `well, that happened` rather than misery.

This matters because the brand should remain fundamentally optimistic even when the language is cynical.

---

## 7. Australian identity

Australia is the origin, attitude, humour, language and standard.

It is not the clip-art library.

Do not default to native wildlife, meat pies, thongs, Hills Hoists, servos, wheelie bins, national foods, bush clichés or other tea-towel Australiana as a shortcut to identity.

Any Australian subject may appear when the concept genuinely demands it and the image succeeds without relying on national recognition. The Australianness itself must not be the joke.

### Cultural mechanism, not costume

Useful Australian qualities include understatement, anti-pretension, affectionate insult, giving things a go, distrust of unnecessary ceremony, taking the piss out of yourself, practical loyalty and the instinct to puncture anyone getting too impressed with themselves.

These should emerge through behaviour and language rather than national symbols.

---

## 8. Subject vocabulary

### Dogs

If a dog is deliberately used as a shirtfaced design subject, the canonical dog vocabulary is:

- Australian Cattle Dog / Heeler;
- Koolie.

Do not default to fashionable generic streetwear dogs such as French bulldogs, Dobermanns, pitbulls or arbitrary breeds simply because they suit an aesthetic.

The Heeler/Koolie rule is not Australiana. These dogs belong naturally to the lived shirtfaced world.

Dog humour comes from behaviour, situation, expression and relationships — never costume or national symbolism.

A Heeler or Koolie may carry an entire image without text. Useful characteristics include intelligence, alertness, strong working-dog presence, total commitment to rest when resting, and the visual language of companionship without sentimentalising it.

Two dogs sharing a couch, stealing blankets, leaning on each other or simply occupying the same space can express mateship without announcing `mateship`.

### Subject selection principle

Use real things from the shirtfaced world, not Australian symbols.

A Heeler asleep on someone's washing may be shirtfaced.
A Heeler dressed in stereotypical Australian costume is souvenir content.

Subject vocabulary is a distinct future design-engine layer. Reference libraries may inform treatment, composition, placement and production, but they do not decide what belongs in the shirtfaced universe.

Conceptual pipeline:

`shirtfaced worldview → concept mechanism → subject vocabulary → treatment → composition archetype → garment/placement → production constraints`

The worldview and concept mechanism must remain upstream of the reference engine. A corpus can teach how excellent apparel is constructed. It cannot be allowed to average shirtfaced into the taste of the corpus.

---

## 9. Humour target and dignity

The target of the joke matters.

Characters who violate expectations are often the heroes, not the victims of the joke. The nun bombing the pool is funny because she owns the moment, not because she is a nun being ridiculed.

People in shirtfaced imagery should generally retain dignity even when the situation is ridiculous.

Useful question:

**Who has agency in this joke?**

If the answer is `the person we're laughing at has none`, the concept needs scrutiny.

The strongest shirtfaced humour often lets the subject win.

---

## 10. Retired visual lane

Scientific/specimen plates, engineering diagrams, technical drawings, blueprints, exploded views, numbered callouts, pseudo-academic charts and fake technical documentation are not a core shirtfaced design lane.

They may be revisited only if a future concept is exceptional enough to justify breaking this rule. Cleverness alone is not enough.

Likewise, generic streetwear aesthetics do not become shirtfaced merely because a wordmark or swear is added.

---

## 11. Garment test

Every concept must answer:

**Would someone actually pay to wear this if they encountered the garment before the explanation?**

A design must succeed as a desirable product before the joke, story or brand context is applied.

A second test is required:

**Would this still feel like shirtfaced if the wordmark were removed?**

If the answer is no, the concept or execution is relying on branding to manufacture authorship.

A third test:

**Would this idea still be interesting if the profanity were removed?**

A fourth:

**Is the design showing the worldview, or merely printing a sentence that describes it?**

---

## 12. Range behaviour

Mix subjects and visual languages aggressively.

Use text-only, image-only and combined designs.
Use photography, illustration, typography, polished graphics, crude graphics, sporting language, fashion language, poster language, editorial language and unexpected visual collisions.

Do not let one successful treatment become the house style for every design.

Whatever style or content is used, the authorship must remain recognisably shirtfaced.

Crops, tanks, crews, hoodies and headwear are their own products, not resized tees.

### One worldview, many visual languages

Consistency comes from the decision-making intelligence behind the work, not from forcing every design through one visual filter.

The reference corpus should expand the number of ways shirtfaced can express itself, not narrow the brand toward the average visual style of successful streetwear.

---

## 13. Tone

Bold, funny, irreverent, optimistic, warm-hearted, willing to give anything a go, attracted to the absurd, happy to poke fun at stereotypes, and punching up rather than down.

Dead-serious execution against a ridiculous premise remains a strong recurring mechanism, but not the only mechanism.

shirtfaced may be classy, quiet, beautiful, filthy, crude, loud, tender, stupid or unexpectedly sincere. The surface tone can change. The worldview underneath should not.

### Anti-performative rule

Do not strain to prove that shirtfaced is funny, Australian, inclusive, rebellious, caring or cool.

If those qualities need to be announced constantly, the work is not demonstrating them strongly enough.

## 14. Favourite vocabulary

### itsbullshitmate

Preferred form: `itsbullshitmate`

One word. No apostrophe. No comma. Lowercase when used in normal brand styling.

It works because crushing the sentence together turns it from dialogue into a verdict.

Potential uses:

- tiny chest;
- huge back;
- cap embroidery;
- hoodie sleeve;
- neck-label easter egg;
- tonal premium version;
- giant condensed type;
- understated country-club treatment;
- `shirtfaced / itsbullshitmate`.

Treat it as a recurring phrase/device rather than a one-off gag.

### what the ever loving fuck

Current canonical phrase:

`what the ever loving fuck`

Its strength is escalation through unnecessarily elaborate language. It is disbelief with manners attempting — unsuccessfully — to remain involved.

Do not overuse it. It works because it feels like something someone actually says when ordinary `what the fuck` is insufficient.

### you're not welcome

This phrase has meaning only when paired with an action that proves the opposite. Used alone it is hostility; used after an act of care it becomes shirtfaced affection.

### come as you are. don't be a dickhead.

This is both vocabulary and philosophy. It may support customer-facing use when the execution avoids corporate-values language.

### I'll take the rare win before I fuck it up again

A tonal reference for self-deprecation without self-pity. Potential customer-facing use should preserve its conversational rhythm rather than polishing it into advertising copy.

## 15. Emerging shirtfaced test — v2

A concept is moving toward shirtfaced when several of these are true:

1. The underlying action reveals more warmth than the language admits.
2. The joke is played straight rather than performed for the audience.
3. Someone breaks an expectation and becomes more admirable for doing it.
4. The humour punches up, inward or sideways rather than down.
5. Profanity appears because it belongs in the moment, not because the design needs edge.
6. The design remains desirable before its joke is explained.
7. Removing the logo does not remove the authorship.
8. The subject feels observed from real life rather than selected from an Australian-symbol checklist.
9. The design can be beautiful, classy, crude, loud, quiet or absurd without losing the underlying character.
10. There is enough confidence to leave part of the joke unsaid.
11. The person inside the joke retains dignity or agency.
12. The concept shows care through behaviour rather than announcing sentiment.
13. The idea would still have something to say without a swear word.
14. The work welcomes people without turning identity into marketing collateral.
15. The design is willing to make shirtfaced itself, or the wearer, part of the joke.
16. Failure, embarrassment or bad judgement is treated as survivable rather than tragic.
17. The absurdity follows an internal logic instead of being random.
18. The execution commits completely once the premise is accepted.
19. A competitor's logo could not be substituted without something essential feeling wrong.
20. It feels observed, lived or recognised rather than focus-grouped into existence.

## 16. Known gaps to keep discovering

The worldview is now substantially clearer than the visual language. The following remain deliberately unresolved and should be learned through repeated concept and design rounds rather than invented prematurely.

### 16.1 Visual signatures

Which recurring non-logo cues make an image recognisably shirtfaced across photography, illustration and typography?

Do not force an answer. Record patterns from approved work.

### 16.2 Emotional range boundaries

How tender can shirtfaced become before it loses its bite? How dark can it become before optimism disappears? How crude can it become before it becomes cheap?

These boundaries require examples and counterexamples.

### 16.3 Sexuality and sex humour

The brand can be adult, cheeky and inappropriate, but the line between confident sexual humour and lazy objectification is not yet codified. Discover it through review, keeping agency and punch direction central.

### 16.4 Conflict and anger

The voice can say `fuck off`, but when is anger funny, when is it protective, and when does it simply become aggression? Protective loyalty appears native to the worldview; cruelty does not.

### 16.5 Sentimentality threshold

Affection is central, but overt sentimentality may weaken the characteristic contradiction. Determine when direct sincerity is powerful precisely because it is rare.

### 16.6 Brand authority over time

Some pure `shirtfaced` identity products will become stronger as recognition grows. Do not confuse future brand equity with current product desirability. Until the wordmark has earned standalone demand, identity products must still succeed through form, treatment, garment quality or secondary idea.

### 16.7 Community behaviour

`Come as you are. Don't be a dickhead.` should eventually govern comments, collaborations, events, moderation, ambassadors and customer interactions, not just apparel copy. Operational rules are not yet written.

### 16.8 Engine protection

The large reference corpus will create powerful evidence about composition, placement, hierarchy, colour, print method and garment architecture. Guard against letting frequency become taste. Popularity in the corpus is evidence of a pattern, not permission to erase the worldview.

The engine should optimise **expression of a shirtfaced concept**, not generate a concept by averaging what other brands have done.

---

## 17. Final creative rule

A technically excellent design that could have come from any competent streetwear brand is a failure.

A funny idea that nobody wants to wear is also a failure.

The target is both:

**desirable product + unmistakable worldview.**

When those two agree, it is shirtfaced.
