# shirtfaced — The Wearer Is the Audience

Status: Canonical creative rule

## Core rule

**The wearer is the audience.**

shirtfaced does not need a stranger looking at the garment to understand the setup, punchline, irony, reference or observation.

If they get it, great.

If they don't, nothing is lost.

In many cases, making the idea less immediately legible to an outside observer makes the joke stronger for the person wearing it.

A shirt is not required to explain itself.

---

## We don't explain

Never add information solely to help an observer understand the joke.

If removing something makes a design more cryptic **without making it less meaningful to the wearer**, remove it.

Do not add:

- explanatory back copy;
- a second line that translates the first;
- commentary describing why the image is funny;
- text that restates what the image already communicates;
- a slogan that turns an observation into a conventional punchline;
- brand philosophy used as an instruction manual for the concept.

The audience is allowed to wonder:

> What the fuck does that mean?

The wearer may know exactly what it means. That is enough.

---

## Design-engine rule

When evaluating optional text or visual information, ask:

1. Does this add a new tension, contradiction, rhythm, authorship or second reading?
2. Or is it only helping an outsider understand?

If the answer is the second, delete it.

The engine should prefer unresolved confidence over explanatory completeness.

`meaningful to wearer > legible to observer`

`observation > explanation`

`implication > commentary`

`confidence > accessibility of the joke`

---

## Example — RARE WIN

`RARE WIN` is sufficient.

If the image shows a magnificently trivial achievement being treated with absurd importance, additional copy such as `NOTHING SPECIAL. EVERYTHING CELEBRATED.` weakens the garment because it explains the mechanism.

Valid supporting treatments may include:

- `RARE WIN` only;
- the image only;
- `RARE WIN` plus a small shirtfaced mark;
- image plus `RARE WIN`;
- no back print at all.

The concept should not explain why the achievement constitutes a rare win.

---

## Relationship to the wider canon

This rule strengthens the existing restraint principle: **we don't explain.**

It also clarifies who the work is actually for.

shirtfaced is not designing jokes printed on shirts for spectators to consume.

**shirtfaced designs shirts for people who are already in on the joke.**

That distinction is canonical.
