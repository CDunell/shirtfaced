## Status

## Purpose
Define recurring graphic-construction archetypes used by successful premium apparel brands. This document classifies mechanics only. It does not create SHIRTFACED concepts, stories, slogans, collections or lore.

## Evidence Base
This pass extends the existing corpus with official current product and collection pages from Stüssy, Represent and Kith, alongside the previously reviewed official-product corpus covering Butter Goods, Carhartt WIP, Aries, Rhude, Awake NY, Fear of God Essentials, Maharishi, by Parra, Willy Chavarria and Re/Done.

New products and ranges reviewed include:

- Represent Classic Parts and Owners’ Club Script;
- Kith current Graphic Tees range, The Matrix Has You, Morpheus, Trinity, Icon and Collage tees.

Important limitation: this pass establishes recurring construction families. It does not claim market-share percentages or exact dimensional frequency.

# Core Finding

Premium graphic apparel does not rely on one visual style. It repeatedly uses a small set of construction archetypes. Cohesion comes from controlling hierarchy, framing, identity placement and information density while allowing subject matter and illustration style to change.

The constitutional system should therefore approve graphic archetypes, not prescribe one permanent aesthetic.

---

# Primary Graphic Archetypes

## G1 — Isolated Emblem

### Definition
One self-contained mark, symbol, crest, mascot or compact composition surrounded by substantial garment space.

### Evidence
Compact front marks recur across Stüssy, Kith, Carhartt WIP, Butter Goods, Rhude and Represent. They range from pure logo marks to small illustrated devices, but retain a single readable silhouette.

### Mechanic
The emblem behaves as one visual object. Internal complexity may vary, but the outer boundary remains compact and immediately legible.

### Constitutional rule
An isolated emblem must:
- read as one silhouette before its details are inspected;
- have one dominant centre of gravity;
- avoid detached decorative fragments that weaken the boundary;
- retain intentional clear space around the complete form.

### Confidence
High.

---

## G2 — Framed Image

### Definition
A photograph, illustration or collage presented inside an explicit or implied rectangular field, often with a caption, brand name or small information line.

### Evidence
Photographic and poster-derived front graphics recur across Carhartt WIP, Kith, Represent, Rhude and collaboration products. Kith’s Collage and film-derived tees, Carhartt WIP photographic prints, and Represent poster or motorsport-derived treatments all use bounded image fields.

### Mechanic
The frame converts complex imagery into one manageable shape. It creates a strong first-read rectangle before the viewer processes the contents.

### Constitutional rule
A framed image must have:
- one dominant image field;
- a deliberate relationship between image edge and garment space;
- supporting text attached to the frame or clearly separated from it;
- no accidental half-frame created by unrelated floating elements.

The frame may be visible, cropped or implied by the image boundary.

### Confidence
High.

---

## G3 — Poster Stack

### Definition
A vertically organised composition combining headline, image, secondary wording and small information into a single poster-like unit.

### Evidence
This structure appears repeatedly in music, motorsport, film and event-derived graphics across Represent, Kith, Stüssy and Rhude. It also appears in several Carhartt WIP and Butter Goods graphics where illustration and informational text form one bounded hierarchy.

### Mechanic
The eye enters through one dominant element, then moves through secondary image or language, then resolves in small supporting information.

### Constitutional rule
A poster stack must contain no more than three hierarchy levels:
1. primary entry element;
2. secondary image or display text;
3. supporting information.

Additional elements must merge into one of those levels rather than creating a fourth competing tier.

### Confidence
High for the three-level reading structure. Exact element count remains contextual.

---

## G4 — Image With Attached Wordmark

### Definition
A primary illustration or photograph with a brand wordmark, title or short phrase integrated above, below, across or immediately beside it.

### Evidence
This hybrid appears across Stüssy, Kith, Carhartt WIP, Butter Goods, Aries, Rhude and Represent. The wordmark is not a separate slogan panel; it functions as part of the image composition.

### Mechanic
The image earns attention while the wordmark supplies identity or interpretation. Either may lead, but they share one visual boundary.

### Constitutional rule
The relationship must be one of the following:
- title above image;
- caption below image;
- wordmark intersecting image;
- wordmark contained inside image;
- image replacing part of letterform or lockup.

Floating text with no alignment, overlap or framing relationship is not integration.

### Confidence
High.

---

## G5 — Type-Led Display

### Definition
Typography is the hero graphic rather than a caption supporting imagery.

### Evidence
Type-led hero treatments recur in Stüssy World Tour and S Box systems, Represent Owners’ Club and motorsport families, Aries display treatments, Butter Goods logo tees and Kith logo-led products.

### Mechanic
Desirability depends on proprietary lettering, scale, rhythm, outline, distortion, texture or historical reference rather than verbal cleverness alone.

### Constitutional rule
A type-led graphic must succeed as a shape before it succeeds as a sentence. Approval requires:
- a distinctive silhouette;
- controlled spacing and line breaks;
- clear primary and secondary type roles;
- no reliance on the wording alone to make generic typesetting desirable.

### Confidence
High.

---

## G6 — Information System

### Definition
A composition built from addresses, numbers, dates, locations, technical labels, icons, lists or administrative-looking information.

### Evidence
Stüssy World Tour and DNA-derived systems, motorsport treatments from Represent, jersey-number systems, and various Kith and Carhartt WIP graphics use structured information as visual material.

### Mechanic
Small factual-looking components create density and authenticity while a larger identifier provides the entry point.

### Constitutional rule
Information-system graphics must use:
- one dominant identifier;
- one alignment grid;
- repeated spacing logic;
- consistent secondary type styling;
- controlled icon family where icons are present.

Random microtext used merely to fill space is prohibited.

### Confidence
High.

---

## G7 — Freeform Illustration

### Definition
An illustration without a hard rectangular frame, allowing the silhouette of the subject or scene to define the outer boundary.

### Evidence
This recurs across Butter Goods, by Parra, Aries, Stüssy, Awake NY and Carhartt WIP. Subjects range from characters and objects to surreal or symbolic scenes.

### Mechanic
The illustration reads as a cut-out object against the garment. Its contour is therefore more important than internal detail at first glance.

### Constitutional rule
A freeform illustration must pass a silhouette test:
- convert the artwork to a single solid shape;
- confirm that the principal subject remains recognisable or visually compelling;
- remove detached details that do not improve the first read;
- protect negative space inside and around the silhouette.

### Confidence
High.

---

## G8 — Collage Field

### Definition
Multiple photographs, illustrations, fragments, textures or symbols combined into one bounded composition.

### Evidence
Kith explicitly sells collage artwork, while collage construction also recurs in collaboration and poster-derived products from Represent, Stüssy, Rhude and music or film ranges.

### Mechanic
The collage appears complex at close range but must collapse into one dominant mass at distance.

### Constitutional rule
A collage must have:
- one dominant visual anchor;
- one consistent crop or edge logic;
- no more than two secondary focal clusters;
- one unifying treatment such as shared colour, halftone, border, texture or overlap language.

A collection of unrelated assets placed together is not a collage system.

### Confidence
Medium-high.

---

## G9 — All-Over or Seam-Challenging Field

### Definition
Artwork approaches, crosses or deliberately interacts with seams, sleeves or the outer garment boundary.

### Evidence
Stüssy’s current Sport tee explicitly permits printing across seams on some sizes. Jumbo treatments also recur in Represent collaboration and large-scale graphic families.

### Mechanic
The garment body becomes the image field rather than merely holding a centred print.

### Constitutional rule
This archetype is exceptional, not default. It requires:
- production approval for seam interaction;
- size-by-size placement testing;
- tolerance for controlled variation;
- artwork that remains coherent when distorted by drape and construction.

### Confidence
Medium-high as a recognised premium mechanic; low as a general default.

# Framing Systems

Every graphic must select one framing state before development:

## F0 — Unframed
The artwork boundary is defined by its natural silhouette.

## F1 — Hard frame
A visible rectangle, circle, crest, badge or panel encloses the artwork.

## F2 — Implied frame
A photograph, colour block or aligned text creates a boundary without a drawn outline.

## F3 — Broken frame
Elements intentionally cross the boundary while the frame remains readable.

## F4 — Garment field
Seams or the body of the garment act as the frame.

### Constitutional rule
One graphic may combine a hard or implied frame with a controlled break. It must not mix several unrelated frame logics unless one is clearly subordinate.

# Information-Density Classes

## D1 — Sparse
One emblem, wordmark or subject. Immediate read; minimal secondary detail.

## D2 — Layered
One hero plus one supporting type or image layer.

## D3 — Dense
Poster, collage or information system with multiple details but one clear entry point.

### Constitutional rule
Density is not quality. A design must choose its density class deliberately.

- D1 requires exceptional silhouette or typography.
- D2 requires a clear hero/support relationship.
- D3 requires an explicit grid, frame or overlap system.

A design that is visually dense without a dominant entry point fails.

# Visual Entry Path

## Finding — Premium graphics are scanned in stages

Across image-led, type-led and hybrid products, the eye is normally given:
1. one first-read mass;
2. one second-read identity or subject;
3. optional close-read detail.

### Constitutional rule
Every graphic must be reviewed at three distances:

- **Distance read:** Does one silhouette, frame or word shape register?
- **Conversational read:** Is the main subject or title clear?
- **Close read:** Do secondary details reward inspection without being required for comprehension?

A design that only works at close range is not approved as a hero graphic.

# Image-First Versus Text-First

## Image-first
The image creates desire; type identifies, frames or rewards inspection.

## Text-first
The lettering itself creates desire through form, scale and execution.

## Hybrid
Image and text form one inseparable lockup.

### Constitutional rule
Every concept must declare one of these modes before design begins. Image and text must not compete for equal dominance unless they are intentionally fused into a hybrid lockup.

---

# Recognition Without Repetition

The reviewed brands create cohesion through combinations of:
- recurring layout archetypes;
- permanent identity marks;
- repeated scale relationships;
- controlled blank and wash choices;
- recurring type voices;
- familiar framing systems;
- repeated production methods.

They do not require every seasonal illustration to share one art style.

### Constitutional rule
SHIRTFACED cohesion should be built from stable mechanics surrounding variable artwork. Seasonal subject matter may change substantially provided the garment still uses approved layout, hierarchy, identity, framing and production systems.

---

# Anti-Patterns

The following are mechanically weak unless deliberately justified:

## AP1 — Equal competition
Image, headline, logo and supporting text all demand first attention.

## AP2 — Floating debris
Small stars, icons, words or textures are added without alignment or structural purpose.

## AP3 — Generic type carrying the concept
The wording is expected to sell the shirt while the lettering remains ordinary.

## AP4 — Unbounded complexity
A detailed illustration or collage lacks one dominant outer shape or frame.

## AP5 — Fake information
Random dates, coordinates or microtext imitate authenticity without a coherent information system.

## AP6 — Decorative distress
Texture is applied uniformly as a filter rather than responding to image construction, print process or garment wash.

## AP7 — Redundant surfaces
Front and back repeat equal versions of the same composition without creating a second visual role.

## AP8 — Technique as concept
Puff, crack, rhinestone, flock or specialty ink is expected to make weak artwork desirable.

# Provisional Constitutional Graphic Selection Process

Before artwork begins, specify:

1. Placement archetype: A1–A8 from the main research constitution.
2. Graphic archetype: G1–G9.
3. Framing state: F0–F4.
4. Density class: D1–D3.
5. Reading mode: image-first, text-first or hybrid.
6. Scale role by active garment zone: S0–S4.
7. Permanent identity placement.
8. Primary, secondary and optional tertiary hierarchy.
9. Canonical blank, colour, fit, weight and wash.
10. Print method and any specialty modifier.

A design should not proceed to finished artwork until these mechanics are internally coherent.

# Findings Not Yet Constitutionalised

This pass does not establish:
- how frequently each graphic archetype occurs across the whole premium market;
- exact image-to-text ratios;
- exact maximum number of collage assets;
- exact print dimensions;
- exact negative-space percentages;
- a mandatory preference for illustration, photography or typography;
- a mandatory distress treatment;
- a universal ink-count ceiling.

Those require neutral catalogue counting and direct image measurement.
