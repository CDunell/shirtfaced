# SHIRTFACED — Collection Architecture Research

## Status
Active — evidence-based research pass

## Purpose
Document repeatable collection-level mechanics used by successful premium apparel brands. This file concerns how products are organised into coherent ranges. It does not define stories, campaigns, worlds or garment concepts.

## Evidence Standard
Every finding is classified as one of:
- **Observed evidence** — directly visible in current official brand catalogues or official brand history.
- **Supported inference** — a reasonable mechanism derived from multiple observed examples.
- **Derived planning framework** — useful for Shirtfaced, but not presented as an industry fact.

Exact ratios are not constitutional unless directly measured across a sufficiently broad neutral sample.

# Findings

## Finding 01 — Strong Collections Contain Persistent Product Families

### Observed evidence
Represent treats Owners Club as a long-running product family rather than a one-season graphic. The range spans tees, hoodies, outerwear, pants, shorts, sweaters, tanks, caps and bags. The current Owners Club catalogue contains both long-running core pieces and newer silhouettes. Represent's official history states that the first Owners Club hoodie shaped the brand's future and that colourway expansion drove strong uptake.

Fear of God ESSENTIALS similarly repeats stable product families including classic tees, hoodies, sweatpants, crewnecks and multi-pack basics while seasonal collections alter fabrics, cuts, colour and collaboration content.

### Supported inference
A persistent family gives the range continuity, lowers the recognition burden on every new product and creates a dependable commercial base around which more expressive garments can rotate.

### Constitutional implication
A Shirtfaced range should not consist only of unrelated seasonal graphics. It must contain at least one repeatable product family whose identity can survive changes in colour, fit, fabrication and category.

### Confidence
High.

---

## Finding 02 — Signature Identity Can Expand Across Categories

### Observed evidence
Represent extends Owners Club across tees, hoodies, zip-throughs, mesh shorts, tanks, caps, tote bags, outerwear, pants and sweaters while retaining the family name and identity. Fear of God ESSENTIALS extends the ESSENTIALS identity across tees, hoodies, sweatshirts, sweatpants, shorts, outerwear, denim, hats, tanks, dresses and footwear.

### Supported inference
Recognition becomes stronger when the same identity system appears on different garment forms without requiring identical artwork placement on each item.

### Constitutional implication
Core identity systems must be portable. Every permanent Shirtfaced family should specify how it translates to at least:
- tee;
- hoodie or crewneck;
- cap or small accessory.

Portability does not mean identical sizing or placement. Each garment needs a native execution.

### Confidence
High.

---

## Finding 03 — Colour Depth Is a Collection Mechanic, Not Just a Product Option

### Observed evidence
Represent's Owners Club tee and hoodie families are offered in large colour runs, with current official pages showing roughly fourteen or more colours on staple products. The wider Owners Club collection also retains a controlled garment-colour vocabulary across multiple categories.

Fear of God ESSENTIALS similarly repeats broad neutral and muted colour groups across categories while introducing seasonal colours and collaboration-specific palettes.

### Supported inference
Colour-depth allows one recognised construction to satisfy different customer preferences without fragmenting the collection's identity. It also creates visual volume in the range without requiring a new graphic for every SKU.

### Constitutional implication
For proven core products, colour expansion may be a more coherent growth mechanism than constant graphic invention. Colourways must be planned at family level, not chosen independently product by product.

### Confidence
High for colour-depth as a recurring mechanic. No universal colour count is supported.

## Finding 04 — Core Families Evolve Through Controlled Variables

### Observed evidence
Represent describes Owners Club's evolution through refined fits, elevated fabrics, new silhouettes, updated textures, water-based graphics and washed finishes while retaining the same identity. Its 2025 reimagined range contained 33 pieces for men and women and was explicitly framed as an evolution rather than a replacement.

Fear of God ESSENTIALS repeats classic silhouettes while changing fleece weight, nylon construction, vintage cuts, sport forms, washes and collaboration content.

### Supported inference
A durable family remains commercially alive by changing controlled variables while preserving its recognition structure.

### Constitutional implication
Every repeatable family must distinguish:

**Locked elements**
- family name;
- identity asset;
- core recognition behaviour;
- approved placement logic.

**Variable elements**
- colour;
- fit;
- fabrication;
- wash;
- secondary placement;
- category;
- seasonal accent.

A refresh should change variables without erasing the reason the family is recognised.

### Confidence
High.

## Finding 05 — Capsules Are Narrower Than Core Ranges

### Observed evidence
Represent x Oasis was a nine-product capsule spanning tees, hoodies and accessories, significantly narrower than the 100-plus-product Owners Club family. Fear of God ESSENTIALS collaboration groups similarly reuse a small set of garment types—such as merch tees, hoodies, crewnecks, sweatpants and shorts—across multiple partner identities.

### Supported inference
A capsule concentrates attention by limiting category breadth and repeating a small number of product forms around one temporary subject or partner.

### Constitutional implication
A Shirtfaced capsule should be treated as a bounded product event, not allowed to overwrite or impersonate the permanent range. It should define:
- capsule identity;
- limited garment set;
- relationship to permanent Shirtfaced identity;
- start and end state.

### Confidence
High.

---

## Finding 06 — Collaboration Architecture Recombines Existing Systems

### Observed evidence
Represent x Oasis combines existing Owners Club products and established Represent garment forms with Oasis-specific imagery and titles. Fear of God ESSENTIALS collaboration-led assortments retain ESSENTIALS garment naming and silhouettes while adding league, team or institution identities.

### Supported inference
Successful collaborations are recognisable as both parties because the host brand contributes a stable product architecture rather than functioning merely as a blank merchandise supplier.

### Constitutional implication
Future Shirtfaced collaborations must preserve at least two Shirtfaced recognition cues, such as:
- canonical fit or blank;
- known placement architecture;
- permanent micro-branding;
- established typography behaviour;
- stable product-family name.

### Confidence
High.

## Finding 07 — A Collection Needs Different Levels of Visual Intensity

### Observed evidence
Current Represent and ESSENTIALS catalogues contain quiet staples, repeated logo families, more expressive graphics, technical garments and bounded collaborations within the same overall brand offer. Not every product is designed as the visual hero.

### Supported inference
If every garment is equally loud, the range has no rhythm and no dependable entry product. Different intensity levels serve different customer intentions and prevent visual competition across the collection.

### Constitutional implication
Every proposed range must classify garments by visual intensity:
- **quiet** — minimal identity or tonal execution;
- **core** — recognisable family execution;
- **statement** — dominant graphic or unusual treatment;
- **event** — capsule or collaboration-specific.

This is a planning framework derived from observed assortment structure, not a measured industry taxonomy.

### Confidence
Medium-high for the underlying mechanic; medium for the four-part framework.

---

## Finding 08 — Product Repetition Can Be Intentional and Valuable

### Observed evidence
Represent repeats the same Owners Club tee and hoodie construction across many colourways. Fear of God ESSENTIALS repeats classic tees, hoodies and fleece products across colours, genders, seasons and collaborations.

### Supported inference
Repeated products are not evidence of creative failure when they function as recognised staples. They provide continuity, lower purchase risk and make expressive drops easier to understand.

### Constitutional implication
Do not require every SKU to introduce a new visual concept. Repetition is permitted when at least one meaningful variable changes and the product belongs to an established family.

### Confidence
High.

---

## Finding 09 — Collection Naming Creates Navigable Architecture

### Observed evidence
Represent organises products under recognisable named systems including Owners Club, Original Owners Club, 247, seasonal lines and collaborations. Fear of God separates ESSENTIALS as a pillar and uses collection, sport, institution and collaboration naming to organise product groups.

### Supported inference
Naming helps customers understand whether a garment is core, seasonal, technical or collaborative before examining the individual design.

### Constitutional implication
Every product must belong to a named system. Avoid orphan products that cannot be clearly assigned to:
- permanent family;
- seasonal collection;
- capsule;
- collaboration;
- experimental category.

### Confidence
High.

---

## Finding 10 — Collection Cohesion Can Come From Shared Construction Rather Than Shared Artwork

### Observed evidence
Within Owners Club, products vary in silhouette, category and colour while retaining family naming, identity and material/finish logic. ESSENTIALS collections similarly connect varied categories through repeated silhouettes, restrained identity, colour behaviour and naming rather than one repeated graphic.

### Supported inference
A collection can remain coherent while individual graphics differ if shared construction rules remain visible.

### Constitutional implication
At least three collection-level cues must repeat across a range. Approved cue classes include:
- family identity asset;
- garment silhouette;
- placement architecture;
- colour behaviour;
- fabrication or wash;
- typography behaviour;
- trim or micro-branding;
- naming convention.

This "three-cue" threshold is a derived review framework, not a measured market law.

### Confidence
High for multi-cue cohesion; medium for the three-cue threshold.

## Finding 11 — Two Product Lines May Separate Acquisition From Monetisation

### Status

**Derived planning framework, not observed or supported evidence.** This finding did not
come from the Represent/ESSENTIALS corpus above — it comes from `docs/SESSION_HANDOVER_
2026-08-07.md` §3. Recorded here because Hot List item 9 asks for it named explicitly, not
because this pass's evidence standard has been met. Treat the confidence rating below
literally.

### The hypothesis

| | Line 1 — Brand mark | Line 2 — Standalone design |
|---|---|---|
| Bought because | It's Shirtfaced | The design is good |
| Buyer | Already knows the word | Has never heard of us |
| Job | Monetise believers | Recruit strangers |
| Branding | Headline | Signature — back neck, hem, sleeve |
| Economics | High margin, infinite reorder | Variance, failure rate, drops |
| Contains | Wordmark, crest, all drinkware, group custom | The five design mechanics |
| Review weighting | Recognition-heavy | Must survive HF-09 |

### Supporting evidence

**For:** Black Milk Clothing's sequencing — designs sold first, community-branded product
followed once an audience existed to sell it to. Unsourced beyond `SESSION_HANDOVER_2026-
08-07.md` itself — no citation trail, unlike the Represent/ESSENTIALS findings above.

No counter-example is recorded. An earlier draft of this finding cited Budgy Smuggler as a
contradicting case; that comparison is not a model for Shirtfaced and the claim behind it
was never independently verified, so it is withdrawn rather than repeated here unsourced.

### Constitutional implication

`DESIGN_REVIEW_SCORECARD.md` HF-09 ("Weak Without the Logo") already tests for the Line 2
property without naming it: a design that fails HF-09 is functioning as Line 1 and should
be reviewed as one — weighted toward Brand Recognition (category F) and permanent identity
assets, not toward independent visual value. A design that clears HF-09 comfortably is
functioning as Line 2 and should be reviewed for whether it can recruit a stranger who has
never heard of Shirtfaced, which the current weighted categories do not separately test.

Every product should record which line it is functioning as, alongside its Collection Role
(§4 of the Constitution) and Layer (below) — a Line 1 product in the Permanent Core layer
and a Line 2 product in the Statement Graphics layer are the common cases, but the two axes
are independent, not the same distinction twice.

### Cheapest test

Post both to a cold organic surface and read tag-and-send rate, not likes — paid testing is
the wrong instrument for an organic-first brand. Untested for Shirtfaced as of this
document.

### Confidence

Low. One unsourced supporting example, no counter-example on record, no Shirtfaced-specific
data. Do not treat the table above as settled review policy — treat it as the question the
next handful of designs should answer.

# Collection Architecture Model

The following model is derived from the findings and is intended as a planning tool.

## Layer 1 — Permanent Core
Long-running, replenishable product families. Recognition and wearability matter more than novelty.

## Layer 2 — Seasonal Evolution
Existing families adjusted through colour, fabrication, silhouette, finish or secondary graphics.

## Layer 3 — Statement Graphics
More expressive products that generate visual energy while remaining inside the permanent mechanics.

## Layer 4 — Capsules and Collaborations
Bounded events with narrower product sets and a defined end state.

The model does not prescribe quantities or revenue allocation.

# Mandatory Collection Review

Before approving a collection, record:
1. Which products belong to permanent families?
2. Which products are seasonal evolutions?
3. Which products are visual statements?
4. Which products are capsules or collaborations?
5. Which identity cues repeat across the range?
6. Which colours are family colours and which are seasonal?
7. Which garments act as quiet entry products?
8. Which garment is the intended visual hero?
9. Does every product have a clear named home?
10. Are any products competing without serving a distinct role?
11. Can the core identity translate across tee, fleece and accessory?
12. Does a collaboration still look like Shirtfaced?
13. Is each product functioning as Line 1 (brand mark) or Line 2 (standalone design), and
    is it reviewed with the matching weighting — see Finding 11?

# Anti-Patterns

- A range made entirely from unrelated statement graphics.
- Treating every product as a hero.
- Creating a new logo treatment for every garment.
- Expanding one concept across too many categories without demand evidence.
- Using colourways with no family-level logic.
- Allowing collaborations to erase the host brand.
- Calling random products a collection because they launched together.
- Replacing proven staples merely to appear creative.
- Applying the same artwork dimensions to every garment category.
- Adding filler SKUs that serve no commercial or visual role.

---

# Claims Not Yet Supported

This pass does not support:
- a universal staple-to-statement ratio;
- an ideal number of products per collection;
- a universal number of colourways;
- a mandatory product-category mix;
- a universal drop cadence;
- a claim that larger collections perform better;
- a specific pricing ladder;
- that the Line 1 / Line 2 split in Finding 11 is settled review policy rather than an
  untested hypothesis.

Those require neutral commercial data rather than catalogue observation alone.
