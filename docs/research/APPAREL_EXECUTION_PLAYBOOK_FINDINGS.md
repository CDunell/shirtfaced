# SHIRTFACED — Apparel Execution Playbook Findings

## Status
Integrated secondary-source findings

## Source
User-supplied **Streetwear Apparel Design Playbook**, provided 7 August 2026. The playbook summarises claimed recurring practice across Supreme, Stüssy, Palace, Kith, Corteiz, Fear of God, Off-White and current 2025–26 streetwear leaders.

## Evidence Classification
This document does not treat every supplied statement as independently proven fact.

- Rules that align with the existing multi-brand research corpus are promoted as **supported mechanics**.
- Garment-specific dimensional figures are retained as **working production ranges**, not constitutional law, until verified against blanks, decoration vendors and measured garments.
- Brand examples are retained as context, not as sole evidence.

# Integrated Findings

## Finding 01 — One Dominant Idea Per Garment

### Observation
The supplied playbook repeatedly prioritises one clear focal point, one strong idea and immediate readability.

### Alignment with existing research
This reinforces the existing findings on hierarchy, negative space, scale roles and controlled information density. It also supports the graphic-archetype requirement that each design declare a primary visual entry point.

### Constitutional rule
Every garment must have one dominant proposition. Secondary branding, language and decoration may support it but must not compete with it.

A design fails this rule when two or more elements demand equal first attention without an intentional split-surface hierarchy.

### Confidence
High.

---

## Finding 02 — Readability Must Be Tested at Multiple Distances

### Observation
The playbook requires high contrast and legibility from several feet away while warning against tiny type, low-contrast artwork and fragile linework.

### Alignment with existing research
This directly supports the established distance, conversational and close-reading tests.

### Constitutional rule
Every design must pass three viewing conditions:

1. **Distance read** — the silhouette, focal point or dominant word is recognisable from several feet away.
2. **Conversational read** — secondary image and type hierarchy becomes clear at normal social distance.
3. **Close read** — small supporting details reward inspection without being necessary to understand the primary design.

Low contrast may be used only when subtlety is the declared concept and the primary form remains readable through silhouette, scale or tonal separation.

### Confidence
High.

---

## Finding 03 — Staples and Seasonal Graphics Perform Different Commercial Roles

### Observation
The playbook separates simple logo or wordmark staples from expressive graphic drops.

### Alignment with existing research
This reinforces the constitutional separation between permanent identity and seasonal artwork.

### Constitutional rule
The product system must distinguish:

- **Staples:** repeatable identity-led garments built around stable wordmarks, logos, monograms or icons.
- **Seasonal graphics:** concept-led garments that create novelty, energy and collection change.

Seasonal graphics must not mutate the permanent identity system. Staples must not be burdened with seasonal complexity.

### Confidence
High.

---

## Finding 04 — Brand Consistency Comes From Locked Identity Assets, Not Identical Artwork

### Observation
The playbook calls for consistent logo treatment, type style and colour logic while allowing expressive graphics.

### Alignment with existing research
This supports the distinction between permanent and seasonal voices, but the phrase “same type style and colour system across the whole line” is too rigid to adopt literally. Existing evidence shows successful brands use multiple seasonal display voices and palettes while maintaining stable identity anchors.

### Constitutional rule
The following permanent assets must remain controlled:

- official logo proportions;
- clear-space rules;
- approved wordmark constructions;
- permanent identity typography;
- core one-colour reproductions;
- canonical identity placements;
- approved brand-colour relationships.

Seasonal display typography and colour may vary when at least one permanent recognition mechanism remains intact.

### Confidence
High.

---

## Finding 05 — Garment Geometry Is Part of the Composition

### Observation
The playbook repeatedly requires designing around body length, seams, pockets, armholes, stretch, hood position and garment drape rather than approving isolated flat artwork.

### Alignment with existing research
This strongly reinforces the existing blank-and-print architecture rule.

### Constitutional rule
No artwork is production-approved until tested on the intended garment block.

The specification must account for:

- neckline and collar clearance;
- shoulder slope;
- chest width;
- body length;
- armholes;
- pockets;
- hood overlap;
- seams and panels;
- fabric stretch and drape;
- expected print distortion when worn.

A flat artwork file or generic mock-up is insufficient evidence of correct placement.

### Confidence
High.

---

## Finding 06 — Production Restraint Is a Design Advantage

### Observation
The playbook favours fewer colours, solid shapes, embroidery-safe marks and avoidance of ultra-fine details.

### Alignment with existing research
This supports screen-print-first construction and the rule that specialty techniques are modifiers rather than concepts.

### Constitutional rule
Artwork must be constructed for its intended decoration method from the beginning.

For screen print:
- preserve printable line weights;
- avoid detail that depends on perfect registration;
- use deliberate spot-colour or controlled halftone logic;
- ensure the design survives a reduced-colour version.

For embroidery:
- simplify internal detail;
- avoid fragile strokes and small counters;
- test minimum letter height and stitch density with the supplier;
- preserve recognisable silhouette at cap scale.

Fewer colours are preferred where they strengthen clarity, consistency, cost control and reproduction. They are not a universal aesthetic ceiling.

### Confidence
High for restraint; medium for any fixed colour limit.

# Garment-Specific Execution Rules

## Graphic Tee

### Supported mechanics
- Declare one focal point.
- Use a front hero, back hero or coordinated front/back hierarchy.
- Preserve negative space around the dominant composition.
- Use line weight and colour separations appropriate to screen printing.
- Secondary identity may occupy the chest, back neck, shoulder, sleeve or hem.

### Working dimensional guidance
The supplied range of roughly 8–12 inches for a large centred print is retained as a production starting point only. Final width must be resolved proportionally against the actual blank and size grading.

### Prohibited defaults
- unstructured collage with no dominant entry point;
- essential tiny text;
- low contrast without a deliberate subtlety strategy;
- fine detail that cannot survive production.

---

## Logo Tee

### Supported mechanics
- The logo must remain recognisable in one colour.
- Proportions, spacing and construction must not drift between releases.
- Small chest and centred placements are both valid archetypes.
- Logo garments function as stable repeatable products rather than experimental artwork fields.

### Working dimensional guidance
The supplied 2.5–4 inch chest-width range is retained as a starting band, subject to the logo silhouette, garment size and embroidery or print method.

### Prohibited defaults
- redrawing the permanent mark to fit each concept;
- detail that collapses at small size;
- arbitrary changes to aspect ratio, tracking or internal spacing.

---

## Wordmark-Led Garment

### Supported mechanics
- Typography is the primary image, not an annotation.
- Letterforms must remain legible and structurally sound at production size.
- Large centred, stacked, arched, chest-scale and back-scale treatments are valid archetypes.
- Tracking and word shape must be tested at actual size.

### Qualification
Condensed type is one valid tool, not a universal requirement. Scripts, extended sans, serif, blackletter and custom lettering remain available when aligned with the assigned typography voice and production method.

### Prohibited defaults
- fragile strokes;
- counters that fill in;
- ornamental complexity with no identity role;
- random seasonal mutation of the permanent wordmark.

---

## Crop Top

### Supported mechanics
- Placement must be recalculated for the shorter torso block.
- The primary graphic generally sits higher than on a standard tee.
- Wider or more horizontal compositions may better suit the reduced body length.
- Important content must remain clear of the hem and likely fold line.

### Working dimensional guidance
The supplied 2–3 inch neckline offset and up-to-12–14 inch width are retained only as initial fit-test ranges. They are not universal rules.

### Constitutional implication
Crop products require their own placement template. A regular tee layout must not be scaled or cropped blindly.

---

## Cap

### Supported mechanics
- Embroidery is the default premium decoration route unless another method is intentional.
- Primary front-panel art should be a logo, short wordmark, monogram or bold icon.
- Side and rear marks are secondary identity zones.
- The design must survive simplification and curved-panel distortion.

### Working dimensional guidance
The supplied maximum envelope of approximately 2–2.5 inches high by 4 inches wide is retained as a common starting range, subject to cap construction, frame limits and supplier specification.

### Prohibited defaults
- complex illustrative scenes;
- thin lines;
- small text;
- uncontrolled multi-colour stitching;
- assuming a flat rectangle across structured front panels.

---

## Tank

### Supported mechanics
- Use a dedicated narrower placement template.
- Keep critical artwork clear of armhole edges.
- Higher placement often preserves balance on the open silhouette.
- Compact logos, emblems and bold vertical or centred compositions are safer than wide tee-derived graphics.

### Constitutional implication
Tank artwork must be re-composed, not merely transferred from the tee file.

---

## Hoodie

### Supported archetypes

1. **Staple hoodie:** small chest identity with optional sleeve, neck or hem signature.
2. **Statement hoodie:** large back hero with smaller front identity above the kangaroo pocket.

### Supported mechanics
- Front art must clear the pocket and its visual mass.
- Back art must account for hood overlap in both worn-down and worn-up states.
- Sleeve prints are secondary zones, not automatic filler.
- Heavyweight fabric, drape and print hand materially affect the premium result.

### Working dimensional guidance
The supplied 6–7 inch top offset below the rear neck seam is retained as a fit-test starting point, not a constitutional constant.

### Prohibited defaults
- front designs swallowed by the pocket;
- back designs hidden by the hood;
- tee placements copied unchanged;
- large decoration approved without worn-body testing.

# Portable Workflow Gate

Every new garment design must answer:

1. Is this a staple or a seasonal graphic?
2. What is the one dominant proposition?
3. Which approved layout archetype does it use?
4. Which scale role applies to each active zone?
5. Which permanent identity asset remains stable?
6. Does the design pass distance, conversational and close-reading tests?
7. Is the artwork built for the actual print or embroidery method?
8. Has it been tested on the intended garment block and worn silhouette?
9. Are seams, armholes, pocket, hood, hem and stretch accounted for?
10. Does the design retain intentional negative space?

Failure on any item returns the design to development.

# Claims Retained as Guidance, Not Constitution

The following supplied values require supplier validation and blank-specific testing before becoming hard rules:

- 8–12 inch graphic-tee width;
- 2.5–4 inch logo/chest width;
- 2–3 inch crop neckline offset;
- 12–14 inch crop width;
- 2–2.5 inch by 4 inch cap embroidery envelope;
- 6–7 inch hoodie rear-neck offset.

They are useful working ranges. Treating them as universal would repeat the exact mistake this research phase is designed to avoid.

---

# Net Constitutional Additions

This playbook strengthens and extends the existing system with the following reusable rules:

- one dominant proposition per garment;
- mandatory three-distance readability testing;
- explicit staple-versus-seasonal classification;
- locked logo geometry and one-colour integrity;
- garment-specific placement templates for tee, crop, tank, cap and hoodie;
- decoration-method feasibility as an early design gate;
- hoodie pocket and hood exclusion zones;
- cap embroidery simplification requirements;
- prohibition on blindly porting one garment layout to another;
- dimensional values treated as starting ranges until verified.
