# SHIRTFACED — Graphic Composition Mechanics

## Status
Active — constitutional research pass

## Purpose
Define the repeatable composition mechanics that make premium graphic apparel readable, desirable and coherent before subject matter, humour or story are chosen.

This document does not create SHIRTFACED concepts. It converts the existing cross-brand research corpus into composition rules that can be applied to future artwork.

## Evidence Base
This pass synthesises the documented corpus already reviewed across Represent, Stüssy, Butter Goods, Aries, Rhude, Kith, Carhartt WIP, Awake NY, Fear of God Essentials, Maharishi, by Parra, Willy Chavarria and Re/Done, together with the accepted apparel execution playbook.

No new frequency claims are made here. Exact dimensional rules remain provisional until direct measurement is completed.

# Constitutional Findings

## Finding 01 — One Dominant Visual Proposition

### Observation
Successful graphic garments repeatedly present one element as the primary reason to look: one image, one wordmark, one symbol, one scene or one concentrated composition.

### Mechanism
The eye requires an immediate entry point. Secondary elements can add context, identity or reward, but they must not compete for first attention.

### Constitutional rule
Every design must identify one dominant visual proposition before refinement begins.

The dominant proposition may be:
- an image;
- an illustration;
- a word or wordmark;
- a symbol;
- a framed composition;
- a single scene;
- a deliberately unified collage.

A design fails hierarchy review when two or more elements demand equal first attention without an intentional reason.

### Confidence
High.

---

## Finding 02 — Composition Must Work at Three Reading Distances

### Observation
Premium graphic apparel combines immediate recognition with closer detail. The garment is encountered first at distance, then conversationally, then at inspection range.

### Constitutional rule
Every design must pass three reading tests:

1. **Distance read** — the dominant silhouette, colour block or word shape is recognisable from several metres away.
2. **Conversational read** — the main image, title or brand relationship is clear at normal social distance.
3. **Close read** — secondary text, texture, distress, provenance or fine detail rewards inspection.

A design that works only in a zoomed artwork file is not garment-ready.

### Confidence
High.

---

## Finding 03 — Hierarchy Should Usually Be Limited to Three Levels

### Observation
The reviewed systems repeatedly separate hero, support and signature functions. Overloaded compositions become difficult to read when too many elements operate at similar visual weight.

### Constitutional rule
A standard composition should use no more than three hierarchy levels:

- **H1 — Hero:** the first-read element.
- **H2 — Support:** title, secondary image, frame, number or contextual language.
- **H3 — Signature:** permanent logo, provenance, micro-text or production detail.

Additional levels require a clear structural function. Decorative accumulation is not a function.

### Confidence
Medium-high. The three-level ceiling is a strong working rule, not a statistically proven universal law.

---

## Finding 04 — Silhouette Precedes Detail

### Observation
Bold wordmarks, clear emblems, isolated figures, strong rectangular fields and large image masses remain legible before their internal detail is understood.

### Mechanism
At distance, the eye reads outer shape, contrast and mass before linework or small text.

### Constitutional rule
Every composition must have a recognisable outer silhouette or dominant mass.

The silhouette test must be performed by:
- reducing the artwork to thumbnail size;
- viewing it in monochrome;
- blurring it slightly;
- checking whether the principal shape remains identifiable.

Detail may enrich the design but must not be responsible for holding it together.

### Confidence
High.

---

## Finding 05 — Image-First, Type-First and Hybrid Are Separate Systems

### Observation
Premium graphics recur in three principal reading modes.

### Constitutional rule
Every design must declare one composition mode:

### C1 — Image-first
The image or illustration creates desire. Typography identifies, titles or rewards.

### C2 — Type-first
The lettering itself is the hero. Supporting imagery, if present, must not weaken the word shape.

### C3 — Hybrid
Image and typography form one inseparable lockup. Neither should appear pasted onto the other after completion.

A design must not drift between modes during development. When the image and text compete rather than integrate, the composition must be reclassified and rebuilt.

### Confidence
High.

---

## Finding 06 — Negative Space Is Protected Territory

### Observation
Successful products repeatedly preserve quiet zones around hero artwork, isolate small identifiers from large graphics and allow the garment body to remain visible.

### Constitutional rule
Negative space must be planned as an active component.

Every composition must define:
- collar clearance;
- side clearance or an intentional seam-breaking exception;
- separation between H1 and H3 elements;
- at least one quiet zone;
- a clear boundary between the artwork and garment construction unless using a jumbo-field archetype.

Unprinted space is not wasted space. It is what gives the printed area authority.

### Confidence
High for the principle. Exact percentages remain provisional.

---

## Finding 07 — Framing Must Have a Job

### Observation
Premium graphics use open compositions, bounded rectangles, badges, poster fields, circles, arches and seam-crossing fields. Frames are effective when they organise the artwork, not merely decorate it.

### Approved framing systems

- **F0 — Open field:** no enclosing border; the figure or word shape defines the composition.
- **F1 — Hard rectangle:** poster, photograph, label or print-block logic.
- **F2 — Soft boundary:** distressed edge, tonal fade, irregular crop or organic silhouette.
- **F3 — Emblem:** circular, oval, shield, badge or seal structure.
- **F4 — Typographic frame:** text forms the upper, lower or surrounding boundary.
- **F5 — Jumbo field:** artwork deliberately extends toward or across normal print boundaries.

### Constitutional rule
A frame must perform at least one function:
- contain visual complexity;
- establish silhouette;
- organise hierarchy;
- create contrast with the garment;
- signal a known visual form such as poster, badge or photograph;
- control cropping.

Frames added only because the centre feels empty must be removed or justified.

### Confidence
High.

---

## Finding 08 — Cropping Must Increase Energy or Focus

### Observation
Poster-derived, photographic and oversized graphics often crop figures, text or image fields. Effective cropping makes the garment feel larger than the printed rectangle or concentrates attention.

### Constitutional rule
Cropping is permitted only when it:
- strengthens the focal point;
- increases scale or movement;
- removes irrelevant information;
- creates a deliberate edge relationship;
- supports a known frame system.

Cropping must not remove the identifying part of the subject, damage the joke, interrupt essential lettering or look like an export error.

### Confidence
Medium-high.

---

## Finding 09 — Controlled Imbalance Is Stronger Than Accidental Misalignment

### Observation
Premium compositions may be centred, offset, asymmetrical or vertically weighted. Their strength comes from intentional balance rather than mandatory symmetry.

### Constitutional rule
A composition may use:
- optical centring;
- geometric centring;
- deliberate left or right bias;
- high or low vertical bias;
- asymmetrical counterweight.

The designer must identify the balancing device: negative space, secondary text, frame edge, colour mass, small signature or garment seam.

Nothing should be “slightly off” without a reason. That is not edge; it is poor alignment wearing sunglasses.

### Confidence
High.

---

## Finding 10 — Contrast Must Establish Reading Order

### Observation
High contrast is consistently useful for distance readability, but premium systems also use lower-contrast secondary marks, washes and tonal support elements.

### Constitutional rule
Contrast must be hierarchical rather than uniformly maximal.

- H1 receives the strongest contrast required for immediate reading.
- H2 may use reduced contrast to support rather than compete.
- H3 may be tonal, small or textural provided it remains technically reproducible.

Low contrast is permitted when the silhouette and placement remain clear. Weak contrast caused by indecision is not permitted.

### Confidence
High.

---

## Finding 11 — A Unified Collage Is One Composition; A Pile of Assets Is Not

### Observation
Collage, poster and archival-reference graphics can contain many components while still reading as one visual unit. Failure occurs when each component retains equal independence.

### Constitutional rule
Multi-element artwork must be unified by at least two of the following:
- one dominant frame;
- one colour treatment;
- one shared crop logic;
- one alignment grid;
- one dominant focal point;
- one texture system;
- one typographic hierarchy;
- one silhouette.

If the elements can be removed or rearranged without changing the design’s meaning or eye flow, the composition is probably assembled rather than designed.

### Confidence
Medium-high.

---

## Finding 12 — Brand Recognition Must Survive Seasonal Change

### Observation
The documented brands vary imagery, themes and display typography while retaining recognition through stable wordmarks, recurring layouts, micro-branding, scale relationships and production choices.

### Constitutional rule
Every seasonal composition must preserve at least two stable recognition mechanisms from the permanent system:
- identity mark;
- typography voice;
- placement archetype;
- scale relationship;
- colour discipline;
- framing behaviour;
- line or texture behaviour;
- micro-branding placement;
- blank and fit system.

A seasonal graphic should not need the campaign story to prove that it belongs to the brand.

### Confidence
High.

# Composition Archetypes

These archetypes classify the internal artwork, not its front/back garment placement.

## G1 — Isolated Icon
One object, symbol, figure or emblem with substantial surrounding negative space.

## G2 — Hero Scene
One recognisable scene or visual event. Supporting type remains secondary.

## G3 — Poster Field
Bounded image-and-type composition with a clear top-to-bottom reading order.

## G4 — Wordmark Monument
One word or short phrase treated as the principal graphic mass.

## G5 — Badge or Seal
Compact emblem using circular, oval, shield or crest logic.

## G6 — Image/Title Lockup
A dominant image paired with a title that forms one stable unit.

## G7 — Archival or Photographic Field
Photograph or found-image treatment contained, cropped or processed into a coherent print field.

## G8 — Controlled Collage
Multiple visual fragments unified into one silhouette and hierarchy.

## G9 — All-Over or Jumbo Field
The garment body becomes the visual field and normal margins are intentionally challenged.

# Mandatory Composition Review

Before artwork is approved, document:

1. Dominant proposition.
2. Composition mode: image-first, type-first or hybrid.
3. Graphic archetype: G1–G9.
4. Hero hierarchy element: H1.
5. Supporting hierarchy element: H2.
6. Signature hierarchy element: H3, if used.
7. Frame system: F0–F5.
8. Distance-read result.
9. Conversational-read result.
10. Close-read result.
11. Silhouette test result.
12. Quiet-zone location.
13. Stable brand-recognition mechanisms retained.
14. Production risks.
15. Worn-body review result.

A design that cannot answer these questions is not ready for aesthetic refinement.

---

# Anti-Patterns

Reject or rebuild compositions with:

- two unrelated hero elements;
- equal visual weight across every component;
- tiny essential text;
- detail-dependent silhouettes;
- accidental near-centering;
- decorative frames without function;
- images and type that appear pasted together;
- collage elements with no unifying treatment;
- contrast distributed equally across all hierarchy levels;
- distress used to disguise weak drawing;
- secondary branding enlarged to rescue weak artwork;
- print layouts approved only on flat mockups;
- crops that remove the identifying action or subject;
- quiet space filled merely because it exists.

---

# Rules Still Provisional

This pass does not establish:

- exact maximum number of components;
- exact negative-space ratios;
- exact focal-point coordinates;
- universal preference for centred or asymmetrical layouts;
- universal image-to-text percentage;
- universal maximum of three hierarchy levels for every deliberate maximalist design;
- exact dimensional thresholds for distance readability.

Those require direct image measurement and controlled wear testing.

---

# Constitutional Summary

A premium graphic garment is not made desirable by adding more design. It is made legible and confident by controlling:

- one dominant proposition;
- a recognisable silhouette;
- no more hierarchy than the concept needs;
- planned negative space;
- a deliberate frame or deliberate absence of one;
- controlled contrast;
- clear reading at three distances;
- integration of image and typography;
- stable brand-recognition mechanisms;
- the physical behaviour of the actual garment.
