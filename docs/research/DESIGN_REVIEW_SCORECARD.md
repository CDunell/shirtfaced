# SHIRTFACED — Design Review Scorecard

## Status
Operational framework — derived from the documented research corpus

## Purpose
Convert the design decision framework into a repeatable review and approval system.

This document is not a new research finding. It is an operating tool derived from:
- `GRAPHIC_DESIGN_SYSTEM_RESEARCH.md`
- `GRAPHIC_ARCHETYPE_TAXONOMY.md`
- `GRAPHIC_COMPOSITION_MECHANICS.md`
- `PRODUCTION_VISUAL_LANGUAGE.md`
- `BRAND_RECOGNITION_SYSTEMS.md`
- `BRAND_RECOGNITION_EVIDENCE_AUDIT.md`
- `COLLECTION_ARCHITECTURE.md`
- `DESIGN_DECISION_FRAMEWORK.md`

The scorecard does not turn taste into arithmetic. It prevents weak work from surviving because one attractive feature distracts from structural failures.

---

# 1. Review Principle

Every design is assessed in two layers:

1. **Hard gates** — non-negotiable pass/fail conditions.
2. **Weighted review** — quality assessment after every hard gate passes.

A high score cannot override a hard failure.

---

# 2. Mandatory Review Evidence

Every review submission must include:

- product and garment type;
- canonical blank and colour;
- collection role;
- layout archetype;
- graphic archetype;
- front and back artwork views;
- worn-body mock-up;
- small thumbnail view;
- greyscale view;
- silhouette or blur test;
- actual-size print proof or scaled production proof;
- colour and ink specification;
- print or embroidery method;
- permanent identity treatment;
- production constraints and unresolved risks.

A design cannot be approved from one floating artwork file or one generic mock-up.

---

# 3. Hard-Fail Gates

Any single failure returns the design to the relevant earlier stage.

## HF-01 — No Clear Product Definition

Fail when the garment, blank, fit, colour or production method is undefined.

Return to: Product Definition.

## HF-02 — No Collection Role

Fail when the design cannot state whether it is a core staple, recurring identity product, seasonal expression, capsule product or collaboration.

Return to: Collection Role Assignment.

## HF-03 — No Dominant Proposition

Fail when the reviewer cannot identify the main visual idea within three seconds.

Return to: Graphic Archetype or Composition Construction.

## HF-04 — Hierarchy Collapse

Fail when two or more elements compete equally for first attention without a deliberate dual-focus structure.

Return to: Composition Construction.

## HF-05 — Distance Failure

Fail when the design becomes an indistinct patch, loses its main silhouette, or depends on unreadable small detail at normal viewing distance.

Return to: Composition Construction.

## HF-06 — Garment Conflict

Fail when important artwork collides with seams, pockets, hood fall, armholes, plackets, stretch zones or expected folds.

Return to: Layout Selection or Production Validation.

## HF-07 — Production Failure

Fail when essential lines, gaps, tonal steps, text, embroidery detail or registration tolerances cannot be produced reliably by the nominated method.

Return to: Production Validation.

## HF-08 — Identity Substitution

Fail when a competitor logo could replace the SHIRTFACED identity without materially changing the design.

Return to: Recognition Review.

## HF-09 — Weak Without the Logo

Fail when the artwork has no independent visual value and relies on an enlarged logo to appear finished.

Return to: Concept or Graphic Construction.

## HF-10 — Collection Redundancy

Fail when the design duplicates the role, hierarchy, composition and visual intensity of another product in the same range without a deliberate series rationale.

Return to: Collection Cohesion Review.

## HF-11 — Mock-Up Only Success

Fail when the design works only because of lighting, styling, garment folds, model attitude or photographic treatment.

Return to: Composition or Product Definition.

## HF-12 — Unresolved Rights Risk

Fail when artwork, photography, trademarks, likenesses or references have unresolved usage rights.

Return to: Source and Rights Review.

# 4. Weighted Review

Score each category from 0 to 5.

- **0 — absent or structurally failed**
- **1 — materially weak**
- **2 — below release standard**
- **3 — competent and acceptable**
- **4 — strong**
- **5 — exceptional and clearly intentional**

Weighted total: 100 points.

## A. Product Fit — 10 points

Weight: ×2

Assess:
- relationship to garment silhouette;
- use of available print zones;
- compatibility with seams and construction;
- suitability for the nominated blank and wash;
- whether the physical garment improves the graphic rather than merely carrying it.

## B. Dominant Proposition — 10 points

Weight: ×2

Assess:
- immediate focal point;
- one clear primary idea;
- visual confidence;
- recognisable subject or form;
- absence of unnecessary competing devices.

## C. Composition and Hierarchy — 15 points

Weight: ×3

Assess:
- H1/H2/H3 reading order;
- balance or intentional imbalance;
- framing and cropping;
- eye path;
- relationship between front and back;
- control of density.

## D. Distance and Silhouette — 10 points

Weight: ×2

Assess:
- thumbnail recognition;
- blur-test survival;
- silhouette strength;
- contrast structure;
- readability at conversational and across-room distance.

## E. Typography — 10 points

Weight: ×2

Assess:
- identity, display and information roles;
- legibility at final size;
- spacing and alignment;
- type-to-image relationship;
- absence of decorative type without function;
- brand consistency without forced sameness.

## F. Brand Recognition — 15 points

Weight: ×3

Assess:
- use of permanent identity assets;
- recognisable placement or scale behaviour;
- consistency with established type and production language;
- competitor-substitution resistance;
- whether recognition survives removal of the largest logo.

## G. Collection Contribution — 10 points

Weight: ×2

Assess:
- defined range role;
- distinction from neighbouring products;
- contribution to collection rhythm;
- colour and intensity balance;
- compatibility with recurring product families;
- whether it expands the system without becoming unrelated.

## H. Production Integrity — 15 points

Weight: ×3

Assess:
- screen-print or embroidery feasibility;
- line and gap integrity;
- tonal separation;
- colour-count justification;
- registration tolerance;
- interaction with garment colour, underbase and wash;
- expected ageing behaviour.

## I. Commercial Wearability — 5 points

Weight: ×1

Assess:
- whether a customer could understand the product without explanation;
- whether the design remains wearable outside campaign styling;
- whether the graphic scale and print hand suit likely use;
- whether it offers a credible reason to purchase rather than merely demonstrating technique.

---

# 5. Score Calculation

For each category:

`category score = rating ÷ 5 × category maximum`

Maximum total: 100.

## Approval Bands

- **90–100 — Release candidate**  
  All hard gates passed. Only production proof and final rights confirmation remain.

- **80–89 — Strong, revise selectively**  
  Structurally sound. Specific documented changes required before release.

- **70–79 — Rework**  
  Contains a viable foundation but is not release-ready.

- **Below 70 — Reject or rebuild**  
  Too many structural weaknesses. Do not polish the existing execution by default.

A score of 90 does not approve a design with a hard failure.

---

# 6. Category Floors

Regardless of total score, release requires:

- Product Fit: minimum 3/5
- Dominant Proposition: minimum 4/5
- Composition and Hierarchy: minimum 4/5
- Distance and Silhouette: minimum 3/5
- Typography: minimum 3/5 when typography is present
- Brand Recognition: minimum 3/5
- Collection Contribution: minimum 3/5
- Production Integrity: minimum 4/5
- Commercial Wearability: minimum 3/5

The higher floors on proposition, composition and production reflect their structural role. A technically beautiful print of a weak idea remains a weak product. A brilliant concept that cannot be produced reliably remains unfinished artwork.

---

# 7. Review Sequence

Reviews must occur in this order:

1. Rights and source check.
2. Product and collection-role check.
3. Hard-fail review.
4. Thumbnail, blur and silhouette tests.
5. Front/back hierarchy review.
6. Typography review.
7. Recognition review.
8. Collection-wall review.
9. Production review.
10. Worn-body review.
11. Weighted scoring.
12. Decision and rollback assignment.

Do not begin with close-up craft inspection. Detail quality can seduce reviewers into ignoring weak structure.

---

# 8. Required Visual Tests

## T1 — Thumbnail Test

Display the complete garment small enough that fine detail disappears.

Pass when:
- the dominant graphic remains identifiable;
- the main contrast pattern remains clear;
- the visual proposition survives.

## T2 — Blur Test

Apply enough blur to remove small text and detail.

Pass when:
- H1 remains dominant;
- the design retains a distinct mass and eye path;
- secondary elements do not merge into visual sludge.

## T3 — Greyscale Test

Remove colour information.

Pass when:
- hierarchy survives through value and scale;
- colour is not doing all structural work.

Low-contrast designs may pass when subtlety is intentional, but their hierarchy must still remain detectable.

## T4 — Logo-Removal Test

Temporarily remove the permanent wordmark or logo.

Pass when:
- the seasonal artwork still has independent visual value;
- the remaining design still feels intentionally composed.

## T5 — Competitor-Substitution Test

Replace the identity mark mentally or physically with a competitor mark.

Pass when:
- the design becomes less coherent or clearly false;
- other SHIRTFACED recognition cues remain present.

## T6 — Range-Wall Test

Display the product beside the rest of the proposed range at equal scale.

Pass when:
- it belongs without disappearing;
- it contributes a distinct role;
- visual intensity is balanced across the range;
- no accidental repetition dominates.

## T7 — Worn-Movement Test

Review the design on a body from front, side and back, including normal arm position and garment folds.

Pass when:
- the focal point remains visible;
- important content is not destroyed by movement;
- placement appears deliberate rather than merely centred on a flat blank.

## T8 — Actual-Size Production Test

Output at intended production dimensions.

Pass when:
- smallest text and gaps remain viable;
- tonal steps remain distinct;
- line weights remain intentional;
- print hand and coverage remain acceptable.

---

# 9. Decision Outcomes

Every review ends with exactly one outcome.

## APPROVED FOR PRODUCTION PROOF

All hard gates passed, score and category floors met, no unresolved rights issue.

## APPROVED WITH SPECIFIC REVISIONS

The design is structurally sound. Revisions must be finite, named and independently verifiable.

## RETURN TO STAGE

The design remains viable but must return to a named stage in `DESIGN_DECISION_FRAMEWORK.md`.

## HOLD FOR COLLECTION

The product is individually strong but currently duplicates, overloads or unbalances the proposed range.

## REJECT

The design has no sufficiently strong foundation to justify further polishing.

“Needs work” is not a valid outcome.

# 11. Review Conduct Rules

Reviewers must:

- identify the failed mechanism, not merely state dislike;
- distinguish concept failure from execution failure;
- assess the garment before discussing campaign imagery;
- assess at intended scale before zooming into detail;
- avoid inventing a new design during review;
- assign every required revision to a documented rule or production risk;
- record disagreement rather than averaging incompatible opinions into vague compromise.

Reviewers must not:

- approve because substantial time has already been invested;
- rescue weak artwork with extra logos, texture or speciality ink;
- treat novelty as proof of quality;
- use campaign styling to conceal garment problems;
- demand consistency by making every design visually identical;
- reject variation merely because it is unfamiliar.

---

# 12. Calibration

The numerical thresholds in this document are derived operating rules, not externally researched commercial laws.

They must be calibrated against:
- first physical samples;
- production failure rates;
- internal review consistency;
- customer response;
- returns and wear feedback;
- performance of repeated product families.

Threshold changes must be documented. They must not be quietly adjusted to approve a favoured design.
