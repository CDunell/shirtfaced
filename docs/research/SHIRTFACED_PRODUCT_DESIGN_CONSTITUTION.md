# SHIRTFACED — Product Design Constitution

## Status
Active — consolidated operating constitution

## Purpose
Convert the SHIRTFACED product-design research into one governing system for developing, reviewing and approving premium graphic apparel.

This constitution governs System B — the Product Design System.

It does not govern the Brand Universe, photography, campaigns, characters, lore, social content or community activity.

---

# 1. Constitutional Principle

A SHIRTFACED garment must succeed as a desirable product before brand context, humour, story or marketing is applied.

No concept is exempt from the system because it is funny, topical, personally liked or visually novel.

The governing sequence is:

1. define the product;
2. define its role in the range;
3. select the garment architecture;
4. select the graphic architecture;
5. construct the composition;
6. integrate typography;
7. validate recognition;
8. validate production;
9. review against the collection;
10. approve, revise or reject.

---

# 2. Permanent and Seasonal Layers

## 2.1 Permanent mechanics

The following are constitutional and must remain stable unless formally revised:

- approved layout-archetype library;
- scale-role taxonomy;
- separation of permanent identity from seasonal artwork;
- controlled typography roles;
- recognition through multiple cues;
- screen-print-first construction logic for printed apparel;
- garment-specific placement logic;
- protected negative space;
- production validation on the actual blank;
- collection-role assignment;
- hard-fail review gates;
- documented decision records.

## 2.2 Seasonal variables

The following may change by product or collection:

- image subject;
- illustration language;
- photography use;
- display typography;
- palette within approved production limits;
- distress intensity;
- wash;
- print modifier;
- dominant print surface;
- cultural reference;
- collaboration treatment.

Seasonal variation must operate inside permanent mechanics.

---

# 3. Product Definition

Every product must be defined before artwork begins.

Required fields:

- garment category;
- canonical blank;
- fit block;
- fabric weight;
- garment colour;
- wash or surface treatment;
- production method;
- intended wearer and use context;
- collection role;
- commercial tier;
- target release or capsule.

Artwork created without a defined blank is exploratory only and cannot receive production approval.

---

# 4. Collection Role

Each product must be assigned one role.

## 4.1 Anchor

Persistent identity product that carries recognition over time.

## 4.2 Core

Repeatable, commercially dependable product that supports the range without dominating it.

## 4.3 Expression

Seasonal or drop-led product with stronger graphic intensity or novelty.

## 4.4 Hero

The principal statement product for a drop or capsule.

## 4.5 Collaboration

A product combining external intellectual property, artist input or partner identity with SHIRTFACED architecture.

These roles are operational categories derived from research. They are planning tools, not measured market laws.

A collection must not consist entirely of hero or expression products.

---

# 5. Garment Architecture

The garment must be treated as a complete object.

Each available zone must be assigned one state:

- active graphic zone;
- permanent identity zone;
- intentional negative space.

Zones include:

- front chest;
- full front;
- back;
- back neck;
- sleeves;
- hem;
- side seam;
- hood;
- cap front, side and back panels.

Blank space is valid only when chosen deliberately.

## A5 — Unequal Front-and-Back Graphics

## A6 — Image / Language Split

## A7 — Multi-Zone System

## A8 — Jumbo Field

Every product must select one archetype before final artwork development.

Departure from the library requires a written reason and additional review.

# 7. Scale Roles

Each print zone must be assigned a scale role.

- **S0 — Micro signature:** neck, hem, sleeve edge, woven flag or hardware-scale identity.
- **S1 — Chest identifier:** compact conversational-distance mark.
- **S2 — Emblem:** self-contained composition with substantial surrounding blank.
- **S3 — Hero:** dominant torso composition.
- **S4 — Jumbo:** approaches seams or uses the garment body as the graphic field.

Scale is defined by function first and dimensions second.

Final dimensions must be approved against chest width, body length, shoulder position, pocket location, hood interference, armholes and production limits.

---

# 8. Graphic Archetypes

Each design must select one dominant graphic archetype.

Approved families include:

- image-led hero;
- typographic hero;
- emblem or badge;
- image-and-title lockup;
- poster or editorial panel;
- symbolic icon system;
- collage with controlled frame;
- character or object portrait;
- all-over or jumbo field.

A design may contain supporting elements, but it must have one dominant archetype and one dominant proposition.

---

# 9. Composition Rules

## 9.1 One dominant proposition

A viewer must be able to identify what the design is primarily about.

## 9.2 Three-distance reading

Every design must work at:

- distance — silhouette, colour mass and dominant form;
- conversational range — principal image or words;
- close range — secondary detail and production texture.

Close-range detail cannot substitute for distance recognition.

## 9.3 Hierarchy

Use no more than three functional hierarchy levels unless a fourth is demonstrably necessary:

- H1 — dominant subject or title;
- H2 — supporting mark or language;
- H3 — provenance, detail or micro identity.

## 9.4 Negative space

Every composition must preserve intentional quiet areas and protected clearances.

## 9.5 Silhouette

The design must remain distinct when reduced to major tonal shapes.

## 9.6 Cropping

Cropping must increase scale, tension or focus. Accidental clipping is a failure.

## 9.7 Collage

Collage requires a unifying mechanism such as shared framing, tonal treatment, palette, edge language or alignment.

---

# 10. Typography System

Typography must be assigned by function.

## 10.1 Identity voice

Permanent proprietary wordmark, monogram or stable brand lettering.

## 10.2 Display voice

Seasonal headline, title, number or dominant expressive lettering.

## 10.3 Information voice

Supporting text, date, location, provenance or secondary language.

A fourth type voice requires a documented functional reason.

Typography must be tested at production size for:

- legibility;
- counter closure;
- stroke survival;
- spacing;
- embroidery feasibility where applicable;
- relationship to folds and seams.

Logo geometry, proportions and clear space must not drift between products.

---

# 11. Recognition System

Recognition must be distributed across multiple cues rather than relying on one oversized logo.

Approved recognition cues include:

- permanent identity mark;
- recurring typography behaviour;
- recurring layout architecture;
- recurring scale relationships;
- stable garment silhouettes;
- controlled colour behaviour;
- micro-branding placement;
- repeated production character;
- recurring naming structure.

Every product must retain at least two recognisable SHIRTFACED cues when the principal logo is removed.

Collaborations must combine partner material with existing SHIRTFACED architecture rather than replacing it wholesale.

---

# 12. Production Language

## 12.1 Screen-print-first

Printed artwork must work as a conventional screen print unless another method is structurally essential.

## 12.2 Line hierarchy

Artwork must use a deliberate line system:

- structural lines;
- secondary descriptive lines;
- detail lines.

Fine detail that cannot survive the intended process must be removed or enlarged.

## 12.3 Tone

Continuous tone must be converted into deliberate spot-colour, halftone or threshold systems.

## 12.4 Colour

Every additional ink must perform a clear role: subject separation, hierarchy, dimensionality or identity.

## 12.5 Distress

Distress must follow one coherent wear logic and must not be applied as random digital noise.

## 12.6 Registration

Misregistration may be used deliberately but must not compromise recognition, facial features, type legibility or essential edges.

## 12.7 Specialty techniques

Puff, flock, high-density, silicone, reflective, rhinestone and similar effects are modifiers, not concepts.

## 12.8 Embroidery

Embroidery requires simplified shapes, controlled stitch direction, minimum viable detail and proof at actual size.

## 12.9 POD

POD-compatible variants may simplify colour, texture and detail, but they must preserve hierarchy, silhouette and identity.

---

# 13. Garment-Specific Rules

## 13.1 Tee

Use approved front, back or two-surface architecture. Preserve collar and side clearance unless jumbo treatment is intentional.

## 13.2 Crop

Recalculate placement and scale for the shorter body. Do not reuse regular-tee placement blindly.

## 13.3 Tank

Protect armhole clearances and use a narrower composition.

## 13.4 Hoodie

Front graphics must respect the kangaroo pocket. Back graphics must account for hood coverage. Sleeve graphics are supporting zones unless deliberately promoted.

## 13.5 Cap

Front-panel artwork must be embroidery-first, compact and structurally simple. Side and rear marks remain secondary.

No garment adaptation may be treated as simple scaling.

---

# 14. Collection Architecture

A collection must contain hierarchy, not a row of competing hero products.

Each collection must define:

- persistent families;
- seasonal families;
- anchor products;
- core products;
- expression products;
- hero products;
- colour depth;
- category spread;
- collaboration boundary if relevant;
- visual-intensity rhythm.

Cohesion must come from multiple cues such as layout, typography, palette, silhouette, production and identity placement.

Exact product ratios remain provisional until tested against SHIRTFACED sales and range performance.

---

# 15. Required Review Tests

Every product must undergo:

- thumbnail test;
- blur test;
- greyscale test;
- silhouette test;
- distance test;
- logo-removal test;
- competitor-substitution test;
- worn-body test;
- front/back relationship review;
- range-wall review;
- production feasibility review;
- actual-size typography and detail review.

---

# 16. Hard-Fail Conditions

A product cannot be approved if any of the following is true:

- the product or blank is undefined;
- the collection role is undefined;
- the dominant proposition is unclear;
- hierarchy collapses at thumbnail size;
- essential text is illegible at production size;
- the graphic conflicts with seams, pockets, hood or armholes unintentionally;
- the artwork depends on unsupported production detail;
- permanent identity geometry has been altered without approval;
- the design becomes generic when the logo is removed;
- the design is indistinguishable from a competitor after name substitution;
- the garment has not been reviewed on-body;
- production files do not match the approved art.

Hard fails cannot be averaged away by a high score elsewhere.

---

# 17. Approval States

- **Exploration** — concept or artwork under development.
- **Design Review** — composition sufficiently developed for constitutional review.
- **Revision Required** — specific failed gates must be corrected.
- **Design Approved** — visual system approved; production proof still required.
- **Production Review** — separations, dimensions, blank and method under review.
- **Production Approved** — ready for sampling or manufacture.
- **Rejected** — proposition or execution does not justify further development.
- **Archived** — retained for reference but inactive.

No garment advances because the team is tired of revising it.
