# SHIRTFACED — Design Decision Framework

## Status
Active — executable process

## Purpose
Convert the research constitution into a repeatable design workflow with explicit inputs, outputs, pass/fail gates and rollback triggers.

This document does not generate concepts. It governs how approved concepts become coherent, recognisable and production-ready garments.

# Process Rule

No stage may be skipped because the artwork looks promising.

A design advances only when the required output exists and the gate passes. Failure returns the work to the named earlier stage rather than being patched downstream.

---

# Stage 1 — Product Definition

## Required inputs
- garment category;
- canonical blank;
- fit block;
- fabric weight;
- garment colour;
- wash or surface treatment;
- intended print or embroidery method;
- target retail role.

## Required output
A one-page Product Definition Record.

## Pass gate
Pass only when the actual garment—not a generic mock-up—is defined.

## Rollback trigger
If the blank, fit or fabrication changes later, return to Stage 1 and revalidate all scale and placement decisions.

---

# Stage 2 — Collection Role Assignment

## Approved roles
- persistent core;
- seasonal support;
- statement graphic;
- hero product;
- capsule product;
- collaboration product.

## Required output
The selected role plus its function in the range.

## Pass gate
The garment must add a necessary function to the collection rather than merely being another design.

## Fail conditions
- duplicates the visual intensity of an existing product without adding range value;
- introduces a new language unsupported elsewhere in the range;
- relies on novelty alone;
- cannot be explained in one sentence.

---

# Stage 3 — Recognition Anchor Selection

## Required decision
Choose the recognition cues that will remain stable while seasonal artwork varies.

Possible anchors:
- locked wordmark or monogram;
- proprietary script;
- repeated micro-branding placement;
- stable layout relationship;
- garment silhouette;
- recurring frame or composition behaviour;
- controlled colour behaviour;
- material or trim signature.

## Pass gate
At least two recognition cues must survive removal of the main seasonal graphic.

## Test
**Logo-removal test:** remove the dominant logo. The garment should retain at least one recognisable structural cue.

## Rollback trigger
If recognition depends entirely on enlarging the logo, return to Stage 2 or Stage 3.

---

# Stage 4 — Layout Archetype Selection

Select one approved archetype before artwork development:

- A1 — Small front identifier / large back hero
- A2 — Front hero / small rear signature
- A3 — Front hero / clean back
- A4 — Clean or micro front / back hero
- A5 — Unequal front-and-back graphics
- A6 — Image / language split
- A7 — Multi-zone system
- A8 — Jumbo field

## Required output
Front, back, neck, hem and sleeve zone map. Every zone must be marked as:
- active graphic;
- permanent identity;
- intentional negative space.

## Pass gate
Every active zone must perform a distinct role. Equal duplication requires a written reason.

---

# Stage 5 — Graphic Archetype Selection

Select the dominant construction:
- isolated emblem;
- image-led hero;
- type-led hero;
- image/type lockup;
- poster or flyer composition;
- photograph or photo treatment;
- collage;
- symbolic mark;
- all-over or seam-aware field.

## Required output
A one-sentence statement of what the eye sees first and why.

## Pass gate
The graphic must have one dominant proposition. Multiple elements are allowed only when they form one visual argument.

## Fail conditions
- several unrelated focal points;
- image and text competing at equal weight;
- composition requires explanation before it can be read;
- archetype selected after the artwork was already built.

---

# Stage 6 — Composition Construction

## Mandatory hierarchy
- H1 — dominant read;
- H2 — supporting read;
- H3 — optional close-detail read.

H4 requires a specific functional reason.

## Mandatory tests
1. **Silhouette test** — reduce to solid black. The major shape must remain clear.
2. **Distance test** — H1 must read at several metres.
3. **Conversation test** — H2 must become legible at normal social distance.
4. **Close test** — H3 may reward inspection but cannot rescue H1.
5. **Blur test** — one dominant mass must remain.
6. **Thumbnail test** — the composition must retain its identity when small.

## Pass gate
The design has one dominant focal point, a controlled reading order and intentional negative space.

## Rollback trigger
If added details improve the close view but weaken the distance read, return to the start of Stage 6.

---

# Stage 7 — Typography Integration

## Functional voices
1. identity voice;
2. display voice;
3. information voice.

## Required output
Typography map identifying the role, type style, alignment and scale of every text element.

## Pass gate
Each type treatment performs a different function. Decorative variation alone does not justify another voice.

## Mandatory checks
- correct spelling and wording;
- legibility at final size;
- letter-spacing tested at actual output size;
- no fragile strokes that fail production;
- text hierarchy remains subordinate to the chosen H1 unless the design is type-led.

---

# Stage 8 — Production Translation

## Required decisions
- print or embroidery method;
- ink or thread count;
- underbase strategy;
- line-weight hierarchy;
- tonal method;
- halftone method where applicable;
- distress class;
- texture class;
- edge treatment;
- specialty modifier, if any;
- target print hand;
- seam, pocket, hood and stretch exclusions.

## Pass gate
The design works using its core production method before any specialty effect is added.

## Fail conditions
- puff, flock, rhinestone, crack, foil or high-density treatment is carrying the concept;
- details fall below the printer’s confirmed threshold;
- colour count exists without a visual role;
- POD substitution materially changes the artwork;
- the design was approved only as a screen image.

---

# Stage 9 — Garment Fit Validation

## Mandatory views
- straight-on flat garment;
- front worn view;
- back worn view;
- three-quarter worn view;
- seated or naturally folded view where relevant;
- hood-up and hood-down for hoodies;
- embroidery proof for caps.

## Pass gate
The intended hierarchy survives body curvature, drape, folds, pockets, seams and movement.

## Rollback trigger
If the dominant read disappears when worn, return to Stage 4 or Stage 6. Do not merely enlarge everything.

---

# Stage 10 — Brand Recognition Review

## Mandatory tests
### Logo-removal test
Recognition cannot depend only on a readable logo.

### Competitor-substitution test
Replace the brand mark with a competitor mark. If the design becomes equally plausible, the system lacks proprietary cues.

### Range-wall test
Place the garment beside the current range. It must belong without appearing duplicated.

### Asset-lock test
Permanent identity assets must retain approved proportions, spacing and construction.

## Pass gate
The design uses at least two stable recognition cues and does not borrow its entire visual logic from one external reference.

---

# Stage 11 — Collection Cohesion Review

## Required review
Assess the garment beside the complete proposed collection, not in isolation.

Check:
- role distribution;
- visual-intensity rhythm;
- repeated product families;
- colour depth;
- layout variety;
- graphic-family balance;
- category portability;
- duplication;
- over-reliance on one motif;
- hero/support relationship.

## Pass gate
The garment adds contrast or continuity where the range needs it.

## Fail conditions
- every item behaves like a hero;
- every item uses the same layout;
- no persistent core product exists;
- one seasonal treatment appears on everything;
- a capsule overwhelms the parent identity.

---

# Stage 12 — Production Readiness Gate

A design is production-ready only when all of the following exist:

- approved Product Definition Record;
- collection role;
- recognition anchors;
- layout archetype;
- graphic archetype;
- final front/back/secondary placements;
- final dimensions by garment size;
- separated production artwork;
- spot-colour or thread specification;
- underbase specification;
- distress and halftone specification;
- specialty-process specification;
- printer-confirmed technical limits;
- worn-view approval;
- range-wall approval;
- sample or strike-off review plan.

No missing item may be replaced with “printer to decide.”

---

# Decision Record Template

Every proposed garment must record:

1. Product name or working ID
2. Garment category
3. Canonical blank and colour
4. Collection role
5. Recognition anchors
6. Layout archetype
7. Graphic archetype
8. H1 / H2 / H3 hierarchy
9. Typography voices
10. Production method
11. Scale role by zone
12. Permanent identity placements
13. Intentional negative-space zones
14. Specialty modifiers
15. Known production risks
16. Failed tests and revisions
17. Final approval status

---

# Approval States

- **Draft** — concept exists; no constitutional review.
- **Structured** — product role, archetypes and recognition anchors selected.
- **Composed** — hierarchy and distance tests passed.
- **Production-defined** — method, separations and garment constraints resolved.
- **Range-approved** — recognition and collection gates passed.
- **Sample-ready** — complete production package issued.
- **Approved** — physical sample reviewed and accepted.
- **Rejected** — cannot pass without changing the core proposition.

---

# Rejection Rule

A weak concept must not be rescued through more typography, more distress, larger printing, specialty ink or additional surfaces.

When the dominant proposition fails, reject or redesign the proposition.

---

# Process Success Criterion

A designer unfamiliar with the original creative discussion should be able to use this framework and the governing documents to produce a garment that:

- belongs to the collection;
- is recognisable as part of the brand system;
- has clear visual hierarchy;
- is designed for its actual garment;
- survives production;
- can be reviewed through explicit pass/fail gates rather than taste alone.
