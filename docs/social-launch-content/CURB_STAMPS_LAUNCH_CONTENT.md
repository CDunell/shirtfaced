## 2. First 12 posts — one character a week is the calendar

The launch insight from the playbook: 12 authored characters = a 12-week introduction
calendar before anything new needs drawing. Each week gets one character post (C),
plus 1–2 supporting formats (R/S) from §3. Character post template:

> **Card 1:** the creature, big, on its accent colour.
> **Card 2:** name in Baloo type.
> **Card 3:** the canon line.
> **Caption:** "Meet [NAME]. [Canon line] One of twelve little guys — so far. The
> whole crew's at the link, and the curb list hears everything first."

Week order (proposal — front-load the strongest personalities): Squib, Dreg, Twig,
Grit, Plod, Claw, Snu, Bub, Murk, Grub, Lod, Blip.

Three worked examples:

- **Squib (week 1):** creature card trio. "Meet Squib. Waddles like it's got somewhere
  better to be. It doesn't. First of twelve little guys you'll be seeing a lot of.
  Join the curb — link in bio — and hear about the rest before anyone."

  your kid relates, we can't help you, but we can put it on a hoodie soon."
- **Twig (week 3):** "Meet Twig. Impossibly long. Allegedly a stick. Absolutely not a
  stick. The crew keeps growing — the curb list meets them all first."

## 3. Supporting formats (Reels/TikTok, repeatable)

**Script A — "Which little guy is your kid?"**
- 4 creatures, canon line each, parent VO: "Every kid on the curb is one of these.
  Mine's a Grit. First in line for everything." CTA: "Tag the Plod parent. They'll
  see it in a few days."

**Script B — "Drawing the little guys" (founder BTS)**
- Hook: "I draw small creatures for a living and my toughest critics are under ten."
- Beats: pencil roughs → the line art → the character on its accent colour.
- Close: "This is Snu. Sniffs everything first. Trust issues. Relatable."

**Script C — "The curb" (world-building)**
- Slow pan across the full crew lineup. VO: "Somewhere between your house and the
  park, there's a curb. These are the little guys who live on it." End: "Good people.
  Weird kids. Store opening soon."

**Script D — "Made to be handed down" (values, for parents)**
- Hook: "Kids clothes shouldn't die in one wash."
- Beats: screen-print close-up, fabric, the honest one-liner: "Screen printed, not
  vinyl. Made to be handed down, not landfilled."

**Script E — "Naming the next one" (participation)**
- Show a new unnamed creature. "Creature thirteen needs a name. One word, lowercase
  energy. The curb list votes on the shortlist." (Runs the playbook's vote mechanic.)

## 4. Waitlist + founding-drop sequence

Curb Stamps has a once-only asset: a first drop that has literally never sold anything.

- **Ongoing (all 12 weeks):** every post ends with the same single CTA — "Join the
  curb". No product links exist yet, and that's fine; say so plainly:
  "Store's being built. The crew's already here."
- **Founding members:** "First 100 on the curb list get first pick of the first drop
  ever, and their order number for keeps. There will only ever be one CS-1000."
  *(True — order refs are sequential from CS-1000 in the schema.)*
- **Store-open sequence (when POD + photography are real):**
  - T-14: "The store is real. Doors open [DATE]. Curb list walks in a day early."
  - T-1 (email): Subject: "You're in before the doors open" — "Tomorrow the curb
    opens to everyone. Today it's yours. First pick, first order numbers."
  - T-0: "The curb is open. Twelve little guys, tees, hoodies and caps. Made to be
    handed down."

**Welcome email (on waitlist signup)**
> Subject: You're on the curb
>
> Good people. Weird kids. You're in the right place. Here's the deal: the store's
> still being built, the crew is not. You'll meet a new little guy most weeks, you'll
> vote on names, and when the doors open you're in a day before everyone else.
> That's it. No spam. The curb doesn't do spam.
