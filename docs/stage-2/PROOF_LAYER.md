# shirtfaced — THE PROOF LAYER

**Status:** Draft. Not approved.
**Scope:** Website product truth and Instagram Stories — the layer that answers the
objection the World doesn't.
**Voice:** Storefront throughout. Per `BRAND_VOICE.md` §6, every surface in this
document is Storefront: dry, deadpan, factual, willing to take the piss out of the
brand and the customer in equal measure. **No Identity voice anywhere in here.** A
spec written in Identity voice reads try-hard, and a spec is the one place the
brand has to be believed rather than felt.
**Depends on:** `WORLD.md` (Product Photography Extraction), `CHANNEL_TRANSLATION.md`,
`CAMPAIGN_AND_DROP_SYSTEM.md` §4–§9, `WEARER_IS_THE_AUDIENCE.md`, `README.md` §3
and §10, `studio/worlds/world-01/STORY_ARC.md`.
**Date:** 14 August 2026.

Creates no canon. Edits nothing.

# 0. Why this exists, in dollars

The tee is **$59** and nets about **$24** with shipping charged. Two tees ship
free, so a two-unit order is $118 revenue, $48 gross, minus roughly $11 of parcel
cost — call it **$37 net, about 31%**.

Three things follow from that number, and they set everything below.

**One — returns are the biggest lever in the business.** A return costs the $24
that wasn't earned plus the handling to get the item back and inspected. Call the
handling $14 all in. That means one return wipes out the profit on roughly one and
a half sales.

| Return rate on 100 units | Net profit | Effective per unit |
|---|---|---|
| 10% | $2,020 | $22.44 |
| 25% | $1,450 | $19.33 |
| 35% | $1,070 | $16.46 |

Dropping returns from 25% to 10% is worth about **+39% net profit** at identical
sales volume. In apparel, returns are overwhelmingly a fit problem. So a good size
guide is worth more than a good ad, and it costs nothing to run twice.

**Two — a one-unit order cannot pay for acquisition.** At $24 net, any paid
channel needs a cost per acquisition under about $20 to work on a single tee,
which is not achievable cold in Australia. Paid only works at two-plus units. The
free-shipping-at-two threshold is therefore not a nice gesture, it is the business
model, and **the proof layer's job is to get people to two rather than to get them
to one.**

Somebody who trusts the fit buys two colours. Somebody who doesn't buys one and
maybe sends it back.

**Three — the World sells desire and cannot answer objections.** The ten-post
World 01 cycle contains zero product truth: no weight, no fabric, no measurement,
no price, no fit on a real body. That is correct for the feed and fatal on its
own. `CAMPAIGN_AND_DROP_SYSTEM.md` already has a Phase 5 for exactly this and the
cycle never reaches it.

**The World makes them want it. The proof layer makes them able to buy it.**
Different jobs, different surfaces, different voice, and they must never be
performed in the same asset.

# 1. What proof is, and what it is not

**Proof is not a second photographic practice.** `WORLD.md` is explicit: there is
one world, and a product photograph is a quieter, clearer fragment of it. Every
image in this layer is extracted from a World 01 scene that already exists. Nothing
here authorises a separate shoot, a white background, a studio light or a model
turning to show a garment.

Extraction changes framing and prominence. It relaxes nothing. No enclosure at any
prominence level, camera stays outside what it watches, everything we sell stays
blank, and the Success Test still applies at P3.

**Proof is allowed to be plain.** `WEARER_IS_THE_AUDIENCE.md` forbids explaining a
joke. It does not forbid stating a fact. A GSM figure is not an explanation of a
design; it is the answer to "is this worth fifty-nine dollars," which is a question
the brand is obliged to answer honestly and briefly.

The distinction that matters:

| Never | Always fine |
|---|---|
| Explaining what a graphic means | Saying what the garment weighs |
| A second line that translates the first | A chest measurement in centimetres |
| Copy defending why something is funny | "It'll fade before it cracks" |
| Brand philosophy as an instruction manual | "Thirty days. We won't ask why." |

---

# 2. The six questions

Everything in this document answers one of six things. If a proposed asset answers
none of them, it belongs in the World, not here.

1. **What is it?** Weight, fabric, cut.
2. **Will it fit me?** Sizing, garment measurements, who's wearing it in the photo.
3. **What does it look like on somebody who isn't a model?**
4. **Will it last?** Print method, wash behaviour, what fails first and when.
5. **When does it arrive and what does it cost?**
6. **Can I send it back?**

Question 2 is the margin. Questions 1 and 4 are the price justification. Questions
5 and 6 are the trust. Question 3 is the only one the World can help with, and it
already has.

# 3. Site — the Product Truth Block

One block, identical structure on every product page, always in the same order,
always in the same place. Consistency is itself a trust signal: a customer who
learns where the weight lives finds it instantly on the next product.

Sample copy below is written to be used, not to be paraphrased. Where a number is
unknown it is marked `[TBC]` and must not ship as a guess.

### 3.1 — What it is

> `[TBC]gsm` combed cotton, garment dyed.
>
> Heavy enough that it doesn't go see-through, which is the only weight test that
> actually matters.
>
> Boxy through the body. Sits at the hip. Ribbed collar that holds its shape.

Three lines. Weight, the thing the weight buys you, the cut. No adjectives doing
work a number could do.

### 3.2 — How it fits

The single highest-value module on the site. Four parts, in this order.

**Garment measurements, not body measurements.** A flat table, in centimetres, per
size: chest across the flat, body length from high point of shoulder, shoulder
width, sleeve length. Body measurements make people guess; garment measurements
let them check.

**The instruction that beats every size guide ever written:**

> Measure a shirt you already like flat across the chest and match it to the table.
> Works better than guessing your size, and you already own the shirt.

**Height and size on every photo.** Every image in the fit module carries a plain
caption: `183cm, wearing L`. Not a styling credit — a data point.

**One fit contrast per garment.** The same item on two visibly different bodies,
in the same light, from the same distance. This is the frame that stops the "will
it look like that on me" return before it happens.

### 3.3 — What's printed on it

> Screen printed, `[TBC]` colours.
>
> It'll fade before it cracks. That's the point of printing it this way and it's
> the reason it costs what it costs.

### 3.4 — Washing it

> Cold, inside out, line dry.
>
> Or don't. It'll survive either way, it just won't look as good in two years, and
> two years is the bit we're actually selling.

### 3.5 — Getting it

> Flat rate anywhere in Australia. Free on two or more, because two is the normal
> number.
>
> `[TBC]` business days metro, `[TBC]` regional. Tracking on everything.

### 3.6 — Sending it back

Already live and already right. Do not rewrite it.

> Thirty days. Unworn, unwashed, tags on. **We won't ask why.**
>
> Not because we're precious — we just can't resell it and won't pretend otherwise.

### 3.7 — Who made it

> `[TBC]`

This is the one field that cannot be written before the answer exists. The about
page already commits to this: *"No inventing an origin story we can't back up."*
Leave it out entirely rather than fill it with atmosphere.

**Scene 5 is the most valuable asset in the entire night** and it was written as a
character beat. A bloke taking a hoodie off and giving it to somebody two sizes
smaller, in one continuous piece of available light, is a fit demonstration that no
studio can buy and no customer will read as advertising. Extract it properly.

**On the P3 detail frames.** Each has a real scene, real light and a real reason
the camera is there — a knot being tied, a cuff being pulled over a hand, a garment
changing owner. None of them is a garment presented to camera. If a detail frame
can't be justified by an action already happening in the scene, it doesn't get made.

**One true-colour frame per garment is mandatory.** Nine of the ten scenes are lit
by sodium, fluorescent or festoon light, and none of them tells anyone what black
actually looks like. Scene 10 at 7:20am is the only flat-daylight frame in World 01
and every garment that can appear in it should. A customer who receives a different
black than the one they saw is a return, and returns are the whole argument in §0.

# 5. Stories — the rhythm

Stories sit outside the ten-post cycle per `README.md` §10. They are also the only
surface with a link sticker on it, which makes them the answer to the problem the
World 01 review kept hitting: **the site's best assets exist and nothing tells
anybody they're there.**

### 5.1 — Three card types, never mixed

| Type | Job | Voice | Frequency |
|---|---|---|---|
| **FACT** | Answers one of the six questions. One fact per card. | Storefront | 1–2 / week |
| **FRAGMENT** | A piece of the world. Outtake, ambient, detail, no product claim. | Identity | 1–2 / week |
| **ROUTE** | Points at one named thing on the site. Link sticker. | Storefront | 1 / week, minimum |

**The never-mix rule.** A card is one type. A fact wrapped in atmosphere reads as
an ad and gets tapped through; atmosphere wrapped around a fact wastes both. If a
card is doing two jobs it is two cards.

### 5.2 — FACT cards

One fact, plain type, held long enough to read. No music. No transition.

> `240gsm.`
> `Which means it doesn't go see-through. That's the whole test.`

> `Measure a shirt you already like, flat across the chest.`
> `Match it to the table. Better than guessing.`

> `Thirty days. Unworn, unwashed, tags on.`
> `We won't ask why.`

### 5.3 — ROUTE cards

The route card names a specific asset and links to it. It does not say "link in
bio," it does not say "shop now," and it never explains why the asset is good.

> `count them`
> — links to the full-resolution fixed-camera frame from scene 6

> `all five, full size`
> — links to the crossing gallery

> `the hoodie, on two people`
> — links to the fit module

Three words and a link. The curiosity is the copy. This is the only place in the
entire system where the brand asks for a click, and it asks once a week.

### 5.4 — Highlights, the permanent layer

Four, lowercase, plain, never seasonal. Highlights are the profile's own proof
layer and they persist, which pairs with the pinned row in `STORY_ARC.md` §4 —
pins hold the story, highlights hold the answers.

- **`what it is`** — weight, fabric, print, the P3 detail frames
- **`how it fits`** — measurements, the fit contrast, every height-and-size caption
- **`postage`** — shipping, timing, returns
- **`the night`** — world fragments, outtakes, the stuff that isn't for sale

A first-time visitor who taps the handle gets three pinned posts telling them what
this is, and four highlights telling them whether they can buy it. That is the
whole funnel, above the fold, permanently, with nothing asked of them.

# 6. What the proof layer never does

- Never explains a design, a graphic or a joke. `WEARER_IS_THE_AUDIENCE.md` holds
  everywhere, including here.
- Never uses Identity voice on a specification.
- Never shoots on white, in a studio, or under a light that isn't in the scene.
- Never exceeds P3, and never enters a car cabin, a lift or any small enclosure at
  any prominence.
- Never asks somebody to turn, rotate or present a garment. Front, back and side
  come from actions that were already happening.
- Never runs a countdown timer, a viewer counter or a scarcity claim that isn't
  operationally true. The about page already promised this in public.
- Never claims a fabric weight, an origin or a delivery window that hasn't been
  confirmed. Every `[TBC]` in §3 is a hard gate, not a placeholder to fill with
  something plausible.

# 7. Measurement

Track the proof layer against the thing it exists to move, which is not reach.

**Primary**

- **Units per order.** Target 2.0+. This is the whole business per §0.
- **Return rate, split by size.** A single size returning at double the others is a
  measurement error in the table, not a customer problem, and it is fixable in an
  afternoon.
- **Size-guide open rate**, and return rate for people who opened it versus didn't.

**Secondary**

- Story link taps per ROUTE card, by named asset — this also tells you which site
  asset is worth making next.
- Product-page dwell time before add-to-cart.
- Highlight views on `how it fits` versus `the night`. If `the night` wins by a
  lot, the account has an audience and not a customer base, which is useful to know
  early.

# 8. The hoodie number

Not set here. It needs the landed cost of the blank plus print at real order
quantity, and a competitive set the owner defines rather than one derived from a
design reference — `POSITIONING.md` §1 puts both outside this document.

What stands regardless of the number: order size is the business, a one-unit order
cannot pay for acquisition, and the hoodie should be priced to be the
average-order-value product rather than a second single-unit purchase.

# 9. Open, and not decided here

- Hoodie landed cost, and therefore the hoodie price.
- Whether returns are prepaid or customer-paid. It changes the §0 arithmetic
  materially and it changes the returns copy.
- Where the garments are made — §3.7 cannot ship empty forever.
- Whether the tee's gsm, the print colour count and the delivery windows are
  confirmed anywhere. Every one is a `[TBC]` above and every one is a promise the
  moment it's published.
- Whether a second free-shipping threshold at three units is worth testing, given
  that the entire margin case rests on order size.
