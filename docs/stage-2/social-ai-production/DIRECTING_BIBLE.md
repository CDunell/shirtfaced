# shirtfaced — AI Directing Bible

**Status:** ACTIVE production contract  
**Scope:** Camera, performance, lighting and editorial grammar for AI-generated campaign media

---

## 1. Governing principle

The directing style may change. The shirtfaced production grammar does not.

Recognisable film/director/music-video traditions are technique libraries, not identities to imitate. The system may borrow framing, blocking, pacing, camera behaviour or performance grammar while producing original scenes, characters, dialogue and compositions.

---

## 2. Campaign presentation mix

Default cycle target:

- 30% polished
- 40% documentary
- 20% deliberately rough
- 10% weird

These are presentation classes, not quality grades.

Every output still has to pass structural, continuity, brand and production review.

---

## 3. Camera vocabulary

The shot contract should be able to express:

- ECU / CU / MCU / MS / MLS / WS / EWS
- eye level / high / low / overhead / ground-level
- frontal / three-quarter / profile / rear / over-shoulder
- static / handheld / pan / tilt / push / pull / track / orbit / whip / snap zoom / digital zoom
- focal-length/FOV intent
- framing bias
- foreground obstruction
- depth/focus intent
- safe-crop requirements

These are intent labels. AI generation is judged by the perceptual result rather than whether a synthetic camera reproduced a physical lens mathematically.

---

## 4. Performance grammar

shirtfaced people normally behave as though the event matters more than the camera.

Default performance rules:

- no fashion posing unless the shot is explicitly a fashion/hero treatment
- no unexplained camera acknowledgement
- interaction beats isolated posing
- small awkward behaviours are useful
- characters can be funny without acting like they know the joke
- background people have plausible independent behaviour
- reactions matter as much as actions

A documentary shot that returns evenly spaced people facing camera is a directing failure even when technically clean.

---

## 5. Blocking grammar

For multi-character scenes, specify:

- who owns the action
- who reacts
- distance between people
- facing direction
- travel direction
- eyelines
- entrances/exits
- foreground/midground/background separation
- intentional occlusion

Avoid generic semicircle/group-photo blocking unless story requires it.

---

## 6. Product behaviour

Garments are wardrobe first and product second.

Across a campaign:

- some shots are hero-readable
- many shots are clearly present or partial
- some shots deliberately obscure the product
- some shots contain no product at all

Never require every person to turn artwork toward camera.

Hero coverage is planned deliberately rather than accidentally demanded from every generation.

---

## 7. Lighting grammar

Lighting follows location and story state before aesthetics.

Possible registers:

- harsh flash
- convenience-store fluorescent
- sodium-vapour street / carpark
- pub practicals
- dawn available light
- overcast suburban day
- controlled luxury-fashion light
- CCTV/phone exposure
- MiniDV/camcorder auto-exposure feel

Lighting continuity between adjacent shots matters more than making each isolated frame prettier.

---

## 8. Directing-language library

Useful technique families include:

- dialogue tension and chapter construction
- deadpan symmetry / locked frames
- accelerated character intros
- rhythmic inserts and match cuts
- controlled precision / restrained darkness
- observational aftermath
- rough youth documentary
- absurd premise played straight
- practical/handmade transition grammar
- anxious pursuit
- grounded everyday realism
- skate VHS / MiniDV / 2000s camcorder
- CCTV / surveillance
- local-current-affairs / news
- nature documentary
- luxury fashion film
- music-video grammar
- mockumentary
- infomercial / training-video grammar
- sports broadcast / reality TV

A campaign may combine these, but the choice must have a story/editorial job.

---

## 9. Shot purpose vocabulary

Every planned shot has at least one purpose:

- establish
- reveal
- action
- reaction
- escalate
- complicate
- hero product
- insert/detail
- evidence/found footage
- transition
- aftermath
- callback
- title-card source
- environmental plate

A shot with no plausible editorial purpose should not be generated merely because generation is available.

---

## 10. Motion rules

For video, specify the change over time.

A useful motion brief answers:

- what is true at first frame
- what moves
- what remains locked
- who/what crosses frame
- camera movement
- action timing/order
- what is true at last frame

“Cinematic movement” is not a shot direction.

---

## 11. Found-footage modes

Found/diegetic formats must have internally consistent capture logic.

Examples:

- CCTV: fixed viewpoint, plausible timestamp/framing, indifferent camera
- phone: hand-held behaviour, focus/exposure corrections, imperfect framing
- camcorder: operator behaviour, zoom/focus quirks, era-appropriate texture
- news/current affairs: coverage grammar, reporter/interview framing, graphics only when original and intentional

Do not paste a VHS filter over otherwise polished fashion coverage and call it documentary.

---

## 12. Aspect and platform planning

Source production should anticipate:

- 9:16 TikTok/Reels
- Instagram carousel stills
- possible 4:5 feed crops
- site/email reuse where useful

Safe crop is planned at shot level. Do not assume every landscape composition survives vertical cropping.

---

## 13. Audio intent

Where video uses audio, plan:

- production dialogue
- ambient sound
- silence
- music role
- diegetic sound source
- voiceover/narration where appropriate

Sound is part of directing, not an afterthought added when the picture is finished.

---

## 14. Originality constraint

Reference a technique, not a protected work.

Do not request recreation of:

- exact scenes
- exact dialogue
- distinctive protected characters
- exact branded compositions
- another brand's visual identity

The system must be able to explain the directing intent without relying solely on a director/film name.
