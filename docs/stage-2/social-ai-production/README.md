# shirtfaced — AI-Native Social Production System

**Status:** ACTIVE — Stage 2 production authority  
**Scope:** Instagram + TikTok campaign content development and AI production  
**Upstream of:** SocialBench, social posting, derivative review and publishing systems  
**Production assumption:** 100% AI-generated visual production unless explicitly overridden

---

## 1. Core production rule

shirtfaced social content is **not** based on filming a real event and cutting the captured footage into posts.

The production itself is AI-native.

A "Friday night shoot" means authoring a complete synthetic world and generating the required shots, stills and sequences from that world. If a campaign calls for 60–90 minutes of usable source material, that means generating and directing 60–90 minutes of AI video and companion still imagery — not recording 60–90 minutes of real-world footage.

This changes the production problem from capture to **previsualised authorship**.

Every campaign must therefore be developed before generation at the level of:

- story and premise
- beginning / escalation / payoff / aftermath
- characters and persistent character identity
- wardrobe and exact garment allocation
- locations and spatial continuity
- time of night / day and lighting progression
- props and continuity objects
- scene order
- beat structure
- shot list
- camera position and angle
- focal length / field-of-view intent
- camera movement
- blocking and eyelines
- foreground / midground / background action
- garment visibility and placement
- product exposure without turning the film into a catalogue
- still-photo opportunities
- alternate coverage
- transitions and edit logic
- sound / dialogue / ambience / music intent
- platform derivatives
- continuity constraints between independently generated AI shots

The generation model is the camera department, locations department, casting department, lighting department, wardrobe department and VFX department. The production system must supply those departments with deterministic instructions.

## 2. The shirtfaced social loop — v1

### Cycle length

**14 days**

### Primary publishing volume

**10 primary posts per platform per cycle**  
Approximately **5 feed posts per week**.

### Instagram

**7 motion / 3 photographic**

Photography should normally be a carousel / photo story rather than a filler single image.

### TikTok

**8 motion / 2 photographic**

One Instagram photo-story slot may be translated into TikTok-native motion, slideshow or reconstructed-camera-roll treatment rather than duplicated mechanically.

### Canonical sequence

1. HERO FILM — major directed piece / strongest concept
2. PHOTO STORY — stills from the same world
3. MICRO FILM — 6–12 second moment
4. CHARACTER FILM — person, behaviour, gag or interaction
5. PHOTO STORY — 4–8 image narrative sequence
6. HERO FILM — second major directed treatment
7. FOUND FILM — VHS / CCTV / phone / current-affairs / camcorder / other diegetic format
8. PHOTO STORY — documentary, flash, detail or aftermath imagery
9. MICRO FILM — short highly shareable scene
10. CLOSER FILM — callback, reveal, aftermath or final beat

Canonical rhythm:

**FILM → PHOTOS → FILM → FILM → PHOTOS → FILM → FILM → PHOTOS → FILM → FILM**

This is deliberate. Photography is not inserted merely to separate videos.

## 3. Why photography remains in the system

The social system is motion-led but not motion-only.

Photography has different jobs from video:

- allow the garment to be inspected
- create slower visual punctuation in the feed
- document details and aftermath that do not need motion
- build swipe-based micro narratives
- provide premium fashion imagery without changing the brand into a fashion catalogue
- create reusable campaign assets for web, email, paid media and product presentation

A photographic post must do at least one of the following:

1. tell a micro-story
2. reveal information
3. document evidence of the world
4. strengthen the shirtfaced universe
5. make the garment desirable

Ordinary single-image filler is not a scheduled content category. Exceptional hero images may be used as punctuation when they genuinely deserve a standalone post.

## 4. Production-language ratio

Across a cycle, target the following presentation mix:

- **30% polished** — deliberate fashion-film / cinematic execution
- **40% documentary** — observational, available-light-feeling, lived-in execution
- **20% deliberately rough** — phone, MiniDV, VHS, CCTV, cheap flash, bad zoom, imperfect framing
- **10% weird** — formally strange, surreal, unexpected or difficult to categorise

These ratios describe **presentation language**, not technical quality.

AI outputs must still meet the quality threshold required for publishing.

The collision between polished, documentary and deliberately low-status image languages is part of the shirtfaced identity. Uniformly "cinematic" content is specifically not the goal.

## 5. Directing-language library

The system may borrow filmmaking grammar from recognisable traditions and directors, but must not imitate protected characters, exact scenes, dialogue, branding or copyrighted compositions.

Examples of useful grammars include:

- Tarantino-style tension, dialogue blocking and chapter-like construction
- Wes Anderson-style symmetry, deadpan blocking and locked-off frames
- Guy Ritchie-style character intros, snap movement and accelerated exposition
- Edgar Wright-style inserts, match cuts and rhythmic mundane actions
- Fincher-style precision, controlled darkness and restrained movement
- Sofia Coppola-style observational aftermath and emotional distance
- Harmony Korine-style rough youth-documentary energy
- Spike Jonze-style absurd premises played straight
- Michel Gondry-style practical visual tricks and handmade transitions
- Safdie-style pursuit, anxiety, overlapping action and street urgency
- Sean Baker-style grounded everyday locations and naturalistic behaviour
- skate-VHS / MiniDV / 2000s camcorder grammar
- CCTV / surveillance grammar
- local-current-affairs / news grammar
- nature-documentary grammar
- luxury-fashion-film grammar
- music-video grammar
- mockumentary grammar
- infomercial / corporate-training / sports-broadcast / reality-TV grammar

These are **tools**, not the shirtfaced identity by themselves.

The directing language may change between posts and campaigns. The shirtfaced world, humour, editorial rhythm, product behaviour, casting logic and recurring visual rules create the continuity.

## 6. AI campaign development hierarchy

Every cycle must be built in this order.

### Level 1 — Campaign premise

Define:

- what happened
- why anyone should care
- where it happens
- who is involved
- what changes from the beginning to the end
- where the joke / tension / emotional hook lies
- which garments exist naturally inside the event

The premise must work even if all shirtfaced logos are temporarily removed.

### Level 2 — Story architecture

Develop the complete event, not ten disconnected posts.

Minimum story phases:

1. setup
2. commitment
3. escalation
4. complication
5. peak
6. aftermath

The ten-post loop is then cut from or authored around this larger event.

### Level 3 — Scene architecture

Each scene receives:

- scene ID
- story purpose
- location
- time
- lighting state
- participating characters
- wardrobe / garment state
- props
- entrances and exits
- action beats
- dialogue / sound if applicable
- continuity-in state
- continuity-out state
- candidate post(s)

### Level 4 — Shot architecture

Each shot receives a deterministic shot specification.

Required fields:

- shot ID
- scene ID
- intended duration
- aspect ratio / safe crop plan
- shot size: ECU / CU / MCU / MS / MLS / WS / EWS etc.
- camera height
- horizontal camera position
- camera angle
- lens / field-of-view intent
- camera movement
- subject blocking
- subject direction of travel
- eyeline
- foreground elements
- background action
- focus behaviour / depth intent
- lighting direction and practicals
- garment visible
- garment side visible: front / back / left chest / sleeve etc.
- garment scale in frame
- logo / graphic legibility requirement
- prop continuity
- character continuity requirements
- generation prompt
- negative constraints
- first-frame / last-frame continuity anchors where required
- intended edit-in
- intended edit-out
- still-frame extraction potential

### Level 5 — Generation batches

Shots should be generated in continuity-aware batches rather than as unrelated prompts.

Batch examples:

- same character + same garment + same location + same lighting
- same room from multiple camera positions
- sequential actions requiring matching first / last frames
- clean still-image companion set for a completed scene

### Level 6 — Edit and derivatives

Only after the source scene has continuity do we create platform derivatives.

Derivatives may alter:

- duration
- opening shot
- text overlay
- crop
- pacing
- sound treatment
- caption
- ending

They must not accidentally invent contradictory story facts.

## 7. Garment placement is a directing decision

Products are not pasted into completed stories as an afterthought.

Wardrobe allocation must be decided during story development.

For every recurring character define:

- garment SKU / design
- garment colour
- size / silhouette
- front treatment
- rear treatment
- layer state
- whether sleeves are rolled / garment tucked / jacket covering it etc.
- continuity changes allowed during the story

For every shot define whether the garment should be:

- hero-readable
- clearly present but incidental
- partially visible
- atmospheric only
- deliberately absent

Not every shot should display a readable graphic. Constant product-facing poses destroy the documentary illusion.

The cycle should contain enough intentional hero coverage to sell the product while allowing most of the world to behave as though the camera is documenting people rather than photographing stock.

## 8. AI continuity is a first-class production constraint

Because shots are generated rather than photographed, continuity cannot be assumed.

Each campaign must retain a **continuity bible** containing at minimum:

- character reference images
- face / hair / body / age descriptors
- wardrobe references
- garment artwork references
- location references
- floorplan / spatial relationships where useful
- prop references
- vehicle references
- lighting-state references
- time progression
- scene-to-scene dirt / damage / wetness / costume changes
- locked visual details that must never drift

Continuity failures are production failures, not harmless generative variation.

Examples:

- shirt artwork changes between angles
- tattoo appears / disappears
- car model changes
- room geometry changes
- drink container changes shape
- weather changes between adjacent shots
- character handedness reverses
- rear graphic appears on the front
- object locations break screen direction

These require rejection or regeneration.

## 9. AI source-material model

Do not think in terms of "one finished Reel per prompt".

The preferred model is to generate a **campaign source library**.

A mature campaign may contain:

- hero narrative sequences
- alternate angles
- reaction shots
- inserts
- establishing shots
- transition material
- environmental plates
- close product detail
- phone-video variants
- surveillance variants
- camcorder variants
- portrait stills
- landscape stills
- flash stills
- clean garment visibility frames
- aftermath images
- unused story beats retained for future derivatives
- ambient audio and dialogue plans

A nominal 60–90 minute AI production therefore means a deliberately authored body of generated source footage of that scale where justified — not a claim that 60–90 minutes were physically filmed.

The system should generate only material with a plausible editorial role. Cheap AI generation is not a reason to create undirected sludge.

## 10. Stories are outside the ten-post count

Instagram Stories are connective tissue rather than primary-cycle units.

They may reuse or expose:

- alternate stills
- cut scenes
- character details
- comments / replies
- polls
- fake evidence
- sightings
- outtakes
- garment detail
- countdowns
- launch fragments
- customer content once available

Stories should feel adjacent to the campaign rather than like another mandatory ten-item production schedule.

## 12. Measurement rule

The 10-post / 14-day loop is **v1**, not religious doctrine.

Run at least **3 complete cycles / 30 primary posts** before changing the structural ratio unless there is an obvious production or platform failure.

Measure separately:

- discovery / non-follower reach
- completion / watch behaviour
- shares
- saves
- comments
- profile visits
- follows
- product clicks
- eventual conversion where attribution is available
- performance by directing language
- performance by narrative function
- performance by duration
- performance by garment visibility
- performance by polished / documentary / rough / weird presentation class

Optimisation should modify the formula using shirtfaced evidence rather than blindly chasing generic social-media benchmarks.

---

## 13. Governing principle

**The directing style changes. The shirtfaced production grammar does not.**

The goal is not to make AI videos that look impressive in isolation.

The goal is to create a persistent, recognisable shirtfaced world that can produce years of stories, films, photographs, characters, products and recurring jokes through a controlled AI production system.
