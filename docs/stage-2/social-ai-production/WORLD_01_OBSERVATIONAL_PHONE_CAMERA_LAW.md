# SHIRTFACED — WORLD 01 OBSERVATIONAL PHONE CAMERA LAW

**Status:** ACTIVE production law  
**Scope:** World 01 scene coverage and motion handoff, with W01-P28 as the first implementation scene.

## Purpose

World 01 is not photographed like staged cinema, panoramic event coverage or a camera crew collecting ideal angles.

The governing camera model is:

> **A real person is physically inside the event holding a phone vertically and recording what they can actually see from where they happen to be standing.**

The scene exists independently of that camera. The photographer does not control the room, crowd, characters, action or available sightlines. They can point the phone, turn/pan, change phone zoom and physically move to another position. Nothing else in the world rearranges itself to help the photograph.

## 1. Creative hierarchy

World 01 coverage follows:

`WORLD / EVENT → DISTRIBUTED HUMAN ACTIVITY → OBSERVER POSITION → AVAILABLE SIGHTLINE → VERTICAL PHONE FRAMING → OBSERVED INCIDENT / CHARACTER → MOTION THROUGH TIME`

The retired tendency is:

`scene → shot list → cinematic composition → character`

Named characters remain inhabitants of the world. They are not automatically photographic subjects.

## 2. The observer is physically real

Every generated observation must imply a plausible human photographer. The system must be able to answer: **where is the person holding this phone standing?**

That position determines what can be photographed.

The photographer:

- stands at plausible human chest/eye height;
- occupies physical space inside the environment;
- cannot see through people or objects;
- cannot float above a crowd;
- cannot instantly cross the room;
- cannot obtain a wider view than the position permits;
- cannot make surrounding people clear a sightline;
- cannot create protected foreground/background space around a named subject.

If a proposed observation cannot be explained from a plausible observer position, it is not valid World 01 coverage.

## 3. Vertical phone is the native observation

World 01 social observations are conceived natively through a **9:16 vertical phone viewfinder**.

The landscape contact-sheet canvas may remain a technical transport/layout device where required to preserve a reliable 3×3 sheet. That does not make the imagined camera landscape. Each panel should still be composed as the restricted sightline a vertically held phone would capture.

The narrow field of view is part of the aesthetic:

- incomplete information;
- limited peripheral context;
- subjects entering/leaving frame;
- partial bodies;
- clipped limbs;
- foreground obstruction;
- people disappearing behind other people;
- limited ability to establish the whole environment.

The camera should frequently show less.

## 4. Occlusion is evidence of being there

Foreground obstruction is not a defect. It is one of the strongest signals that the camera exists physically inside the event.

Appropriate obstruction includes shoulders, backs of heads, raised arms, pints, phones, hair, clothing, furniture, door frames and people crossing close to lens.

These may temporarily obscure important characters. A named character does not need to remain completely visible.

Prefer **credible sightline with interference** over **complete subject visibility**.

## 5. Camera movement is physically constrained

From one observer position the photographer can:

- **turn/pan** toward another visible part of the event;
- **change phone zoom** without changing physical perspective;
- absorb small body sway, crowd bumps, delayed reframing and imperfect corrections.

A significant perspective change requires the photographer to physically walk. Moving several metres through a packed room is therefore a **new observer position**, not a magical camera move.

World 01 does not use unmotivated crane, aerial, overhead, dolly, orbit, impossible push-through or stabilised commercial-camera movement.

## 6. Coverage generation becomes sightline discovery

Nano scene coverage is not a request for nine cinematic angles.

It is a request to discover plausible vertical phone observations available to real people physically located within the approved world.

A useful scene coverage sheet may use only two or three observer positions. Several panels can come from the same observer by changing viewing direction, phone zoom or the moment caught through a temporary gap.

This creates stronger continuity than inventing a new ideal camera location for every panel.

## 7. Coverage does not attempt completeness

A World 01 contact sheet does not need a compulsory establishing wide, medium, close-up, reverse, hero shot or overhead simply because those are normal film-coverage categories.

Useful observations are accidental and physically available, for example:

- a character visible between two foreground patrons;
- a clearer glimpse when somebody moves;
- somebody near the frame edge while the phone is aimed elsewhere;
- a stage seen beyond raised arms and heads;
- an object detail that happens to be visible from the same position;
- an imperfect return toward an earlier observation.

The edit assembles fragments of experience. The camera does not need to explain the whole room.

## 8. Character identity remains subordinate

Identity references remain necessary where identity must resolve accurately, but supplying one does not mean "make this person the subject".

It means: **if this person is visible from the observation, this is who they are.**

Authority remains:

1. owner-approved narrative/scene canon;
2. approved world/scene master;
3. observer position and physically available sightline;
4. scene activity;
5. selected observation;
6. minimal identity references required by that observation.

Character references never grant permission to relocate a person, clear the crowd or rebuild the world around a cleaner identity view.

## 9. Nano responsibility

Nano owns **world-preserving observational composition**.

It establishes:

- observer position;
- vertical sightline;
- foreground obstruction;
- depth and scale;
- room geography;
- visible identity where required;
- plausible phone framing.

Nano solves these spatial questions before Veo. Veo is not asked to manufacture coverage through motion.

## 10. Veo responsibility

Veo owns **what changes through time inside an already valid observation**.

For image-to-video, the approved Nano frame already owns appearance, identity, environment, lighting, composition, camera position and sightline.

The positive Veo prompt should therefore stay lean and additive, concentrating on motion. Avoid repeatedly redescribing the world or named subject.

## 11. W01-P28 application

The pub is packed. A phone inside that crowd should not routinely produce one perfect frame containing the full pool table, all of Damo, stool, pint, band, Emma, Brock, a huge crowd and room architecture.

That is staged/panoramic behaviour and should be rejected.

Instead:

- **Pool-table observer:** a person in the crowd near the pool-table region can catch Damo through shifting foreground bodies. The same observer may produce a wider glimpse and a tighter zoom without moving to an impossible position.
- **Bar-side observer:** a person nearer the bar can catch Emma/Brock within the crowd, partially occluded and continuing their own night.
- **Stage sightline observer:** a person at crowd level can see the band only through heads, shoulders, raised arms and drinks.

A return to Damo should feel like somebody pointing the phone back toward something still happening nearby, not like a reset wide shot.

## 12. Coverage validation

Reject a panel if:

- the viewpoint is elevated without physical justification;
- the field of view behaves like a panoramic/cinematic camera;
- the crowd conveniently clears around the intended subject;
- a named character receives protected hero space;
- composition resembles a staged hero shot;
- the camera position is physically impossible;
- room geography is reinvented;
- the observation shows implausibly complete information;
- foreground crowd ceases to affect sightlines.

Do **not** reject merely because:

- a character is partly hidden;
- foreground bodies cross them;
- framing is imperfect;
- action occurs near an edge;
- only part of the room is visible;
- somebody temporarily disappears behind another person.

Those can be evidence that the system is working.

## Production principle

The desired result is not **the best view of what happened**.

It is:

> **a believable view somebody who was there could have captured.**

W01-P28 is the first production scene migrated to this law. Once proven, the same observer/sightline architecture applies across World 01.
