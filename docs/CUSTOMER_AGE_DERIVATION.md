## 0. Why this document is built this way

`CHARACTERS.md` §6 records that ages, occupations, personalities and relationships
were *"supplied by the owner in session, 10 August 2026, drawn directly from their
own family and friends rather than invented."*

That makes the cast a real Australian social group rather than a persona exercise,
which makes it the best evidence in the repo about who this brand is for — better
than any comparable brand, better than any platform statistic, and better than the
unratified "older Australian audience" line in `04-corteiz-mechanics-and-shirtfaced-translation.md`
§3, which cites nothing.

`POSITIONING.md` §1 forbids inferring market tier, price point, distribution or
competitive set from that document. It says nothing about the character bible,
which is a separate source with its own evidence.

### Confidence key

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **STATED** | Written in `CHARACTERS.md`. Quoted. |
| **STRONG** | Derived from a stated fact by a short chain inside canon. |
| **WEAK** | Derived using a general assumption about how people usually are. Labelled as such and usable only in aggregate. |
| **NONE** | Not derivable. Left blank. Do not fill. |

A population median is a prior, not evidence about an individual. Where one is
used below it is marked and it never upgrades a WEAK to a STRONG.

## 2. The derivation

### 2.1 — The school-friend chain pins the middle

Three quoted lines interlock:

- Sophie — *"School friend of Emma's and Charlotte's."*
- Charlotte — *"25, solicitor. School friend of Emma's and Sophie's."*
- Emma — *"Gary's eldest, nursing graduate."*

Charlotte's age is stated. School friends sit in the same school year band. So:

| Character | Derived | Working | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sophie** | 24–26 | School friend of a stated 25-year-old | STRONG |
| **Emma** | 24–26 | School friend of a stated 25-year-old | STRONG |

Emma being a *nursing graduate* corroborates rather than drives this — a three-year
Australian nursing degree from school-leaver entry puts a graduate at 20–21
minimum, which is consistent with 24–26 and does not narrow it. The school chain is
the load-bearing derivation; the degree is a floor check.

This is the single most useful thing in the document. **Three named characters —
Emma, Sophie, Charlotte — are pinned to 24–26 by one stated number.**

### 2.4 — The older end

This is what the derivation is for, and it is the weakest-evidenced part of the
cast, so the working matters most here.

**Gary.** Stated: *"Brother to Damo. Father to Emma and Grace."* Emma is *"Gary's
eldest."*

Gary's age = Emma's age + Gary's age when Emma was born. Emma is 24–26 (§2.1). The
second term is not in canon.

For scale only — and this is a population prior, not evidence about Gary — the ABS
puts the 2024 median age of Australian fathers at **33.9 years across all births**,
rising slowly over the past decade. Median age at *first* birth is lower than the
all-births median, and the relevant year is around 2000–2002, when the median was
lower again. That points to somewhere around thirty as a central figure and gives:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Plausible range** | **45–60** |
| **Central estimate** | **mid-50s** |
| **Confidence** | **WEAK** — the range is derived, the centre is a population prior |

Two further stated facts constrain the picture without giving a number. Emma and
Grace are *half-sisters*, so Gary has children by two relationships with roughly
six or seven years between the daughters. And both daughters are adults.

**This contradicts `STORY_ARC.md`, which writes Gary as late forties and flags the
inference.** Late forties is at the young edge of the derivable range and should be
corrected or confirmed by the owner rather than left as my call.

**Damo.** Stated: *"Gary's younger brother. SK's other half."* Younger than Gary is
an ordering, not an age, and there is no independent anchor. Range follows Gary's
minus an ordinary sibling gap. **Confidence WEAK, and dependent on a WEAK.** Do not
treat as evidence.

**SK.** Stated: *"Damo's partner. Former New York/London L'Oréal marketing
executive... has run campaigns bigger than this one."*

This is the strongest occupational age signal in the cast. Reaching executive level
in a major multinational, holding *two* international postings, and now being a
*former* one, is a career arc with a floor — degree, years to seniority, two
relocations, a return. That does not happen in a decade.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Floor** | early 40s |
| **Confidence** | **STRONG for the floor, NONE for a ceiling** |

SK is the only older-end character whose age band comes from something written down
rather than from a relationship to somebody else's number.

`04-corteiz-mechanics-and-shirtfaced-translation.md` §3 asserts *"The working
SHIRTFACED audience is older, more time-poor and less likely to tolerate friction."*

**Two — my own earlier claim was wrong.** I said the cast skewed 38–42 and sat
above the Australian platform peak. That was based on the five people I chose for

**Three — World 01 is cast away from the cast's own centre.** `STORY_ARC.md` uses
Gary, Damo, SK, Brock and Grace: the entire older end, one unplaceable, and one
student. **It contains nobody from the 24–30 band**, which is the largest group in
canon and the platform's biggest bracket. Emma, Sophie, Charlotte, Mitch and Casey

That may still be the right call — the five in World 01 were chosen because
five-becomes-four needed a countable family unit and because holding cast back for
World 02 was an instruction. But it was a story decision with an audience
consequence that nobody costed, and it should be a decision rather than a
by-product. It is the single most consequential thing in this document.

## 5. The older end, specifically

The question was the older end, and the honest answer is that **its ages are the
worst-evidenced thing in the cast while its buying behaviour is the best-evidenced.**
Canon barely constrains how old these three are and describes exactly how they
behave.

Three stated lines do more work than any age would.

**SK** — *"has run campaigns bigger than this one — which either makes her the
best-qualified person in the group to say an idea is bad, or the least impressed by
any of it."*

Canon is telling you the older end is marketing-immune. A former L'Oréal executive
recognises every mechanic in the drop system on sight. She is not moved by a
countdown, a scarcity claim or a brand story. She is moved by the garment being
good and the facts being true — which is precisely what `PROOF_LAYER.md` was built
to supply, and it means the proof layer matters **more** at the older end, not less.

**Gary** — *"Smart, quiet, sarcastic as fuck, irreverent... The dry centre everyone
else orbits."* Plus the stated life-stage facts: two adult daughters, children by
two relationships. Nothing about money is stated and nothing should be invented.
What is stated is a person who is not sold to and decides for himself, and who
other people arrange themselves around — which is a distribution property, not a
purchase property.

**Damo** — *"The chaos agent... Exhausting, beloved, always the first name in the
retelling."* Highest occasion frequency in the cast. No money fact stated.

**The only money fact in the entire character bible is about Brock** —
*"Loud, generous, financially unreliable, otherwise completely reliable."* It is not
about the older end at all, and there is no basis in canon for the assumption that
older equals wealthier here.

So the defensible older-end statement is about **life stage and disposition, not
age or income**: adult children, completed senior career, immune to marketing, buys
on product truth. That is a customer description you can act on and every clause of
it is quoted.

## 8.3a — The positioning principle this settles

**Owner, 14 August 2026:** *"The older demographic might not see their current self
but instantly recognises their younger self."*

This resolves a tension that ran through four rounds of this analysis and that I
kept mis-framing as a mismatch — the cast centres at 25–26, the money sits older,
therefore the casting must be wrong.

It isn't, because **recognition is retrospective, not reflective.** A 43-year-old
looking at four people on a kerb at 2:35am is not looking for himself as he is now.
He is looking at a night he has had, and the further away it is the more it is
worth. The younger viewer sees a night they are about to have. Both are buying the
same garment for opposite reasons and neither needs a character their own age in
frame.

The repo already had half of this and nobody joined it up.
`04-corteiz-mechanics-and-shirtfaced-translation.md` §3 records the internal
shorthand *"If you have been there, you know"* — that is this principle, written
down as strategy language and never connected to casting.

Three consequences.

**One — casting young is correct for both ends of the market**, and the question of
whether to cast older to match the money is closed. It should not be reopened.

**Two — it kills the mirror-casting instinct permanently.** Adding a 55-year-old to
reach 55-year-olds would be a category error under this principle: it gives the
older viewer their current self, which is the one thing they are not looking for.

**Three — it forced the World 01 recast, and that is now done.** The first draft of
`STORY_ARC.md` ran Gary, Damo, SK, Brock and Grace, which contained nobody from the
25–27 band. Under the principle above that band is not merely the biggest group in
canon and the platform's biggest bracket — **it is the specific thing the older
customer recognises himself in** — so a cast whose youngest adult was 29 was asking
a 43-year-old to recognise a younger self who wasn't in the frame.

Grace was swapped for Emma and SK for Casey. The mechanic is untouched: still five,
still a family with a friend web attached, still one father and one of his
daughters. The night now runs **25, 26, 29, 38, 43**, median 26, with three of the
five in the recognition band. Grace and SK are both worth more whole than
half-spent and are held for World 02.

## 8.4 — Brock at 29

Canon gives Brock no age and one link: *"Mitch's mate."* The owner has set the Emma
cohort at 25–27 and Mitch at 29 as that cohort's ceiling, so Brock sits on the
ceiling at **29** — as old as he can be and still be Mitch's mate.

That is also where his stated character works hardest. *"Loud, generous, financially
unreliable, otherwise completely reliable"* at 25 is an ordinary skint
twenty-something and carries nothing; at 29 it is a trait, and the declined card in
scene 5 of `STORY_ARC.md` is sharp. Settled.
