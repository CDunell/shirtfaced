Every printed t-shirt cracks eventually — the real question worth asking isn't whether it'll happen, but whether it took three washes or three hundred. That gap is almost entirely down to three things: the ink, the heat, and the fabric moving underneath it.

Ink cracks because it's a rigid-ish layer sitting on top of fabric that's constantly flexing, stretching and folding underneath it — every time you move, sit down, or the shirt goes through a wash cycle, the fabric moves more than the ink layer wants to. Plastisol ink, the standard for screen printing, is genuinely flexible and built to move with fabric far longer than most alternatives — which is a real part of why properly screen-printed designs typically outlast DTG ones printed on the same garment.

Heat is the accelerant, not the root cause, and it's the single biggest thing within your own control. A hot wash or a tumble dryer softens the ink binder holding everything together, which lets the surface start micro-cracking far earlier than cold care would allow. This is the actual mechanism behind "cold wash, hang dry" as care advice — it's not an arbitrary rule, it's specifically slowing down the one process that ages a print fastest.

Fabric movement matters more than people expect too. A tight, fitted tee flexes constantly across the exact areas a large chest or back print usually sits — cracking shows up there first for a straightforward mechanical reason, not bad luck. A slightly looser, boxier cut moves less aggressively against the body in the same spots, which is a small but real part of why looser-cut tees tend to keep a large print looking sharp for longer.

Print thickness plays a role too, and it cuts both ways. Thicker ink survives more wear before failing outright — genuinely more durable — but it also cracks more visibly once it does start to go, because there's simply more rigid material to fracture. A thin, well-applied print can actually look "worn in" more gracefully even while technically wearing out at a similar underlying rate.

None of this means cracking is always fine to shrug off — premature cracking, showing up in the first handful of washes, is a real sign of a badly cured or poorly applied print, not just normal ageing. But cracking that shows up gradually after genuinely heavy, long-term wear isn't a defect. It's ink and fabric doing exactly what physics says they'll do eventually — the only real lever you've got is how long "eventually" takes, and that's decided by wash habits more than anything printed on the [Take Your Chances Tee](/products/take-your-chances-tee) or the [Love Fast Die Last Tee](/products/love-fast-die-last-tee) itself.
