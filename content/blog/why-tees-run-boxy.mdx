A boxy, slightly oversized cut isn't a brand getting its sizing wrong — it's a deliberate decision with real reasoning behind it, and understanding that reasoning actually helps you buy the right size instead of guessing.

The core of it is what a boxy fit does for a large graphic. A fitted tee follows the shape of the body, which means any print on it stretches and distorts slightly across the chest and stomach depending on how someone's standing. A boxier cut hangs straighter off the shoulders instead of clinging to the torso, which keeps a large design reading flat and undistorted — exactly what you want when the print is doing most of the work.

The dropped shoulder seam is doing a specific, related job. Instead of the seam sitting right at the edge of the shoulder — where a fitted tee puts it — it sits further down the arm. That does two things: it widens the visual silhouette across the shoulders, and it gives a chest or full-back print more flat, undistorted real estate to actually live on.

There's a comfort argument too, and it's not just marketing — a boxier cut simply moves with the body more than a fitted one does, which matters more the heavier the fabric gets. A 220gsm tee cut fitted can feel restrictive in a way the same fabric cut boxy doesn't, purely because there's more room for the heavier material to move rather than pull.

None of this means "boxy" is the only correct choice, or that a fitted tee is wrong — it's a genuine style decision with real tradeoffs either way, not an objective upgrade. What it does mean is that a boxy cut showing up consistently across a brand's whole range usually isn't an accident or an inconsistency — it's a specific choice about how the graphic and the garment are meant to work together.

Practically, that changes how you should size it. Where a fitted brand wants you matching your usual measurements closely, a boxy-cut brand is built to be worn with breathing room already factored in — sizing to your normal measurement on a boxy cut usually reads oversized on purpose, not by mistake, and sizing down is the move if you actually want something closer to fitted.
