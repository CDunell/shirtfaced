Depends which part of "made" you're actually asking about, and most brands that answer this question quickly are quietly picking whichever half makes the better headline.

Growing the cotton and weaving it into fabric — genuinely rare here at any real commercial scale. Australia doesn't have much of a domestic textile-milling industry left; most blank tees sold here, regardless of brand, start life as fabric woven somewhere in Asia or the Pacific, cut and sewn into a garment there too, then shipped in as a finished blank. That's true of the big global names and it's true of most small independent brands, whether they say so loudly or not.

What genuinely can happen here, and does: the part that turns a blank into an actual design. Screen printing, DTG, embroidery — the physical act of putting a graphic on a shirt is real, skilled, local work, done in actual print shops in actual Australian cities, and it's the part of the process a small brand has the most direct control over. "Designed in Australia, printed here" is an honest claim in a way "made in Australia" often isn't, once you trace the fabric back far enough.

So the honest answer to "are any t-shirts made in Australia" is: the growing and weaving, almost never; the cutting and sewing, rare; the printing and decorating, genuinely common, including from brands that never make a fuss about it. If a brand's claiming full local manufacture from fibre to finished shirt, that's the specific claim worth checking — not "made in Australia" generally, which usually means something narrower once you ask which step it's describing.

It's worth asking the follow-up question too, because "made" often gets stretched to cover things that aren't manufacturing at all. Designed here isn't made here. Photographed here isn't made here. A head office in Australia says nothing about where the actual garment came from. None of those are dishonest claims on their own — they're just different claims to "made," and a brand that lets the two blur together is usually hoping you won't notice the gap.

Where we sit in that chain is simple to state honestly: designed here, on a blank sourced from a nearby but not local supplier, with local Australian printing the whole point rather than an afterthought.
