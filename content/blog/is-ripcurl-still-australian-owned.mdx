No. Rip Curl was founded in Torquay, Victoria in 1969 and stayed Australian-owned for exactly fifty years — then sold to Kathmandu in October 2019, in a deal reported at the time at around $350 million. Kathmandu's parent company, KMD Brands, is the actual owner now, and KMD is a New Zealand-headquartered outdoor and lifestyle group, not an Australian one.

What didn't change: the headquarters. Rip Curl's still run out of Torquay, still trading under its own name, still doing its own thing rather than getting folded into Kathmandu's identity. Ownership moved; the operation on the ground mostly didn't, which is exactly why this question keeps coming up — nothing about walking into a Rip Curl store or looking at a Rip Curl tee gives away that the company behind it changed hands six years ago.

It's a genuinely common pattern in Australian surf and streetwear, not a one-off. Billabong went through its own ownership churn well before this. A brand founded here, built here, with a story that's entirely real, can still end up owned from somewhere else without the storefront ever announcing it. "Australian-owned" and "Australian in origin, attitude, and heritage" are two different claims, and it's worth knowing which one a brand's actually making before you assume they're the same thing.

Worth flagging too: acquisition doesn't automatically mean anything gets worse. A larger parent company can bring real resources — better supply chains, more retail reach, money behind product development — that a genuinely independent brand of the same size couldn't access on its own. The trade is real in both directions, not just a loss.

None of this is a knock on Rip Curl specifically — fifty years independent is a genuinely long run, longer than most brands manage before an acquisition offer becomes hard to refuse. It's just a useful, concrete example of a question worth asking about any brand you're backing for the "Australian" part of its story specifically: who actually owns it today, not just who founded it in 1969.

If where a brand's actually from matters to you, it's worth asking about the ones you're already wearing — including whatever's currently in your own drawer, like the [Mentally On Annual Leave Tee](/products/mentally-on-annual-leave-tee) or the [Offline Since Birth Tee](/products/offline-since-birth-tee). Ownership on its own tells you almost nothing about quality either way — it just tells you who's actually answerable for the decisions being made, which is a different and arguably more useful thing to know.
