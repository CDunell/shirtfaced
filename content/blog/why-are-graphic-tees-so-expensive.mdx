Ask why a plain cotton tee costs $12 and a graphic tee costs $45, and you'll get a lot of vague gestures at "branding." Here's what's actually in the gap, because we went and got real numbers rather than repeat the vague gesture.

Start with the blank. A proper mid-weight cotton tee — the kind that doesn't go see-through in a photo — runs somewhere around $16–19 wholesale before anyone's touched it with ink. That's before print, before staff, before the studio, before the courier.

Then print. This is where the real spread happens, and it's not evenly distributed. Local Australian screen printing at a real, small-batch volume (50–100 units, one colour) lands somewhere in the $13–20 per unit range once setup fees are spread across the run — a $50–60 per-colour screen charge is real money on a 25-unit batch and basically nothing on a 500-unit one. Print-on-demand skips the batch entirely — no minimum, print one, ship it — but that convenience costs roughly $20–28 landed per unit once a real print gets added to a real blank, and if the print-on-demand provider isn't actually printing in Australia (most of the big global names aren't), tack on international shipping and a week or two of transit on top.

So the honest range for one genuinely good, single-colour graphic tee, printed properly, before margin: roughly $25–40 all-in, depending entirely on whether it was screen printed in bulk or printed on demand one at a time. A $45–55 retail tee isn't padding that out with brand tax — it's covering the blank, the print, and enough margin that the business is still standing come the next drop.

The $8 graphic tee at the bottom of a mixed rack is answering a different question. Somebody in that chain — the blank, the print, or the person who made it — got paid less than the numbers above, and it wasn't the brand eating the difference.

None of this is a defence of any particular price tag, ours included. It's just what's actually in a t-shirt before the joke gets printed on it. The other place the price goes is the stuff that's easy to forget exists at all: the courier, the packaging, the returns nobody enjoys processing, and the actual humans doing the printing rather than a machine nobody's paying properly. None of that shows up on the tag either, and all of it is real money before a single shirt reaches a doorstep.
