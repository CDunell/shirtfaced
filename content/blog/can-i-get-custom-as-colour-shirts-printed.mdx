Yes, and it's more straightforward in Australia than most of the guides floating around online make it sound — a lot of them are written for a US audience working with a completely different supply chain.

The short version: AS Colour blanks are genuinely easy to get printed here because plenty of local screen printers already stock and work with them as a default option, not a special request. You're not sourcing an obscure import and hoping someone knows what to do with it — a small AU print shop quoting a run of tees has almost certainly handled this exact blank before.

Two real paths, and they suit different situations. Local screen printing needs a minimum run — real quotes for a small batch sit somewhere in the 25–100 unit range depending on the printer, with the price per shirt dropping hard as the run gets bigger and a flat per-colour setup fee gets spread across more units. It's the better call if you're printing the same design more than once, because the setup cost is a one-off, not a per-shirt tax.

Print-on-demand is the other path — no minimum, print one shirt, ship it — and a genuine handful of Australian-based POD operators exist and will run a design onto an AS Colour blank without you touching a screen or a minimum order at all. It costs more per unit and the design typically prints via DTG rather than screen printing, which is a real, visible quality difference on a large graphic — less opaque, less durable through repeat washes.

Neither path requires you to buy blanks yourself and hand them over — the shops that do this work either source the blank as part of the job or already stock it.

Worth knowing before you get a quote either way: a setup fee is normal, not a sign you're being overcharged. Screen printing needs a physical screen made per colour, and that cost exists whether you order 25 shirts or 250 — it's just brutal on a small order and close to nothing on a large one. If a quote looks expensive per unit, check whether it's actually the setup fee doing that, divided across too few shirts, before assuming the printer's margin is the problem.

What actually decides which path makes sense is volume and how graphic the print is. One design running once, testing an idea, no interest in holding stock: POD. A design you're printing more than once, or a large solid-colour graphic where screen printing's opacity and durability actually matter: local screen print, minimum order and all.
