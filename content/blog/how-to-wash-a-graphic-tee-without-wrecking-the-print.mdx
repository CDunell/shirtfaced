Most people wreck a graphic tee's print inside the first month, and it's almost never the fabric's fault — it's the wash. The blank underneath is usually tougher than the ink sitting on top of it, so the print is the part actually at risk every time the shirt goes through a cycle, not the shirt itself.

**Cold water, every time.** Heat is the single biggest thing that ages a print fast — it softens the ink binder, which is what lets cracking and fading start early. A hot wash won't destroy a print in one go, but it's doing measurable damage every single time, and that adds up faster than most people expect.

**Turn it inside out before it goes anywhere near the machine.** This is the easiest, most-skipped step, and it matters more than almost anything else on this list. Inside out, the print never touches the drum, other garments, zips or buttons directly — it's the fabric taking the friction instead of the ink.

**Wash with similar colours, not just "the darks."** A graphic tee with any real contrast in it is effectively both a light garment and a dark one at once. Bleeding from something else in the load can dull a print's whites just as easily as it can stain a plain white shirt.

**Skip the dryer if you can manage it.** A dryer won't kill a print outright, but it's concentrated heat for an extended period, which is exactly the thing point one already flagged as the enemy. Hang dry costs you nothing but a bit of time and noticeably extends how long a print stays sharp.

**Never iron directly on the print, and don't dry-clean it either.** Ironing is direct, sustained heat and pressure on the one part of the shirt you're trying to protect — if you must iron, do it inside out, low heat, and keep the iron off the printed area entirely. Dry-cleaning solvents aren't formulated with screen-printed ink in mind and can do real, visible damage.

**If the fabric's garment-dyed, expect it to keep changing — that's not a fault.** Garment-dyed pieces keep fading and softening a little with every wash, on purpose. It's the finish doing its actual job, not the wash routine going wrong.

None of this is complicated, which is exactly why it's easy to skip. Cold, inside out, hang dry, don't iron the ink directly — four habits, and they're the difference between a print that still looks sharp a year in and one that's cracked and patchy by month three.
