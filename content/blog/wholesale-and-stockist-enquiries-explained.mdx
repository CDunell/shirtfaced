If you run a shop and want a brand's shirts on your own rack, the process looks nothing like placing a normal customer order — it starts with a document most people outside retail have never heard of: a line sheet.

A line sheet is essentially a wholesale-facing product catalogue, built specifically for a buyer's decision-making rather than a customer's browsing. Where the design details and product story go, a line sheet leads with wholesale price per unit, minimum order quantities, available sizing and colour breakdowns, and lead time — the specific numbers a shop actually needs to work out whether stocking something makes financial sense for their own business, not whether the design itself is appealing.

The wholesale price on that sheet sits meaningfully below retail, and that gap isn't arbitrary — it's the shop's own margin, the reason a retailer takes on the real risk of committing money upfront to stock that might not sell through. A brand selling wholesale is trading margin for reach: lower revenue per unit, in exchange for a shelf and a customer base it didn't have to build itself.

Minimum order quantities exist for a genuinely practical reason on the brand's side too, not just as an arbitrary gatekeeping number. Small-batch print runs already carry a real fixed setup cost per design — the same economics behind why a 25-unit screen-print order costs more per shirt than a 250-unit one. A wholesale order below a certain size can mean the brand is effectively losing money just to get product onto someone else's shelf, which is obviously not sustainable at any real scale.

The actual first step is almost always just an email, not a formal application — reaching out with genuine shop details (what you stock already, roughly what volume you're thinking, where the shop actually is) is normally enough to get a real line sheet sent back and a proper conversation started. It's a genuinely low-friction way in, even though the document that follows looks a lot more formal than a customer ever sees.

Whether it's the [Built Different Tee](/products/built-different-tee), the [Permanent Tee](/products/permanent-tee) or the rest of a range, the wholesale version of that same shirt is a completely different commercial conversation to the retail one — different price, different minimums, different question being answered. Worth knowing the shape of that conversation before you send the first email, so you're asking for the right thing from the start.
