Short of asking a lawyer, "is AS Colour good quality" is one of those questions where everyone's already got an opinion before they've read past the second search result. Here's the actual answer, no affiliate-link theatre.

First, the bit people get wrong: AS Colour isn't Australian. It's a New Zealand company — founded in Auckland in 2005 by Lawrence Railton — that happens to have a very Australian-sounding name and thirty-odd stores across both countries. We're not correcting this because it matters for tariffs. We're correcting it because if you're going to have an opinion about a t-shirt brand, you may as well have the right one.

On the actual quality question: fine, more than fine for the price bracket it sits in. The tee most of our range is built on — the 5026 Classic — is 220gsm combed cotton, which is heavier than the 150–180gsm stuff most "premium" tees are quietly made from. Structured fit, clean print surface, holds a screen-printed design properly instead of the ink sinking in and going patchy after two washes. It's not going to out-luxury a $90 Japanese-loomed tee. It was never trying to.

Customer sentiment backs this up, unglamorously: reviews sit around the "not rubbish, does the job, occasionally a size runs generous" range — which, if you've bought clothes on the internet before, is basically a five-star review with the enthusiasm surgically removed.

Where it earns its keep is print surface. A cheap blank looks fine on a hanger and falls apart the moment ink hits it — texture goes strange, the print cracks early, colours sit wrong. AS Colour's is predictable enough that what you see on screen roughly survives contact with a printer, which is most of what "good quality" means once the marketing's stripped out.

We didn't pick it because it's fashionable to name-drop. We picked it because the [Built Different Tee](/products/built-different-tee) has to survive being worn into the ground before it survives being funny — and a blank that falls apart in the wash undoes both jobs at once. Same logic applies whether it's the [Eight Ball Tee](/products/eight-ball-tee) or anything else in the range: the print's only as good as what it's printed on.

One more thing worth knowing before you size yourself: everything here runs boxy and slightly oversized on purpose, not because the blank came up small. Size down if you want it fitted, size true if you want it huge — either way, that's a cut decision, not a quality one.

Short version: not the fanciest fabric in the world, genuinely solid for what it is, and definitely not Australian — no matter how convincingly it argues otherwise.
