Two people order the exact same design, printed the exact same way, from the exact same shop. One orders 25 shirts, the other orders 250. The per-shirt price they each pay can differ by nearly double, and it's not the printer padding a margin on the smaller order — it's one specific, fixed cost getting divided very differently.

Screen printing needs a physical screen made for every colour in the design before a single shirt gets printed. That screen costs roughly the same to produce whether the run behind it is 25 units or 2,500 — it's a flat, one-off setup cost, not something that scales with volume. Spread across 250 shirts, it barely registers per unit. Spread across 25, it can be a meaningful chunk of what you're actually paying for each one.

That's the entire mechanism behind why small-batch pricing looks steep on paper. It isn't the printer charging more for less — it's the same fixed cost, divided by a much smaller number. A print shop quoting $20 a shirt for 25 units and $13 a shirt for 100 units of the identical design isn't inconsistent or opportunistic. It's the same setup fee, doing the same job, just landing very differently depending on how many shirts it's spread across.

This is also the exact reason print-on-demand exists as a separate category rather than just being "small-batch screen printing, but smaller." POD sidesteps the setup-cost problem entirely by not screen printing at all — DTG has effectively no meaningful setup cost per order, so it can profitably print a single unit. The tradeoff, as always, is print quality and durability on the kind of bold, high-contrast design screen printing handles better.

Multiple colours multiply the problem rather than adding to it in a straight line — each additional colour needs its own separate screen, so a three-colour design at a small volume can cost noticeably more per unit than a single-colour design at the same volume, for a reason that has nothing to do with the ink itself and everything to do with how many screens had to be made.

None of this is a secret the industry hides — it's just rarely explained plainly, which makes a legitimate small-batch quote look like a rip-off to anyone comparing it to a mass-market unit price without knowing why the two numbers exist in completely different contexts. Whatever ends up printed — something in the spirit of the [Roll The Dice Tee](/products/roll-the-dice-tee) or the [Handle With Care Tee](/products/handle-with-care-tee) — that fixed setup cost is exactly why ordering more of the same design, all at once, is nearly always the better economic call than ordering small and topping up later.
