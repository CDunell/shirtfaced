It sits in the same corner of Australian slang as shitfaced, munted, written off, off your chops, maggoted, cooked, legless, blind — a verb and a state, not a name for a kind of person. "I got shirtfaced." "We all got shirtfaced together." Nobody says "I am a shirtfaced" the way they might claim a tribe or an identity, and that's the whole point of it landing as a verb rather than a noun.

That distinction matters more than it sounds like it should. A noun is an identity claim — say you belong to a tribe and you've got something to defend, a reputation to maintain, a version of yourself to live up to in public. A verb is just what happened on a specific night. Lower stakes, lower social risk, and a much wider door for anyone to walk through without first deciding they're "that kind of person."

Here's the actual reframe the word's doing, and it's not what most people assume on first hearing it: shirtfaced means that level of good time, not that many drinks. The sober mate, the designated driver, the pregnant mate — every one of them is still fully in the photo, still part of the same shirtfaced night, because the word was never really measuring blood alcohol in the first place. It's measuring whether the night was actually that good, and being sober doesn't disqualify anyone from that.

That widening does real, practical work. It stops the whole idea collapsing down to "drunk" — which would be a much smaller, much more excludable thing to sell to anyone — and it means the shirt reads as a story about a genuinely good night, worn again on an ordinary Wednesday, rather than a confession about a specific bender that only makes sense twice a year. A garment that only means "I was hammered" gets worn rarely. One that means "we had the best night" gets worn constantly, which is the entire economic argument the word is quietly making on the brand's behalf.

None of this works if the imagery drags it back the other way — schooners in hand, someone visibly falling over, is the version of the joke that collapses straight back down to just "drunk." The version that actually holds the wider meaning is the group shot at the very end of the night: bad chips at 2am, shoes already off, everyone laughing at something that already happened rather than at the drinking that may or may not have caused it.
